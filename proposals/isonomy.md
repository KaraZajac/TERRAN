# Terran isonomy: the common lot and the qualified lot

*Proposal for the next draft of Articles 2, 3, 4, 7, 8 and 9 and Schedules 1, 2 and 6 · drafted by Claude from the third research round, 12 October 2026, at Kara's request, and revised after review · not yet adopted · clause numbers follow the third working draft*

**The proposal:** call the Charter's form of government an **isonomy**, and draw two kinds of sample instead of one.

- **The common lot** draws from all terrans. It decides: the deciding panels, the Agenda Council, the Oversight Council, the emergency panels, the panels that set each field's entry criteria, and the majority of every assembly, whose votes alone carry it.
- **The qualified lot** draws from terrans who have qualified in a field. Anyone can qualify, including by an open assessment that needs no prior credential, and every qualification lapses unless it's renewed. The qualified lot does craft work: a third of every assembly, the science behind the Guardians' statements, the independent offices, and the Guardians' scientific half.

The qualified lot never has the last word. No body drawn by it decides a planetary act, no field writes the rules for entering its own roster, and the votes of all terrans and the Council of Communities' key stay as they are.

**What the evidence says, in short.** Three new research notes back this:

- **The name has a record to live up to.** Isonomia, "equal law", is first attested around 500 BCE. In Herodotus, Otanes defines it by three marks: offices filled by lot, power held to account, and deliberation in public. The draft already has parts of all three ([isonomy](../research/isonomy.md)).
- **A lot among the qualified works when the gate is public**, at least for choosing committees and awarding small grants, in pools of about 100 to 1,100. The IETF has drawn its nominating committee by lot since 1993, and at least five research funders have drawn lots among proposals that passed review. Peer judgement can screen out weak candidates, and the evidence shows it cannot reliably rank strong ones ([qualified lot](../research/qualified-lot.md)).
- **Whoever writes the list holds the power.** Florence drew its officials by lot from purses of approved names, and the Medici ruled by controlling who went into the purses. Boards run by a profession's own members get no protection from antitrust law without active state supervision, the US Supreme Court held in 2015.
- **Experts lead mixed bodies, even where lay members are the majority.** Germany's lower criminal courts seat two lay judges beside one professional, yet in one study lay judges changed about 1% of verdicts. Swedish lay judges, three to a bench with one judge, outvote the judge in 1–3% of cases. Design changes how much lay members count: who decides, how votes are taken, and who chooses the experts ([mixed panels](../research/mixed-panels.md)).

## Why change the draft

The third draft relies on knowledge in three places, and each has a gap:

- **Assemblies drawn from all terrans draft the law (3.3)**, "with experts and Guardians", but the draft doesn't say who picks the experts or who holds the pen. In France's climate convention, experts chosen by the organisers' committee reworked proposals between sessions. Members named external experts (92%) and the technical group (74%) as their main sources of opinion, though some measures departed from what most experts suggested ([mixed panels](../research/mixed-panels.md)).
- **Office-holders come from certified pools (4.4, 4.5, 7.11).** That is already a qualified lot, but the draft doesn't say how anyone qualifies, for how long, or who writes the criteria.
- **Half the Guardians are nominated by "scientific bodies" (8.3)**, and in a world without nations nothing says which (strain 2 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md)). Their impact statements are "prepared by independent scientists" (8.4), chosen by no stated method.

One mechanism answers all three, and a name ties the system together.

## The name

**Isonomy** means "equal law". The physician Alcmaeon is reported to have called health the isonomia of the body's powers, and disease the monarchy of one, around 500 BCE. Athenians sang of the tyrant-slayers who "made Athens isonomous" ([isonomy](../research/isonomy.md)).

**Its first definition is close to the draft's design.** In Herodotus, the Persian nobles who have just overthrown a usurper debate how to govern. Otanes says rule by the many has "the loveliest name of all", isonomia: "It determines offices by lot, and holds power accountable, and conducts all deliberating publicly." Megabyzus answers that the many lack knowledge, so power should go to "the best". The qualified lot answers Megabyzus without giving up Otanes' lot. (Darius argued for a king, and won.)

**Why it fits:**

- **The draft already has Otanes' marks in part.** Lot (2.3, 3.4, 4.3), account (5.2, 7.11), and deliberation in public (2.5, 3.2, 3.5). The gaps are below.
- **It reverses Athens' great failing.** Athens counted only adult male citizens by descent: on one estimate, at most about 16% of the people of Attica. The Charter counts every person from birth (Part I.1).
- **Athens also kept skill out of the lottery.** It elected its generals and some treasurers, and drew most other offices by lot. The qualified lot brings in skill without an election.
- **No one else owns it.** "Demarchy" carries three meanings that contradict each other: Hayek's elected, limited government; Burnheim's government by lot; and the implants in Alastair Reynolds' novels that poll everyone constantly. "Lottocracy" and "open democracy" name other people's designs. Isonomy names none.

**What to be careful about:**

- **A banner, not a blueprint.** Oligarchs claimed isonomia too, and tyrants proclaimed it as a tactic. Maeandrius of Samos proclaimed it; when a citizen asked him to account for the money, he lured citizens in one by one, as if to give it, and jailed them. So the Charter should define isonomy by its institutions, in one line.
- **The English word means equality before the law.** No reader will hear "lot" in it, so the definition has to say so. The gloss "equal law, and an equal chance to serve" keeps both senses. "Equal share", where this idea started, is a minority reading of the Greek.
- **A chance, not a turn.** Athens could promise most citizens a year on its Council. On one round-two estimate, about one terran in 1,950 would serve on a planetary body in a lifetime. Terran isonomy offers an equal chance; turns happen in communities.
- **Not quite "no-rule".** Hannah Arendt read isonomy as "no-rule", with no division between rulers and ruled. The draft keeps courts, enforcement and an executive, so it fits Aristotle's "to govern and be governed in turn" better.
- **No sure Athenian precedent for lawmaking by lot.** Scholars still dispute whether the 4th-century lawmakers, the *nomothetai*, were sworn jurors or a session of the Assembly.

### Three checks the name asks for

Otanes' marks expose three gaps. Athens had all three ([isonomy](../research/isonomy.md)).

- **An account at the end of office.** Every retiring Athenian official rendered account to auditors, and theft or bribery cost ten times the sum. The draft has no end-of-office account.
- **A hearing before office.** Every Athenian official faced questions before taking office, ending with: "Does anybody wish to bring a charge against this man?" Certification (4.4) has no such moment.
- **Deliberation in the open.** The assemblies' own proceedings (3.3) need not be public. Ireland's 2012–14 convention held its plenary sessions in public and its small-group tables in private ([mixed panels](../research/mixed-panels.md)).

### Getting on a roster

Anyone can qualify, and no one stays qualified by default. Each rule answers a failure the research found ([qualified lot](../research/qualified-lot.md)).

- **Several free routes, one of them open to all.** Each field accepts at least two routes, and one is always an open assessment that needs no prior credential. Others can be a credential, a record of work, or, where a field shapes people's lives directly, lived experience. Every route runs in every major language, with a form that needs no device (2.2). Identity checks never need a member nearby; Debian's rule that two members meet each applicant in person shows how that shuts people out.
- **Lapse, with a way back.** A roster place lapses after five years without qualifying work, and a short route restores it. A lapse during a term doesn't end the term. The IETF's eligibility runs on rolling windows of up to five years, Wikipedia removes inactive administrators, and England's Care Quality Commission asks for lived experience within eight years. Wikipedia also warns against "token edits" made only to keep the status.
- **Outsiders write the bar.** A panel drawn by the common lot proposes each field's criteria for adoption by planetary act, published in advance. It chooses its own advisers, at least two on each contested point. No body of practitioners sets its own field's criteria. North Carolina's dental board, six of whose eight members were practising dentists, sent at least 47 cease-and-desist letters to cheaper teeth whiteners. A 2015 US government report found board members from a profession "more likely than public members to favor the interests of their profession".
- **Checkable without exposing anyone.** Each roster is published yearly as counts by region, gender, language and affiliation, and as a pseudonymous list from which anyone can reproduce each draw, with a week for challenges. Only those seated are named, and the route by which they qualified stays private, since lived experience can reveal a health condition or a migration. The IETF's published draws caught errors in 2022 and 2024. The Statistics Office flags sudden surges of new entrants, which the IETF's eligibility rules call "an early-warning sign" of capture.
- **Caps on any one employer.** No more than two members of a body drawn by the qualified lot, or one in a body of ten or fewer, may share an employer, institution or funder, counting subsidiaries and contractors. Members with no affiliation name their funders. Caps limit firms, not industries: by the research note's count, seven firms supplied 58% of the IETF's 2026 volunteers.
- **Interests declared, offices kept apart.** Members declare their interests, step aside where they or their employers would gain, and never sit on an act that sets their own field's criteria. No one may be drawn by the qualified lot while holding an office or working in the civil service, and no one sits on two qualified bodies at once. Without that rule, one captured roster could fill the Earth Science Panel, the ceiling assembly's third, the Guardians' half and their reviewers together. In Ireland's mixed convention, the one issue touching politicians' own seats moved toward their position.
- **Paid without loss.** Qualified service is paid like all service by lot (4.3), and the pay may not cost anyone their benefits, a risk one network funded by England's National Institute for Health and Care Research warns of.

### Mixed assemblies

Each standing assembly would draw two-thirds of its members by the common lot and a third by the qualified lot, from those qualified in its field. Everyone deliberates and drafts, but only the common-lot members' votes decide. The qualified members' count is published, after the deciding panel has voted.

The evidence for those choices ([mixed panels](../research/mixed-panels.md)):

- **A lay majority alone isn't enough.** German lay judges "need to be in a clear majority to have maximum influence". Yet in Germany, where lower criminal courts are two-thirds lay, one study found lay judges changed about 1% of verdicts. In Córdoba, lay members outvoted judges who voted together twice in 360 decisions. No study tests an even split, or lay members and experts drafting law together. In the one related case found, patients in English clinical-guideline groups saw their contributions "not subsequently acted on".
- **One shared majority would hand power to a united third.** With all 450 voting together, the qualified 150 plus 76 common-lot members could pass a draft that the other 224 opposed, or block one most of the common lot wanted.
- **An expert verdict sways the people deciding.** In Korean shadow juries, every juror facing a judge who steered toward a conclusion changed position. So the qualified count reaches the public only after the deciding panel has voted.

Four more rules keep the common lot in charge:

1. **The common lot chooses the outside experts heard,** at least two on each contested point, and experts give evidence rather than conclusions. Danish consensus panels help choose their experts. Ireland's 2016 assembly had speakers ratified by a steering group its members elected.
2. **Common-lot members chair the working groups,** and every draft is published with its authors. On the research's reading, who chooses the experts and who holds the pen matter more than whether experts are in the room.
3. **Equal information.** Every member gets the full file and time to read it. German lay judges who had read the file won their dissents more often.
4. **Open by default.** Hearings and plenary debates are public and recorded; small groups meet in private.

Deciding panels stay wholly common lot. They are the clearest lay check in the design.

### Guardians and their statements

- **The scientific half.** Half the Guardians would be drawn by the qualified lot from those qualified in the sciences of the Earth and the long term, stratified by field and region, and capped by employer. That replaces nomination by scientific bodies, which no one could name without nations. Each finding publishes both halves' counts.
- **Impact statements.** Scientists drawn by the qualified lot would prepare the long-term impact statements (8.4), open to review by anyone on the roster, with every comment and response published. The IPCC already lets first-stage expert reviewers nominate themselves, and publishes every comment and reply. Drawing the authors by lot is untested.
- **Unresolved.** The Guardians' ten-year term sits outside Schedule 1's own advice for Guardians drawn by lot, two to three years. This proposal doesn't change it.

### Starting advisory

Article 9.7 starts each assembly in advisory form, but exempts those for the ceiling, weapons of mass destruction, and identity and voting. Under this proposal, qualified seats in every body, the exempted ones included, would sit without a vote until the Audit Office finds that their roster meets criteria published in advance. The Ombudsman council would check the Audit Office's own roster, since no body may oversee the draws for its own seats (4.3).

## What it costs

- **Rosters at planetary scale.** The Commonwealth would run free, multilingual qualification with routes that need no device, plus identity checks, yearly publication and challenge periods. No source costs this. The largest tested pools are the IETF's 100 to 270 volunteers a year and grant rounds of about 1,100 applications.
- **Screening grows with the pool.** Pools grew once lotteries began, British Academy applications by about 70%. In 2013 New Zealand's Health Research Council judged 99 of 116 applications ineligible.
- **Seats.** Six or seven assemblies of 450, a third qualified, need about 900 to 1,050 qualified members at a time (own arithmetic), plus the Guardians' half and the scientists who prepare statements.

## What could go wrong

| Risk | Evidence | Safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capture of the gate | Florence's scrutinies; practitioner boards | Common-lot panels write the criteria and choose their advisers; refusals appealable to the Court |
| Flooding and fake identities | The IETF's rules call capture "the most potent threat"; online attendance cut the cost of qualifying to "zero financial cost" | Checkable routes; one register entry per terran (4.3); surge warnings; employer caps |
| One roster filling several bodies | One field feeds the Earth Science Panel, an assembly's third, the Guardians and their statements | No office-holders drawn; one qualified body at a time; the Ombudsman council checks the Audit Office's roster |
| Skewed rosters | IETF participants: about 40% North American and 40% European; women 9.82% of regular participants in 2025 | Published counts; stratified draws; free assessment; lived-experience routes |
| Industries, not firms | Seven firms supplied 58% of the IETF's 2026 volunteers | Published concentration; stratify by sector where one dominates |
| Exposure | Lived-experience routes can reveal health or migration | Pseudonymous lists; only those seated named; routes kept private |
| Experts leading the room | Lay judges; Brussels MPs whose recommendations "may be more the MPs' than citizens'" | Only common-lot votes decide; counts released after the panel votes; common-lot control of experts |
| Epistocracy by degrees | Estlund's demographic objection: any test of expertise tends to select a narrow group | Only the common lot decides; voting never needs a qualification |
| Token activity | Wikipedia's warning against "token edits" | Activity that can be checked; a short way back |
| Legitimacy | Swiss voters rejected judges by lot from a qualified pool; at the FWF about half of awardees linked its lottery to low quality | Start advisory; judges' selection unchanged |

## What's proven and what's a bet

Grades follow the [government design](modern-terran-government.md): **A**, binding at national scale or larger for 10+ years with outcome data; **B**, real use with measured results, but advisory, local or short-lived; **C**, pilots or an untested combination of proven parts; **D**, theory only, or a record of failure.

| Element | Grade | Closest precedent | Biggest risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A lot among volunteers who pass a public test | B | The IETF's nominating committee, since 1993 | Flooding; employer skew |
| A lot among proposals that pass review | B | New Zealand's HRC since 2013, the SNSF (to break ties), Volkswagen (2017–21), the FWF, the British Academy | Calls to fund stand-outs on merit |
| Eligibility lists kept by insiders | D | Florence under the Medici; Venice's closed council | Capture of the gate |
| Lay majorities in mixed bodies | A as courts; weak as a check | German, Danish and Japanese mixed courts | Professionals lead anyway: about 1% of German verdicts changed |
| Citizens and insiders drafting together | C | Ireland's 2012–14 convention and the Brussels committees, both with politicians | Insider domination |
| Citizens and experts drafting together | D | English clinical-guideline groups | Lay contributions "not subsequently acted on" |
| Rosters at planetary scale | D | None; tested pools run to about 1,100 | Everything above, at once |
| Earned membership that lapses | B | Debian; Wikipedia's administrators; the IETF's windows | Token activity |
| Lived experience as a route | B | England's Experts by Experience | Contractors cutting pay and coverage |
| Outsiders writing entry criteria | C | Public members on US licensing boards | "little reliable empirical evidence" it works |
| Guardians and statement authors drawn by lot | D | None; the IPCC opens review, not authorship | Legitimacy of the science |
| Judges by lot from a qualified pool | D | Switzerland's Justiz-Initiative, rejected in 2021 | Legitimacy |

## Draft wording

Candidate clauses in the Charter's voice. Bracketed numbers would become Schedule entries.

> **2.3 Isonomy: decided by samples, checked by all.** The Commonwealth is an isonomy: equal law, and an equal chance to serve. Its deciding panels are drawn by lot, every office answers for its work, and its deliberation is open, with stated exceptions. Panels drawn by lot from all terrans, the common lot, decide planetary questions, chosen fresh for each decision. Work that needs a field's knowledge goes to terrans drawn by lot from those qualified in it, the qualified lot, which never has the last word. Enough terrans together can send any planetary act except a ceiling act to a vote of all terrans, as Article 3 sets out. Only such votes change the Foundations and the Articles (Part V).

> **3.3 Assemblies chosen by lot.** For each planetary power listed in 2.4, a standing assembly of 450 terrans studies proposals and drafts planetary law. [Two-thirds] of its members are drawn by the common lot, and [a third] by the qualified lot from those qualified in its field. It decides by a majority of its common-lot members, voting by secret ballot, and publishes the qualified members' count once the deciding panel has voted. Its common-lot members chair its working groups and choose the experts it hears, at least two on each contested point, who give evidence rather than conclusions. Every member has the full file. Its hearings and plenary debates are public and recorded, its small groups meet in private, and every draft is published with its authors. A Budget Assembly, drawn and run the same way, drafts the planetary budget and reconciles acts that conflict. An Agenda Council of 300, drawn by the common lot, assigns proposals to assemblies; *(the rest unchanged)*.

> **4.3, a sentence added.** "Drawn by lot" alone means drawn by the common lot.

> **4.4, sentences added.** Each certifying panel's experts are drawn by the qualified lot from neighbouring fields that do not compete for the same work. Before certifying a candidate, the panel hears in public any objection brought within [30 days]. No one sits on two certifying panels.

> **4.6 Qualified pools (new).** Any terran may join a field's roster by at least [two] free routes, one of them an open assessment that needs no prior credential. Others may be a credential, a record of work, or, where a field shapes people's lives directly, lived experience. Every route is offered in every major language, with a form that needs no device. A roster place lapses after [five years] without qualifying work, and a short route restores it; a lapse during a term does not end the term. For each field, a panel drawn by the common lot proposes the criteria, published in advance, for adoption by planetary act; it chooses its own advisers, at least two on each contested point. No body of practitioners sets its own field's criteria. Schedule 6 lists the fields.

> **4.7 The qualified lot (new).** Each roster is published every year as counts by region, gender, language and affiliation, and as a pseudonymous list from which anyone can reproduce each draw, with [a week] for challenges. Only those seated are named, and the route by which they qualified stays private. No more than [two] members of a body drawn by the qualified lot, or [one] in a body of ten or fewer, may share an employer, institution or funder, counting subsidiaries and contractors. Members declare their interests and funders, step aside where they or their employers would gain, and never sit on an act that sets their own field's criteria. No one may be drawn by the qualified lot while holding an office or serving in the civil service, and no one sits on two bodies drawn by it at once. Assemblies that draft changes to this Charter, and the review assembly (Part V.8), are drawn by the common lot alone.

> **7.12 An account at the end of office (new).** Every Steward, Guardian, judge, ombudsman and member of an independent office gives a public account of their service when it ends, and the Audit Office audits what they spent. For [90 days], anyone may bring a complaint to the Ombudsman council, or, about an ombudsman, to the Planetary Court.

> **8.3 Guardians.** Guardians speak for future terrans and for the living world, because neither can vote. Half are drawn by the common lot, and half by the qualified lot from those qualified in the sciences of the Earth and the long term. Each serves one ten-year term, and successors are chosen automatically so no seat sits empty. Each finding publishes both halves' counts.

> **8.4, second sentence.** Each carries the Guardians' long-term impact statement, prepared by scientists drawn by the qualified lot and open to review by anyone on their roster, with every comment and response published.

> **9.7, a sentence added.** Qualified seats in any body, including those excepted above, sit without a vote until the Audit Office finds that their roster meets criteria published in advance; the Ombudsman council checks the Audit Office's own roster.

## Choices for Kara

1. **Where should the name live?** In Article 2.3, as above; in Part I.4, which defines the Commonwealth; or in the Preamble, as a line of principle. Wherever it goes, it needs its one-line definition: equal law, and an equal chance to serve.
2. **What should the qualified third do in an assembly?** The proposal lets it deliberate, draft and vote, with only the common lot's votes deciding. Alternatives:
    - **A double majority:** each pool must agree, as in Denmark's mixed courts. That would give the experts a veto.
    - **One shared majority:** everyone's vote counts. A united third could then carry or block drafts with a minority of the common lot.
    - **No experts as members:** a qualified drafting team writes on the assembly's instructions and adopts nothing.
    - **Half and half,** the first version of this idea, which no study has tested.
3. **When should the qualified count be published?** The proposal releases it after the deciding panel votes, since an expert verdict sways lay deciders. Before would inform the panel, at that cost.
4. **Which fields take lived experience?** Health and care are the tested cases. Farming, disability and migration are candidates.
5. **How long before a qualification lapses?** No source tests the length. Five years matches the IETF's longest window, and the Care Quality Commission allows eight; the proposal makes it a setting.
6. **How should the Guardians' halves decide?** The proposal keeps one majority and publishes both counts. The alternatives: a majority of either half could pull the one-year brake, or both halves could be required.
7. **Judges stay as they are.** The proposal leaves 7.2 alone, since Swiss voters rejected judges by lot from a qualified pool. Say if you want that revisited.
8. **Which of Athens' checks?** The proposal adds all three: the account at the end of office, the hearing before certification, and open proceedings. Athens also voted on its generals in every presidency and capped Council service at two terms; either could follow.
