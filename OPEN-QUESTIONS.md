## Fixes and credits

These correct the text against itself, its sources, or the precedents it credits. They change wording, not policy.

- [x] **VIII.5 contradicts itself.** *Fixed in the second draft.* "A later vote replaces an earlier one" lets an online vote cast after an in-person vote replace it, against the very next rule. Estonia's rule is simply that paper wins. Suggested wording: *"An in-person vote is final and replaces any online vote, whenever cast. Among online votes, the last counts. Online voting closes before in-person voting does."* "Can always be changed" should become "can be changed until voting closes". ([digital voting](research/digital-voting.md))
- [x] **I.2 inverts the scholarship.** *Fixed in the second draft.* More communication built more nations, not fewer: UN membership rose from 51 states in 1945 to 193 in 2011, and scholars tie nations to print and mass literacy. Suggested wording: *"Nations grew with the printing press and mass literacy, which taught strangers who would never meet to see themselves as one people. Today's networks reach every terran, so that bond can now span the planet."* "Has made us one people" should become "can make us one people". ([commonwealth](research/commonwealth.md))
- [x] **V.4's start-up claim.** *Fixed in the second draft.* Finance is one barrier to cooperatives among several, alongside the cost of collective decisions, unsellable membership stakes and weak law. Reword "a main reason". ([business](research/business.md))
- [x] **The ceiling's names.** *Fixed in the second draft.*
    - "Freshwater use" was retired in 2023, and the verdict flips under its replacement: inside the old limit, outside the new one since 1905.
    - "Biodiversity loss", "air pollution" and "chemical pollution" narrow or shift what is actually measured.
    - Fix: use the official process names, with plain glosses. ([floor and ceiling](research/floor-and-ceiling.md))
- [x] **The Preamble's "toward collapse"** *Fixed in the second draft.* outruns the sources, which speak of a rising risk of irreversible change. ([floor and ceiling](research/floor-and-ceiling.md))
- [x] **Credits.** *Fixed in the second draft.*
    - Credit Sweden's Ombudsman (1809), the original of the office. ([deliberation](research/deliberation.md))
    - Part X credits Ecuador's rights of nature for the Guardians, but Ecuador has no guardian office. What X.6 takes from Ecuador is the reversed burden of proof in its Article 397, which goes uncredited. Credit Article 397 and the Wingspread Statement there. ([long view](research/long-view.md))
    - Robeyns: "adapts" fits better than "draws on". She pairs her €10M limit with a €1M ethical limit and a €200,000 inheritance limit. ([wealth and tax](research/wealth-and-tax.md))
    - The draft departs from the Earth Constitution it credits, and could say so. There, the Presidium is elected by the parliament, not by everyone; the Ombudsmus is a council of five, not one person; and prosecutors issue arrest warrants, where IX.6 requires a court. ([deliberation](research/deliberation.md), [accountability](research/accountability.md))
    - "Nordic" prisons aren't one model: two-year reconviction runs from 14% to 40% across the five countries. Credit Norway. ([accountability](research/accountability.md))

## The draft's five questions

### 1. The numbers

> Are the proposed figures right: a $10 million wealth limit, the first $2 million free of wealth tax, and pay limits at 50 and 100 times the lowest pay?

*In part: the third draft keeps these figures as defaults, and moves every number into the Schedules with a reason and a range ([Schedule 1](schedules/1-safeguards.md), [Schedule 2](schedules/2-settings.md)). Whether the figures are right is still open.*

**What the research says**

- **The limit's source.** $10M matches Robeyns' "roughly ten million per person". She frames it for the Netherlands and pairs it with a €1M ethical limit and a €200,000 inheritance limit. ([wealth and tax](research/wealth-and-tax.md))
- **A rough estimate of reach** (orders of magnitude, from a fit across UBS, Knight Frank and Forbes figures):
    - About 2.9 million adults (2.6–3.2 million) hold more than $10M.
    - About $79 trillion ($75–105 trillion) sits above the line, 15–20% of household wealth.
    - About 23 million adults hold more than $2M. ([wealth and tax](research/wealth-and-tax.md))
- **The clauses around the figures matter more than the figures.**
    - IV.5 fixes only the start and says the rate rises; the slope is left to a vote. It could stay near 1% until everything above $10M passes to the Commons Fund.
    - IV.6's income-based cap is the kind that let France's richest 0.001% pay about 0.1% of their wealth a year. As written, it would override any steep schedule above about $4M.
    - Whether "earn" means realized or accrued, nominal or real, decides how much is ever paid. ([wealth and tax](research/wealth-and-tax.md), [money](research/money.md))
- **A dollar limit bites unevenly.** At market exchange rates, $10M buys about five times more in India than in Switzerland. ([money](research/money.md))
- **50x and 100x sit where pay-ratio taxes start, but those taxes divide by median pay.** Portland and San Francisco start at 100:1, and US bill S. 2818 at 50:1. V.3 divides by the lowest pay, so it bites harder. Binding caps run from 6:1 to 35:1.
- **The scale of today's pay.** At the US federal minimum wage ($15,080 a year), 100x is $1.51M. The average S&P 500 CEO received $22.8M in 2025, excluding Elon Musk's Tesla package; with it, the average pay ratio was 5,387:1. ([business](research/business.md))
- **No steeply rising ratio tax has been tried.** Only Israel's binding 35:1 cap, with lost tax deductions, has been shown to change pay. ([checks](research/drafting-session-checks.md))

**Options**

- Write the schedule into the Charter, and average wealth over three to five years, as V.3 already does for pay.
- Replace IV.6's cap with deferral and instalments, or with a cap against a presumed yield. If it stays, add a minimum payment, as Spain's 20% floor does.
- Add rules on inheritance and gifts, and define "relatives".
- State amounts in a unit other than market-rate US dollars (see question 4).
- On pay:
    - Decide who pays: a surtax on the organization, or a personal rate.
    - Define pay as salary, bonus, stock at vesting, and working owners' dividends.
    - Add an absolute ceiling beside the ratio, so high-wage firms can't escape it.

**Sub-questions**

- [ ] What does "earn" mean in IV.6, and is there a minimum payment?
- [ ] What schedule runs from 1% at $2M to the limit?
- [ ] What are the rules on inheritance and gifts, and who counts as a "relative"?
- [ ] Who pays the pay-ratio tax, and how are dividends and unvested stock counted?
- [ ] Does the 100% rate above 100x clash with IV.6's cap on combined taxes?

### 2. Paper votes

> Should Charter amendments stay on paper until online voting can be fully secured, or should every vote be digital?

*Second draft: votes that change the Charter are cast on paper too, all votes of all terrans fall on one yearly day, and keys are held three-of-five (VIII.5, VIII.6, VIII.11). Whether every vote of all terrans should be on paper is still open.*

**What the research says**

- **The leading independent reviews conclude remote online voting can't yet be secured.**
    - The US National Academies (2018)
    - Park, Specter, Narula and Rivest (2021)
    - 21 computer scientists in January 2026
    - No system yet delivers a vote that is secret, verifiable, failure-tolerant and changeable all at once online. Estonia's Academy of Sciences found no high or critical risks in its mobile-voting plans, but six medium ones. ([digital voting](research/digital-voting.md), [checks](research/drafting-session-checks.md))
- **Key handling has already failed in practice.**
    - The IACR voided and re-ran its November 2025 election after a trustee lost one of three required key shares.
    - Basel-Stadt couldn't count 2,048 e-votes in March 2026 after irregular handling of the PIN-protected USB sticks holding the decryption key. Outcomes were unaffected. ([digital voting](research/digital-voting.md))
- **Online voting continues anyway.** About 222,000 Swiss voters (3.95%) are expected to be able to vote online on 29 November 2026. Estonia's online share peaked at 51.1% in 2023 and was 45.6% in 2025. ([digital voting](research/digital-voting.md))

**Options** (detailed in [digital voting](research/digital-voting.md))

- **A. Paper governs.** Charter votes are on hand-marked paper, checked by risk-limiting audits.
- **B. Dual channel.** Online voting is allowed, but the paper count is official for Charter votes.
- **C. Conditional switch.** Paper stays until named tests pass: public intrusion tests, threshold keys, verifiable revoting and a failure-free cycle. The switch itself needs a two-thirds paper vote.
- **D. All-digital.** End-to-end verifiable online voting throughout. Cheapest, but against the evidence.
- **E. Risk-scaled.** Paper for amendments and planetary votes; verifiable online voting for community votes.

Whatever the choice, fix VIII.5, and hold keys on a k-of-n threshold with recovery drills. With paper, a failed audit should trigger a hand count, not a new vote.

### 3. Transition

> Are half and nine in ten of humanity the right points for stages two and three?

**What the research says**

- **The thresholds copy the Earth Constitution's population triggers but drop its other safeguards.** There, stage two also needs half of nations, referendums need 25% turnout, rivals may join in pairs, and members pledge never to use force against each other. ([commonwealth](research/commonwealth.md))
- **"Half of humanity has joined" is undefined.** It could mean individual digital citizens, people in member nations, or both. Estonia's e-Residency, the credited model, has about 144,000 members. ([commonwealth](research/commonwealth.md))
- **Who could decide.** On mid-2026 figures, seven states (51.1%) could trigger stage two on their own. Five nuclear-armed states holding 9.5% of humanity could stay outside through stage three. ([commonwealth](research/commonwealth.md))
- **On armies, the Charter is more cautious than its precedent.** The Earth Constitution disarms member armies at its second stage (Art. 17.4.06), not only at the end. ([commonwealth](research/commonwealth.md))

**Options**

- Define "joined": residents of member nations and communities, plus digital citizens elsewhere, without double counting.
- Add a second key: half of humanity *and* half of nations (Earth Constitution 17.4.01), or an EU-style 55%/65% rule.
- Gate stage three on every nuclear-armed state joining and completing a verified handover.
- Gate every stage on independent verification and a waiting period, with a review if a stage stalls.
- Or keep 50% and 90% and say why.

### 4. Money

> With no nations, what money does the Commonwealth use in place of US dollars?

**What the research says**

- **The question hides two.** What unit measures the Charter's amounts? That is needed now. And what money do terrans use, and who issues it? That can come later. ([money](research/money.md))
- **"Adjusted each year for prices" names no index.** The obvious one, US CPI, published no October 2025 index for most series because of a government shutdown. ([money](research/money.md))
- **No global money plan since Keynes has gone beyond the IMF's limited SDR.** The euro shows money without fiscal and banking union strains under stress; it needed emergency backstops. ([money](research/money.md))
- **A model for amounts exists, with a catch.** The Montreal Convention writes its liability limits in SDRs and revises them for inflation. But the SDR is about 43% US dollar. ([money](research/money.md))
- **A central bank would combine powers VIII.8 keeps apart.** And the name "Terra" is taken, by a 2001 proposal and by the collapsed Terra/LUNA stablecoin. ([money](research/money.md))

**Options**

- **Smallest fix.** Name an index, with a published fallback when data are missing.
- **Amounts in a supranational unit.** SDRs revised Montreal-style, though the SDR is 43% dollar, or purchasing-power-parity (PPP) dollars. Or two yardsticks: the floor in PPP terms, the wealth limit in a market-rate basket.
- **Plural money, one yardstick.** Communities keep their money, and all Charter amounts are measured in one Commonwealth unit.
- **A clearing union at stage two**, settling balances between member currencies in that unit.
- **Book money before cash**, as the euro did from 1999 to 2001.
- **A money board that fits Part VIII.** Single long terms, and no lending to the Stewards or the Commons Fund.
- **A privacy floor for any digital money.**
- Avoid "terra" as a name.

### 5. Leaving after stage three

> Once nations are gone, may a community ever leave the Commonwealth, and on what terms?

*Second draft: XII.8 adds a way out if safeguards fail. The people keep their floor rights, the community keeps its ceiling duties, and the Court keeps its authority over past acts (option B below). Leaving by choice is still open.*

**What the research says**

- **The credited precedent already answers it.** Earth Constitution 14.1.4 lets individuals and groups "peacefully leave" once the Federation covers 90% of Earth's territory. They go to a disarmed zone of at most 5% of habitable land. ([commonwealth](research/commonwealth.md))
- **Exit clauses elsewhere are rare and hedged**: EU Article 50, the two-thirds vote St Kitts and Nevis requires, and Canada's clear-question rule. ([living charter](research/living-charter.md))
- **"Leaving" is unclear for a Commonwealth defined "not by territory"**, yet a community that leaves keeps its land, air and emissions. ([living charter](research/living-charter.md))
- **The stakes.** Bryan Caplan puts the chance of lasting global totalitarianism at 0.1% if the number of countries holds, and 25% with one world state. ([checks](research/drafting-session-checks.md))

**Options** (detailed in [living charter](research/living-charter.md))

- **A. No community exit.** Only individuals may leave. Simplest for the ceiling, but it leaves the no-exit risk unanswered.
- **B. Exit from governance, not from duties.** Floor rights stay with the people, ceiling duties follow through trade measures, and the Court keeps authority over past acts.
- **C. Hedged exit by choice.** Two local two-thirds votes a year apart, a clear-question test, a successor constitution published in advance, and a negotiated transition.
- **D. Remedial exit only**, after a Court finding of grave, persistent breach.
- **E. Mutual consent**: a local supermajority plus a planetary majority.

B is the only exit option that directly answers the ceiling problem. It pairs with C, D or E.
