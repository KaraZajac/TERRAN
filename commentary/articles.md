# Commentary on the Articles of Terra

*Not binding. Why the Articles say what they say, with the evidence behind them · drafted by Claude from the research notes, 12 October 2026 · where this reading and Kara's intent differ, hers governs*

The research notes and thoughts cite the second working draft's numbering. Article 1 was Parts IV and V; Article 2 was Part VII with VIII.8, VIII.10 and VIII.13; Articles 3 to 6 were the rest of Part VIII; Article 7 was Part IX with VIII.9; Article 8 was Part X; and Article 9 was Part XII. The [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md#third-working-draft--12-october-2026) maps each clause.

## Article 1 — The Culture of Enough

Article 1 caps private wealth, ties top pay to the lowest, and keeps organizations out of politics, so wealth can neither push others below the floor nor buy public decisions.

**Why it says this:** The wealth limit adapts Ingrid Robeyns' "political limit of roughly ten million per person". She frames it for the Netherlands, beside a €1 million ethical limit [uu/2023_what-is-limitarianism] and a €200,000 inheritance limit. A rough estimate puts about 2.9 million adults above $10 million, holding about $79 trillion above the line ([wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md)). Pay lines at 50 and 100 times sit where pay-ratio taxes start, but those divide by median pay, so 1.12 bites harder. Israel's 35:1 cap for financial firms cut executive pay 27% with stock returns no worse. Bans on corporate and union donations are mainstream, as in Canada and France ([business](../research/business.md)).

**Where it strains:** The fair-burden cap (1.6) is the kind that let France's richest 0.001% pay about 0.1% of their wealth a year; on an illustrative calculation, it would override a schedule reaching 100% at the limit above about $4 million. The slope from 1% to the limit is left to planetary act, so it could stay near 1%. Until stage three the rich can leave: Norway lost 82 wealthy residents in 2022–23 (strain 6 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md)). At market exchange rates, $10 million buys about five times more in India than in Switzerland (strain 12), and 1.11 also catches unions, NGOs and campaign groups (strain 13). See "The numbers" in [open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

**Read more:** [wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md), [business](../research/business.md).

## Article 2 — Working Together

Article 2 keeps planetary government small, open and answerable. Samples decide, all terrans can check them, a short list of matters is planetary, records sit on open logs, and scrutiny rises with power.

**Why it says this:** On the research's estimates, universal voting at a legislature's load would cost each adult 9–160 hours a year, and sampling about 3–6 seconds ([the government design](../proposals/modern-terran-government.md)). The short list (2.4) follows Switzerland and Nigeria and keeps only goods that fail without near-universal participation or restraint ([scale and federalism](../research/scale-and-federalism.md)). The Court must rule on objections within 90 days because the EU's court has never struck down a measure on subsidiarity grounds. Open logs follow Certificate Transparency, which gives tamper-evidence without tokens or mining [ietf/2013_rfc-6962]. About 800 million people lack any official ID, hence a non-digital route to every service and vote ([digital voting](../research/digital-voting.md)). The Council of Communities (2.10) gives member communities a key of their own, keeping rival centres of power, the first safeguard on the research's threat model ([failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md)).

**Where it strains:** The non-digital route answers part of strain 8 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md), but enrolling everyone once, from anywhere, without state registries or compulsory biometrics is unsolved. Transparency that scales with power has no threshold yet, and the EU Court of Justice struck down general public access to beneficial-ownership registers in 2022 (strain 9). The right to organize opposition (2.9) costs money that 1.11 bars organizations from spending (strain 13). A binding doughnut test invites gaming, and its data lag by years. Whether the digital identity is a right or a duty is open ([open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)).

**Read more:** [digital voting](../research/digital-voting.md), [modern governance](../research/modern-governance.md).

## Article 3 — How Terrans Decide

Article 3 replaces the universal vote with a division of labour. Anyone may propose, assemblies drawn by lot draft, a fresh panel of at least 1,000 decides each act, and enough terrans can call any act except a ceiling act to a vote of all terrans.

**Why it says this:** No legislature chosen by lot has held full lawmaking power ([sortition at scale](../research/sortition-at-scale.md)). So the design splits agenda, drafting and deciding across bodies, as Bouricius proposed [bouricius/2013_multi-body-sortition]. The vote on demand (3.5) follows Switzerland's optional referendum: most acts take effect without a vote, and 127 of 218 challenged acts survived ([scale and federalism](../research/scale-and-federalism.md)). Panels vote on the exact text because Ireland's government rewrote an assembly's care clause before its 2024 referendums, though no source shows this caused the defeats ([deliberation](../research/deliberation.md)). Ceiling acts (3.6) adapt the Montreal Protocol, with lower- and higher-footprint regions standing in for its two groups of parties. Code never overrides the text (3.8), since Australia's Robodebt automated an unlawful rule ([modern governance](../research/modern-governance.md)).

**Where it strains:** Binding decisions by samples have never been tried (strain 11 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md)). Only 2–5% of those invited usually accept, and in France's climate convention members helped draft only about a fifth of the measures they voted on. On the proposal's estimate, calling a vote of all terrans takes about 30 million signatures, and organized money could pick the targets. Whether the original proposal should sit on the ballot beside the assembly's version is open ([open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)).

**Read more:** [sortition at scale](../research/sortition-at-scale.md), [decision mechanisms](../research/decision-mechanisms.md).

## Article 4 — Votes, Draws and Offices

Article 4 secures the machinery the other Articles depend on, so no single failure or capture breaks it: secret, checked votes, public draws, certified candidates, and offices the Stewards cannot command.

**Why it says this:** 4.1 follows Estonia, where a paper vote cancels every online vote whatever the order ([digital voting](../research/digital-voting.md)). Key handling has already lost every electronic ballot in two real votes: the IACR voided and re-ran its November 2025 election after a trustee lost one of three required key shares [iacr/2025_election-update], and Basel-Stadt could not count 2,048 e-votes in March 2026. When any three of five key holders can act, losing two loses nothing. With paper, a failed audit leads to a hand count, so destroying a key share can no longer force a new vote. Draws follow the IETF's verifiable lottery, which anyone can check ([polycentric governance](../research/polycentric-governance.md)). The offices are written into the Charter because independence held in ordinary law can vanish, as it did for most US independent regulators in June 2026 ([executive and administration](../research/executive-and-administration.md)).

**Where it strains:** No system yet gives an online vote that is secret, verifiable, failure-tolerant and changeable at once (strain 8 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md)), and revoting fails against a coercer who stays until polls close. Whether every vote of all terrans should also be on paper, and whether the offices' eight-year terms are single, are open ([open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)). Only volunteers serve, so their traits creep into every draw, and capture of the certifying panels is a named risk.

**Read more:** [digital voting](../research/digital-voting.md), [executive and administration](../research/executive-and-administration.md).

## Article 5 — The Stewards

Article 5 sets up a small executive that can act but cannot rule. Seven Stewards carry out planetary acts with no veto and no army, and need approval from both an Oversight Council drawn by lot and the Council of Communities, either of which can remove them.

**Why it says this:** No election has ever covered humanity; on the research's estimates, one in-person planetary vote would reach about 5.9 billion adults and need about 6 million polling stations ([scale and federalism](../research/scale-and-federalism.md)). The model is Switzerland's seven-member Federal Council, a collegial body that Swiss voters have refused three times to elect directly [switzerland-fch/2026_brief-guide]. A body that watches the executive chooses it and can remove it, the route the research found best evidenced ([executive and administration](../research/executive-and-administration.md)). One term "counting any part of a term" answers a pattern: at least a third of incumbents who reached a term limit tried to stay, and FIFA's Council endorsed reading its president's 2016–2019 years as not a term. Only verified or sampled comments on draft rules count (5.3), because one US rulemaking drew nearly 18 million fake comments out of more than 22 million ([failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md)).

**Where it strains:** Two-key approval with a council drawn by lot is untested ([the government design](../proposals/modern-terran-government.md)). Collegial rule spreads blame, as the 1999 report on the Santer Commission showed. One-term rules resist capture but shift knowledge to permanent staff. None of the [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md) concerns the executive, and [open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md) marks the old questions on its term and removal settled.

**Read more:** [executive and administration](../research/executive-and-administration.md), [failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md).

## Article 6 — Emergencies

Article 6 lets the Commonwealth act fast in a crisis without letting a crisis become a path to rule by decree. Emergencies need a hazard listed in Schedule 5, expire unless panels drawn by lot renew them at rising thresholds, and can never change the Charter, postpone a vote, or stop the Court or the Ombudsman council.

**Why it says this:** In 60 democracies from 1974 to 2016, erosion was 75% more likely under a state of emergency [luhrmann-rooney/2021_autocratization-by-decree]. The more an emergency constitution favoured the executive, the more people died in natural disasters, controlling for severity ([failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md)). The 21-day limit, three-month extensions and rising thresholds build on South Africa's section 37. Its government then used a disaster law instead, for 750 days, so 6.2 defines an emergency measure by its effect, whatever law it uses. Continuity (6.3) resembles Germany's Joint Committee, which cannot amend the Basic Law, but also bars postponing votes, where Germany's model extends electoral terms. Urgent acts (6.4) follow Switzerland's urgent laws, which take effect at once, are time-limited, and cannot be renewed once rejected ([scale and federalism](../research/scale-and-federalism.md)).

**Where it strains:** Sunset clauses are often renewed without real review, "democracy's snooze button". Confirmation by a panel drawn in advance is an option from the research, not a precedent. Whether an urgent act can be ratified within its year, when the long-view wait is itself a year, is open ([open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)). After stage three no outside review exists. The [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md) predate the emergency clause.

**Read more:** [failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md), [scale and federalism](../research/scale-and-federalism.md).

## Article 7 — Accountability

Article 7 enforces the Charter in the open with courts and money, keeps force to arrests a court orders, and aims punishment at repair.

**Why it says this:** The enforcement ladder follows the EU's power to suspend funds over rule-of-law breaches. Hungary lost €1.04 billion when suspended funds lapsed at the end of 2024, but reformed only after its government lost the April 2026 election ([accountability](../research/accountability.md)). Single long terms have support: Strasbourg judges grew less likely to side with the states that nominated them once terms became non-renewable, in preliminary results. A fixed bench with automatic seating answers Poland, where presidents refused to swear in judges the Sejm had chosen ([failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md)). Norway's prisons, the credited model, reconvicted 19% of those released in 2022 within two years. The Ombudsman council draws on Sweden's office of 1809 and the Earth Constitution's council of five.

**Where it strains:** No modern state lets an allotted body choose judges, and Swiss voters rejected judges by lot 68% to 32% in 2021 [switzerland/2022_federal-council-decree-vote-results], so strain 5 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md) is answered only in part. Withholding funds reached Hungary's people, and the shield for final recipients was "largely ineffective" (strain 10). Open hearings and open logs clash with privacy law, which protects suspects, children and people with spent convictions as well as victims (strain 9). Open standing overloaded courts in Colombia, India and Hungary; how the Court filters cases, and whether ombudsmen serve single terms, are open ([open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)).

**Read more:** [accountability](../research/accountability.md), [failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md).

## Article 8 — The Long View

Article 8 gives future terrans and the living world a voice, since neither can vote. Guardians attach a long-term impact statement to every planetary act and can delay one a year for likely serious, irreversible harm, and whoever proposes a risky activity must show it is safe.

**Why it says this:** The drafting-session research tied lasting guardians to protected funding and independent appointment, and its [checks](../research/drafting-session-checks.md) found that the bodies that lasted only advise or require follow-or-explain. Wales' Commissioner is appointed, funded and removable by the ministers it scrutinizes, a weakness the protected budget and automatic successors avoid ([long view](../research/long-view.md)). The delay needs likely serious, irreversible harm, close to the line Ecuador's Constitutional Court drew for precaution in Los Cedros. The reversed burden of proof (8.6) follows Ecuador's Article 397 and the Wingspread Statement; under the EU's REACH law such a burden yields data, often poor.

**Where it strains:** Israel's commissioner rarely used its delay power because it drew antagonism, and the office was abolished over cost and "too much authority to interfere" [fdsd/2017_knesset-commission]. Follow or explain (8.5) outruns its Welsh model, which a Senedd committee said in March 2026 "lacks the power" to hold bodies to account (strain 11 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md)). Which scientific bodies nominate Guardians without nations is unanswered (strain 2). V.7 does not protect the Guardians' budget, so a change to the Articles under V.5 could cut it, and neither the share nor the number of Guardians is set. The standard of proof under 8.6 is open ([open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)).

**Read more:** [long view](../research/long-view.md), [drafting-session checks](../research/drafting-session-checks.md).

## Article 9 — Becoming the Commonwealth

Article 9 sets how the Commonwealth grows: by choice, in stages, never by force. Joining nations hand over weapons of mass destruction on joining, while armies go only when nine in ten terrans have joined.

**Why it says this:** The stage thresholds copy the population triggers of 50% and 90% in the Constitution for the Federation of Earth [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]. On armies the Charter is more cautious than its precedent, which disarms member forces at its second stage ([commonwealth](../research/commonwealth.md)). Authority over past acts (9.5) matches the ICC's practice in the Philippines case, where the Court kept jurisdiction over crimes committed while the state was a party. Bodies begin in advisory form (9.7) because binding decisions by samples have no track record ([the government design](../proposals/modern-terran-government.md)). The way out (9.8) answers part of the no-exit risk: Bryan Caplan puts lasting global totalitarianism at 0.1% if the number of countries holds and 25% with one world state, one economist's judgement ([failure modes](../research/failure-modes.md)).

**Where it strains:** Stage one has no non-aggression pledge, no paired joining for rivals and no named verifier, all of which the Earth Constitution has. Five nuclear-armed states holding 9.5% of humanity could stay outside through stage three, and before it a member could hand over its weapons, leave and rebuild (strain 7 in [pressure points](../thoughts/pressure-points.md)). Leaving by choice after stage three is still open (strain 6), and no state has ratified the Earth Constitution in 49 years (strain 11). Whether half and nine in ten are the right points is open ([open questions](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)).

**Read more:** [commonwealth](../research/commonwealth.md), [living charter](../research/living-charter.md).
