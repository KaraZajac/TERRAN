# Pressure points

*Where the first research pass says the working draft is under strain · compiled by Claude from the research notes, 10 October 2026*

Read Part by Part, most of the draft holds up against its precedents. Read across Parts, thirteen strains recur. In each, two clauses pull against each other, or a mechanism carries more than its precedent ever has. Each entry names the clauses, the evidence and the notes to read. The ways out are options, collected in [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

## 1. Bound by choice, or bound from birth?

The Preamble says the Charter belongs to "every terran who chooses to live by it". Part I makes everyone a terran from birth, and Part II's principles "bind every terran".

Principle IV adds that "those who take others' freedom without just cause cannot claim the Charter's protection for their own". That sits uneasily with I.1, where standing is something "no one can revoke", and with IX.4, which punishes no one without a hearing. The Satanic Temple's tenet behind Principle IV was a moral warning to "forgo one's own" freedom; in a constitution, forfeiture can strip legal protection.

Two strands found this independently. Read: [lineage](../research/lineage.md), [commonwealth](../research/commonwealth.md).

## 2. Science or the vote?

The ceiling "follows the best available science" (III) and "moves only as the science does" (XI.4). Yet planetary matters, "including the ceiling", are settled by all terrans (VII.4), and no body is named to certify the science.

Boundary values are judgements about acceptable risk. They were revised in both 2025 and 2026, and two expert groups measure nitrogen differently. The same gap recurs in the Guardians, half nominated by unnamed "scientific bodies" in a world without nations (X.3). It recurs again in the long-view test, which has no stated criteria and no named judge (X.4).

Read: [floor and ceiling](../research/floor-and-ceiling.md), [long view](../research/long-view.md), [living charter](../research/living-charter.md).

## 3. Already past the ceiling

Seven of nine planetary boundaries were crossed as of September 2026. So "no terran, community, or institution may push the planet past its limits" can't be met as written. Without a rule for dividing the ceiling into shares and timelines, the prohibition binds everyone or no one.

The floor can only rise (XI.4), while the ceiling only follows science. The 2018 "good life for all" study, with data to 2015, found that no country met basic needs within sustainable resource use. The draft has no rule for when the two collide. The studies that fit everyone inside assume deep cuts by the richest.

Read: [floor and ceiling](../research/floor-and-ceiling.md), [living charter](../research/living-charter.md), [wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md).

## 4. Hard to amend, easy to reinterpret

XI.3 needs two-thirds in two planetary votes a year apart. That is harder than any precedent the research found. In Nevada's two-vote system, the yes share fell on the second vote in 14 of 17 cases, by 8.7 points on average, though 13 of the 17 still passed by simple majority.

Meanwhile Principle VII lets "spirit" override the text, and any terran may bring a case (IX.1). Together these move change from voters to the Planetary Court, the pattern India's basic-structure doctrine shows. XI.4's limits don't protect themselves either, and there is no route to a new constitution.

Read: [living charter](../research/living-charter.md), [lineage](../research/lineage.md).

## 5. Who checks the Court?

The Planetary Court interprets by spirit, hears anyone, directs the inspectors (IX.3) and umpires most of the other strains. Its judges are chosen by an assembly drawn by lot, which no modern state the research found does. Swiss voters rejected choosing judges by lot, 68% to 32%, in 2021.

The Ombudsman oversees the courts (IX.10), but the draft doesn't say whether that reaches judgments or only administration.

Read: [accountability](../research/accountability.md), [deliberation](../research/deliberation.md), [living charter](../research/living-charter.md).

## 6. Exit that runs out

Individuals may leave communities (VI.3), and members may leave the Commonwealth until stage three (XII.5). After that, there is no exit and no route to start over. The research doc of 9 October warned that a world without exit needs much stronger checks on power. The source it cites is starker than it reported: Bryan Caplan puts the chance of lasting global totalitarianism at 0.1% if the number of countries holds, and 25% with one world state ([checks](../research/drafting-session-checks.md)).

Exit also protects less than it seems. Okin argues it is weakest for girls and women raised inside a community, and Sosis found secular 19th-century communes lasted 6.4 years on average.

And exit cuts against the Charter. Until stage three, the rich can leave the wealth limit: Norway lost 82 wealthy residents in 2022–23 alone. A community that leaves keeps its land, air and emissions.

Read: [lineage](../research/lineage.md), [living charter](../research/living-charter.md), [wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md), [commonwealth](../research/commonwealth.md).

## 7. Disarming in a world still armed

Stage one asks members to hand over weapons of mass destruction while their neighbours may stay armed. It has no non-aggression pledge, no paired joining for rivals and no named verifier. The Earth Constitution, the credited precedent, has all three: a pledge (17.1.10), paired joining (17.1.09) and a World Disarmament Agency.

Stage three dismantles armies while five nuclear-armed states, holding 9.5% of humanity, could stay outside. Before stage three, a member could hand over its weapons, leave and rebuild.

Read: [commonwealth](../research/commonwealth.md).

## 8. A digital state that must be secure, private and universal at once

The digital ID is issued at birth and needed to vote, which makes it compulsory in practice. Courts in India and Jamaica pushed back on compulsory ID, and EU law keeps its wallet voluntary. Enrolling everyone once, "from anywhere", without state registries or compulsory biometrics is unsolved.

No system yet gives a secret, verifiable, failure-tolerant and changeable online vote. Key handling has already lost every electronic ballot in two real votes. The IACR voided and re-ran its 2025 election after a trustee lost a key share, and Basel-Stadt couldn't count 2,048 e-votes in 2026 (outcomes unaffected). As written, VIII.5 even lets a later online vote override an in-person one.

Read: [digital voting](../research/digital-voting.md).

## 9. Open ledgers against privacy

Transparency scales with power (VII.6), but no thresholds are set. Enforcement actions go on open ledgers (IX.10) with a privacy exception for victims only, and hearings are open (IX.4).

Existing law points the other way. The EU Court of Justice struck down general public access to beneficial-ownership registers in 2022, and a public register of traffic penalty points in 2021. And a blockchain can't forget a spent conviction.

Read: [accountability](../research/accountability.md), [digital voting](../research/digital-voting.md), [wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md).

## 10. Withholding money without punishing populations

The enforcement ladder withholds "funds and benefits" (IX.6), while penalties must never fall on whole communities (IX.5). In Hungary's case, the shield for final recipients was "largely ineffective", and the leverage worked only after an election changed the government.

Read: [accountability](../research/accountability.md).

## 11. Precedents scaled up a thousandfold

Most credited precedents are smaller and weaker than the clauses built on them:

| Credited precedent | What the record shows |
| --- | --- |
| Ireland's assemblies | Advisory, with none running in October 2026 |
| vTaiwan | Its 28 cases were all non-binding |
| Wales' follow-or-explain duty | Narrow; a Senedd committee said in March 2026 that the Act "lacks the power" to hold bodies to account |
| New Zealand's river guardians | Their legitimacy comes from iwi relationships and a Treaty settlement, not lot or expertise |
| Nordic prisons | Not one model: two-year reconviction runs from 14% to 40% |
| Constitution for the Federation of Earth | No country has ratified it in 49 years |

The Charter applies each at planetary scale. That isn't a reason to drop them. But its clauses carry more than their precedents have ever borne, so the safeguards around them matter more.

Read: [deliberation](../research/deliberation.md), [long view](../research/long-view.md), [accountability](../research/accountability.md), [commonwealth](../research/commonwealth.md).

## 12. A charter without nations, priced in one nation's money

Every amount is in US dollars, adjusted by an unnamed index. The obvious index, US CPI, published no October 2025 index for most series because of a government shutdown.

At market exchange rates, $10 million buys about five times more in India than in Switzerland, so the limit binds far harder in Switzerland. Using purchasing-power parity reverses the distortion. "Its own money" names no issuer, and a central bank would combine powers that VIII.8 keeps apart.

Read: [money](../research/money.md), [wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md).

## 13. Money out of politics, or organizing out of politics?

V.2 bars every organization from spending "to influence a vote or a public decision". As written, that catches parties, unions, NGOs, groups campaigning for or against a Part VIII proposal, and ordinary lobbying.

VIII.10 guarantees the right to "organize peaceful opposition to any decision", and organizing usually costs money. No democracy the research found bans spending by NGOs, unions or campaign groups; they register them and cap their spending.

The European Court of Human Rights has gone both ways. It found a £5 limit on one campaigner disproportionate (*Bowman*, 1998). But it upheld the UK's ban on paid political broadcast ads by campaigning bodies, 9 votes to 8 (*Animal Defenders*, 2013).

"The press stays free" also lacks a working definition. US courts doubted media companies can be told apart from other companies. The UK and Canada exempt news and editorial content, not media companies.

Read: [business](../research/business.md), [deliberation](../research/deliberation.md).
