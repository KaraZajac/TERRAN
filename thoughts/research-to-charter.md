# From research to Charter

*How the drafting session's research of 9 October 2026 became the working draft of 10 October 2026 · traced by Claude from the two documents and the research notes, 10 October 2026*

The draft took almost all of the research's advice, often nearly word for word. This file traces 37 recommendations and findings from the [research doc](../research/drafting-session-2026-10-09.md) into the [Charter](../CHARTER.md):

- 30 adopted
- 4 adapted
- 1 left out: a minimum-turnout rule for binding votes
- 2 left open, as the Charter's open questions on paper votes and on leaving after stage three

Several parts of the draft were not researched in the doc at all. They are listed at the end.

## Pay ratios

| # | What the research said | What the Charter says | Outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Measure against the lowest full-time-equivalent pay, counting contractors, franchise and outsourced workers. | V.3 measures against the "lowest full-time pay", and "the lowest pay counts contractors and outsourced workers". | Adapted: "full-time-equivalent" became "full-time", which invites hiring part-timers to lower the ratio ([business note](../research/business.md)); franchise workers aren't named |
| 2 | Measure the top as total pay, stock included, averaged over several years. | V.3: "the highest counts stock and is averaged over several years." | Adopted |
| 3 | A 1,000:1 line is far looser than any precedent: binding caps run 6:1 to 35:1, and tax schemes start at 50:1 to 100:1. | V.3 starts the tax at 50 times the lowest pay and takes everything above 100 times. | Adopted: the draft moved into the precedent range |
| 4 | A graduated tax starting near 50:1 and rising steeply fits Kara's "softer-limits idea" better than a single cutoff. | V.3: pay above 50 times "is taxed on a steeply rising scale", and pay above 100 times "goes entirely to the Commons Fund". | Adopted, ending in a 100% rate above 100 times |

## Wealth limit and taxes

| # | What the research said | What the Charter says | Outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Use a glide path, not a cliff: rates that rise with wealth and reach 100% at the limit, so the limit is both soft and hard. | IV.5: a wealth tax that "starts at 1% and rises toward the limit", with "the exact rates" set by vote. IV.4: wealth above $10 million passes to the Commons Fund each year. | Adapted: the start and the end are fixed, but the slope between is left to a vote. IV.6's cap would override any steep schedule above about $4 million ([wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md)) |
| 6 | Reference points for the limit: €1M (Robeyns' personal limit), €2.2M (what most Dutch survey respondents called more than enough), €10M (Robeyns' political limit), $32–50M (where US wealth-tax plans began). | IV.4: a $10 million limit, Robeyns' political limit restated in dollars. IV.5's $2 million tax-free threshold matches €2.2M only in size: that figure is a mean for a family of four, excluding pensions, while IV.5's is per person ([wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md)). | Adopted: one reference point (€10M) |
| 7 | Report every asset class, and look through companies, trusts and family members. | IV.8 "Nothing hidden": every asset is reported, and wealth held through companies, trusts or relatives counts as the owner's. | Adopted |
| 8 | Let founders pay in shares to a commons fund, or defer with interest, instead of forcing fire sales. | IV.7 "Paying in shares", with the shares passing to the Commons Fund. | Adapted: shares kept, deferral with interest dropped |
| 9 | Cap the combined tax on people below the limit, so taxes never take more than their assets earn. | IV.6 "A fair burden": combined taxes never take more than "their income and assets earn in a year". | Adopted, widened to count income too |

## Direct digital democracy

| # | What the research said | What the Charter says | Outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 13 | Use digital ID to prove a person may vote, with selective disclosure, and keep ballot secrecy separate from identity. | VIII.5: identity "proves they may vote but never reveals how they voted". | Adopted; selective disclosure isn't named |
| 14 | Put deliberation before each planetary vote: an assembly chosen at random reviews the proposal, and the ballot matches what it recommends. The research cites Ireland's 2024 referendums, lost after the government rewrote an assembly's proposal. | VIII.2–VIII.4: common ground is mapped, an assembly drawn by lot writes the ballot, and terrans vote "exactly as the assembly wrote it". | Adopted. The [deliberation note](../research/deliberation.md) finds the rewrite documented only for the Care amendment, and no evidence that it caused the defeats |
| 15 | Keep a non-digital way to vote, audits after every vote, and backup keys, so one failure never voids a decision. | VIII.5: an in-person vote replaces any online one. VIII.6: every result is audited, Charter votes are also cast on paper, and keys are held by several independent bodies. | Adopted, with a gap: a failed audit means a new vote and no key threshold is set, so destroying one key share could force a new vote ([digital voting](../research/digital-voting.md)) |
| 16 | Entrench the Seven Principles so no majority can override them. | XI.4: no amendment may weaken any terran's protection under them. | Adopted |
| 17 | Expect low, selective turnout; binding votes may need a minimum participation rule. | VIII.4: "A majority decides, except where this Charter requires more." There is no turnout floor. | Left out |
| 18 | Remote online voting cannot yet be secured. | VIII.5 keeps online voting for ordinary votes. VIII.6 adds paper for Charter votes. | Open question: "Paper votes" |

## Global enforcement and checks on power

| # | What the research said | What the Charter says | Outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 19 | Enforce through a planetary court open to individuals, plus the power to withhold funds and benefits. | IX.1: any terran may bring a case. IX.6: findings are published, then funds and benefits withheld, then assets reclaimed. | Adopted |
| 20 | Give the Commonwealth independent revenue and infrastructure, and cap any single region's share of revenue. | XII.3: the Commonwealth takes on planetary revenue at stage two. X.7: the Guardians get a fixed share of it. | Adapted: revenue kept, regional cap dropped |
| 21 | Put an independent ombudsman over every agency, including the enforcers. | VIII.9 and IX.10: the Ombudsman may investigate any institution, including courts, inspectors and peace officers. | Adopted |
| 22 | Build internal exits: free movement between communities, and possibly a lawful opt-out procedure. | VI.3 and VIII.10 guarantee exit from communities and free movement. XII.5 allows leaving until stage three. | Open question: "Leaving after stage three" |
| 23 | Make jurisdiction over past acts survive any departure (the ICC model). | XII.5: the Court "keeps its authority over acts committed while it was a member". | Adopted. It matches ICC practice: in the Philippines case the Court kept jurisdiction over crimes committed while the state was a party, though Rome Statute Art. 127(2)'s text is narrower ([commonwealth](../research/commonwealth.md), [living charter](../research/living-charter.md)) |
| 24 | Courts and money, not force. | IX.6: an enforcement ladder of publishing, withholding and reclaiming. Only a court may order an arrest. | Adopted |
| 25 | Target individuals, not populations. | IX.5 "People answer, not populations". IX.6: peace officers carry only what they need to detain an individual. | Adopted |
| 26 | No vetoes, and no unanimity for sanctions. | VIII.7: the Stewards hold no veto. Enforcement runs through the Court. | Adopted |
| 27 | Transition in stages, as the Earth Constitution proposes: weapons of mass destruction handed over first, national militaries dismantled only at the end. | XII.2 (stage one) and XII.4 (stage three). | Adopted. The research misread its source: the Earth Constitution disarms member nations' forces at its second stage (Art. 17.4.06), so the Charter's armies-last staging is more cautious than its precedent ([commonwealth note](../research/commonwealth.md)) |
| 28 | Sovereignty pooled voluntarily, treaty by treaty, with a legal exit, as the EU did. | XII.1: joining is by choice. XII.5: leaving is allowed until stage three. | Adopted |

## Objections the draft answers, and the ones it leaves open

The research doc named the strongest case against each idea. The draft answers most of them.

| Objection raised in the research | The draft's answer |
| --- | --- |
| A wealth tax can pass 100% of income on low-return assets. | IV.6 caps combined tax at what income and assets earn. The [wealth and tax note](../research/wealth-and-tax.md) warns that caps of this kind let France's richest 0.001% pay about 0.1% of their wealth. |
| Founders rich on paper may have to sell or shut down. | IV.7 lets them pay in shares. |
| Banning organizations but not individuals shifts power to the very rich. | V.2 caps individual spending too. |
| Co-ops become two-tier. | V.4 makes long-term workers members. |
| A ban on all organizations also hits nonprofits, unions and the press. | V.2 keeps the press free. Nonprofits and unions are still barred from spending on votes. |
| One world authority removes the two historical checks on tyranny: somewhere to flee, and rival states. | The draft strengthens voice: the Ombudsman (VIII.9), free movement (VIII.10) and entrenched principles (XI.4). Exit stays open until stage three (XII.5); after that, it's an open question. |
| A pay ratio punishes low-wage industries more than high-wage ones at the same top pay. | **Not addressed.** |
| Remote online voting can't yet be secured. | **Partly addressed.** In-person votes override online ones (VIII.5) and Charter votes go on paper (VIII.6). The rest is the "Paper votes" open question. |

## Parts the research doc did not research

The research doc presupposes some of these, recommending that the Seven Principles be entrenched and assuming Guardians and planetary votes, but it researched none of them:

- **The Seven Principles** (Part II). They adapt The Satanic Temple's Seven Tenets in number, order and substance, and the draft credits no source ([lineage note](../research/lineage.md)).
- **The doughnut itself** (Part III): the floor's eleven items and the nine planetary boundaries, credited to Kate Raworth and the planetary-boundaries framework.
- **The culture of enough** (IV.1–IV.3) and **the commons** (IV.9).
- **Worker board seats at one third** (V.5). The research cites Germany's half-the-board rule above 2,000 staff; the draft chose "at least a third".
- **A utopia of utopias** (Part VI). It restates Robert Nozick's "framework for utopia" from *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (1974), also uncredited, and pairs it with a floor and a wealth limit that Nozick opposed ([lineage note](../research/lineage.md)).
- **Digital citizenship from birth, open ledgers and blockchain, and transparency that scales with power** (VII.2, VII.5, VII.6). The research found that blockchain "fixes none of internet voting's problems". VII.5 limits it to "wherever it truly helps".
- **Judges chosen by lot-drawn assemblies, inspectors who answer to the Court, and humane confinement** (IX.2, IX.3, IX.9).
- **The amendment rule and the review each generation** (XI.3, XI.5).
- **The stage thresholds**: half of humanity, then nine in ten (XII.3, XII.4).

How these entered the draft isn't recorded; the drafting conversation itself wasn't kept, only its two documents. The Charter credits sources for Part III, VII.2, IX.9 and the stages. For Parts II and VI, the lineage note identifies uncredited sources. The research notes in [research/](../research/README.md) now cover all of these parts.
