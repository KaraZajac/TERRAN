# How the Charter is built

*A reading of the first working draft, 10 October 2026 · by Claude · where this reading and Kara's intent differ, hers governs · it cites first-draft clauses, several of which the second draft changed ([changelog](../CHANGELOG.md)); the nine moves still describe the second and third drafts, and the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md#third-working-draft--12-october-2026) maps the Parts cited here to the third draft's Articles*

The Charter applies one test everywhere: does this bring more terrans inside the doughnut and keep them there (VII.7)? Most of its other clauses are machinery for passing that test without creating a power that could abuse it. Nine design moves recur across the twelve Parts. Naming them makes the draft easier to extend consistently, and shows where a new clause would break the pattern.

| Job | Parts | Question answered |

## Nine recurring moves

**1. One test.** The floor and the ceiling (Part III) define the test. "The economy exists for one purpose": passing it. Every institution answers to it (VII.7). Freedom stops at it: one terran's utopia ends where it would push another below the floor (VI.5). Amendment stops at it too: the floor can't be lowered, and the ceiling moves only with science (XI.4).

**2. Floor and ceiling at every scale.** The doughnut's shape repeats inside the Charter:

| Domain | Floor | Ceiling | Clause |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Living standards | The eleven social needs | The nine planetary boundaries | III |
| Wealth | First $2 million tax-free | $10 million limit | IV.4–IV.5 |
| Pay | Tax from 50 times the lowest pay | Everything above 100 times goes to the Commons Fund | V.3 |
| Tax burden | | Combined taxes never exceed what income and assets earn | IV.6 |
| Liberty | The same freedom for every terran | Confinement only to protect others | Principle IV, IX.9 |
| Amendment | No lowering the floor or the Principles | The ceiling moves only as science does | XI.4 |

**3. Soft limits that harden.** Limits arrive as rising rates that end in a hard line. Pay is taxed on a steeply rising scale from 50 times the lowest pay, and everything above 100 times goes to the Commons Fund (V.3). The research doc calls this Kara's "softer-limits idea" ([research](../research/drafting-session-2026-10-09.md)). Wealth has the same shape in outline: a tax from 1% above $2 million that "rises toward the limit", and everything above $10 million passing to the Commons Fund (IV.4–IV.5). The slope between is left to a vote, so the wealth limit is a gradual climb only if the vote makes it one ([wealth and tax](../research/wealth-and-tax.md)).

**4. No single point of failure.** This is security engineering applied to government:

- powers split four ways: law, administration, courts and Guardians (VIII.8)
- voting keys held by several independent bodies (VIII.6)
- Charter votes also cast on paper, so they "never depend on software alone" (VIII.6)
- Guardians' successors chosen automatically (X.3)
- Stewards with no veto and no army (VIII.7)
- an Ombudsman who may investigate any institution, including the courts and the enforcers (VIII.9, IX.10)

**5. Reversible by design.** Votes can be changed, and an in-person vote overrides an online one, so a vote cast under pressure can be undone (VIII.5). A failed audit means a new vote (VIII.6). Mistakes create a duty to repair (Principle VI). Punishment exists "to restore, not to destroy" (Part IX). The Charter itself "can be wrong, so it is built to change" (XI).

**6. Time as a check.** The Charter adds delay where the stakes are high:

- a year's wait and a second vote for proposals that fail the long-view test (X.4)
- two votes at least a year apart for amendments (XI.3)
- one ten-year term for Guardians (X.3) and one long term for judges (IX.2)
- a review of the whole Charter every 25 years (XI.5)

**7. Consent, not force.** Every person is a terran from birth (I.1), but every institutional step is chosen. Communities, organizations and nations join by adopting the Charter (XII.1). Communities are chosen and can be left (VI.2–VI.3, VIII.10), and members can leave until stage three (XII.5). The Commonwealth grows "by what it offers ... never by conquest" (XII.6).

**8. Scrutiny scales with power.** Everyday private life stays private, while wealth and power bring transparency (VII.6). People who knowingly use an institution to push terrans below the floor or the planet past the ceiling answer personally (IX.5). Organizations can't buy votes, and individuals' spending on votes is capped (V.2).

**9. Borrow what exists.** Most mechanisms adapt an existing institution or proposal, and the draft credits it in an italic "draws on" or "adapted from" note:

- Estonia's digital state and e-Residency
- Ireland's citizens' assemblies and Taiwan's vTaiwan
- Germany's codetermination and Mondragon's cooperatives
- the EU's courts and funding conditions
- Norway's prisons
- Wales' Future Generations Commissioner, New Zealand's river guardians and Ecuador's rights of nature
- the ICC and the Constitution for the Federation of Earth

The research notes test each precedent against the record: [research/](../research/README.md).

## Where the moves pull against each other

The moves are consistent, but not frictionless:

- **One test vs. a utopia of utopias.** The ceiling is planetary, so a utopia that uses more than its share of it shrinks everyone else's. VI.5 stops a utopia at the ceiling, but the Charter doesn't say how shares of the ceiling are divided among communities.
- **Consent vs. no exit.** Move 7 holds until stage three. After it, exit from the Commonwealth itself is the open question "Leaving after stage three", and the research doc's warning about the no-exit problem applies with full force.
- **Reversibility vs. time.** Votes can be changed at will (move 5), yet some decisions are slowed by a year or more (move 6). Which decisions sit on which side is decided case by case, not by a stated rule.
- **No single point of failure vs. one Commonwealth.** The Charter splits power inside the Commonwealth, but the historical check of rival states shrinks at stage three, when nine in ten terrans are inside. Almost everything rests on the internal checks working.

These tensions, and the others the research notes surface, are collected in [pressure-points.md](pressure-points.md).
