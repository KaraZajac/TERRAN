# The floor and the ceiling: Raworth's doughnut, planetary boundaries and the doughnut test

*Research note · Charter Preamble, Part III, Part VII.7 (with VII.4 and XI.4) · first pass, 10 October 2026 · compiled by Claude, not yet reviewed by Kara*

The Charter's floor mixes Raworth's 2012 and 2017 lists, two versions behind her 2025 list, and its ceiling uses some retired names. Seven of nine boundaries were crossed as of 21 September 2026, so the no-transgression rule needs a fair-share reading. Boundary values are precautionary value judgements, revised in both 2025 and 2026, which sits badly with "not negotiable" and with naming no body to adopt them. No dataset counts people "inside the doughnut", and studies that find room for everyone assume deep cuts at the top.

## What the Charter says

- The Preamble puts "a safe and just space for humanity" between a floor and a ceiling. It holds that "the planet's limits are not negotiable."
- Part III lists 11 floor entitlements and nine boundaries in plain language. "No terran, community, or institution may push the planet past its limits."
- Part III says the ceiling "follows the best available science and moves only as that science does"; Part XI.4 repeats this.
- Success is "how many terrans live inside the doughnut and how far we stand from each boundary" (Part III). Part VII.7 asks every institution the same question.
- Part VII.4 settles planetary matters, "including the ceiling", by all terrans. Part XI.4 bars amendments that lower the floor.

## 1. Raworth's doughnut in three versions

Raworth's 2012 Oxfam paper drew the first doughnut for the Rio+20 summit [raworth/2012_safe-and-just-space]. Its 11 social dimensions were "illustrative", drawn from governments' Rio+20 submissions [raworth/2012_safe-and-just-space]. Voice, jobs and resilience had only example indicators, with data "To be determined" [raworth/2012_safe-and-just-space]. The paper called both rings "essentially normative boundaries" [raworth/2012_safe-and-just-space].

The 2017 version, in the book and a journal article, had 12 dimensions based on the 2015 Sustainable Development Goals [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut] [raworth/2017_doughnut-economics]. The 2025 version, with Andrew Fanning, kept 12 and renamed three [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut]. It tracks 22 social and 13 ecological indicators from 2000, updated yearly [deal/2026_what-is-the-doughnut] [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut]. Raworth expects more change as science, social commitments and data evolve [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut].

The Charter's list is the 2012 list, with income and jobs merged and housing added from 2017. Glosses like "lifelong learning" and "meaningful work" match no indicator in any version [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut]. DEAL calls Doughnut Economics "not a set of policies and institutions, but rather a way of thinking" [deal/2026_about-doughnut-economics].

**For the Charter:** The Charter turns a compass into binding entitlements, a departure from DEAL's framing. Its list is neither Raworth's current one nor an argued alternative. Because Part XI.4 bars lowering the floor, the draft should say how items can be updated without that counting as lowering.

## 2. Planetary boundaries, 2009–2026

Rockström and 28 co-authors proposed nine boundaries in *Nature* on 23 September 2009 [rockstrom/2009_safe-operating-space] [biermann/2020_boundaries-of-pb-framework]. The companion paper calls boundaries "human-determined values" set at a safe distance from danger [rockstrom/2009_planetary-boundaries-ecology-society]. Setting that distance "involves normative judgments of how societies choose to deal with risk and uncertainty" [rockstrom/2009_planetary-boundaries-ecology-society]. Steffen et al. (2015) added regional sub-boundaries and found four boundaries crossed [steffen/2015_planetary-boundaries-update]. How societies develop, they added, involves "political decisions that must include consideration of the human dimensions, including equity" [steffen/2015_planetary-boundaries-update]. Richardson et al. (2023) proposed boundaries for every component and found six crossed [richardson/2023_six-of-nine]. Precaution puts each boundary "at the lower end of the zone of increasing risk" [richardson/2023_six-of-nine].

PIK's Planetary Boundaries Science lab has published an annual Planetary Health Check since 2024 [pik/2024_planetary-health-check]. The 2024 check found six crossed; ocean acidification stood at 2.80 against a 2.75 boundary [pik/2024_planetary-health-check]. On 24 September 2025, PIK reported ocean acidification crossed "for the first time", making seven [pik/2025_seven-of-nine-press-release]. A revised pre-industrial baseline (3.57, not 3.44) had raised the boundary to 2.86 [pik/2025_planetary-health-check] [richardson/2023_six-of-nine]. The 2026 check (21 September 2026) again found seven, all "at their highest recorded levels of transgression" [pik/2026_phc-press-release]. DEAL's 2025 doughnut, on the older baseline, still counts six crossed by 2022 [deal/2026_what-is-the-doughnut] [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut].

The numbers move. Since 2023 the land boundary rose from 75% to 78%, and the water boundaries from 10.2% and 11.1% to 12.9% and 12.4% [pik/2026_planetary-health-check]. The forest-cover estimate rose from about 59% to 64% through a corrected calculation, "not actual forest recovery" [pik/2026_planetary-health-check]. Richardson et al. excluded natural elements that humans mobilise from novel entities [richardson/2023_six-of-nine]. The 2026 check includes "human-made and -mobilized" materials and calls its own control variable "not actionable" [pik/2026_planetary-health-check]. Raworth chose plain names "for ease of comprehension by a wide public audience" [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut].

**For the Charter:** The nine processes match, but "freshwater use" is out of date and changes the verdict. "Biodiversity loss", "air pollution" and "chemical pollution" narrow or shift what is measured. Plain names work if a schedule ties each to its official variable. "Not negotiable" fits the physical system, not the boundary values.

## 3. "Safe and just": the Earth Commission

The Earth Commission published "safe and just Earth system boundaries" in *Nature* on 31 May 2023 [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. Safe boundaries protect Earth-system stability. Just boundaries minimise "exposure to significant harm to humans from Earth system change" [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. The stricter of the two governs [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs].

Justice tightens climate and aerosols most [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. The safe climate boundary is 1.5°C; the just one is "at or below 1.0 °C" [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. At 1.0°C, "tens of millions" already faced extreme humid heat [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. Warming was 1.2°C, and a return below 1.0°C "may not be achievable in the foreseeable future" [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. The authors therefore call for adaptation and compensation [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. For aerosols, justice adds a health limit of 15 µg/m³ annual mean PM2.5 [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. For nitrogen, it trims the global surplus limit from 61 to 57 Tg N/yr [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. Seven of eight global boundaries were already exceeded [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs].

The authors call just boundaries "necessary but not sufficient"; justice also needs access to resources and fair procedures [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. They sketch a "corridor": minimum access to water, food, energy and infrastructure below the ceiling [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. Their criteria include interspecies justice, "rejecting human exceptionalism" [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. Their nitrogen variable (surplus) differs from the boundary framework's (fixation) [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs] [pik/2026_planetary-health-check].

**For the Charter:** The Charter uses "just" in Raworth's sense: the floor. The Earth Commission also uses justice to lower the ceiling, because a breached ceiling harms people near the floor. On that reading the climate ceiling is already broken and cannot soon be restored. The Charter has no duty to protect or compensate those harmed meanwhile; Part IX.7 returns wealth to the commons, not to victims.

## 4. Measuring progress and the doughnut test

O'Neill et al. (2018) compared needs met with downscaled boundaries in over 150 countries. They found that "no country meets basic needs for its citizens at a globally sustainable level of resource use" [oneill/2018_good-life-for-all]. Fanning et al. (2022) followed more than 140 countries from 1992 to 2015 [fanning/2022_shortfall-overshoot-nations]. Countries crossed boundaries faster than they met social thresholds [fanning/2022_shortfall-overshoot-nations]. The project's website still shows data only to 2015 [leeds/2026_good-life-for-all-site].

Fanning and Raworth (2025) made the global doughnut an annual monitor for 2000–2022 [fanning/2025_doughnut-world-out-of-balance]. GDP more than doubled, but progress on deprivation must run five times faster to meet all needs by 2030 [fanning/2025_doughnut-world-out-of-balance]. The richest 20% of countries (15% of people) caused over 40% of overshoot. The poorest 40% (42% of people) bore over 60% of shortfall [fanning/2025_doughnut-world-out-of-balance]. Median shortfall in 2022 was 35% of people, about 3 billion [deal/2026_what-is-the-doughnut]. That supports the Preamble's "billions without enough".

**Per person.** I found no estimate of how many people clear every social threshold at once. The doughnut reports the share short on each indicator, then a median [deal/2026_what-is-the-doughnut]. Some indicators give residents their country's condition, such as living under "an autocratic regime" [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut]. The ecological ring cannot be split by country in a directly comparable way [fanning/2025_doughnut-world-out-of-balance]. Their country-group analysis had equal per-person shares for only four of nine boundaries [fanning/2025_doughnut-world-out-of-balance]. Data on inequality "across the whole global population" are "not yet available" [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut]. Carbon is the exception. Chancel (2022) estimated that the bottom 50% emitted 12% of greenhouse gases in 2019, and the top 10% emitted 48% [chancel/2022_global-carbon-inequality]. Those are group estimates, not individual measurements.

A per-person count would need household surveys on each floor item, like a multidimensional poverty index; the UN's 2026 dashboard already uses one [un/2026_beyond-gdp-hleg-report]. It would also need a rule giving each person a share of each boundary, and footprints by income group. If "inside" requires the planet itself to be within the ceiling, nobody is inside today. Distance to each boundary, by contrast, is published yearly for 13 control variables [pik/2026_planetary-health-check].

**Adoption.** Amsterdam launched its City Doughnut on 8 April 2020 [raworth/2020_amsterdam-city-doughnut]. Raworth called it "not a report and assessment of Amsterdam", but a workshop tool [raworth/2020_amsterdam-city-doughnut]. The city aims to halve new raw materials by 2030 and be fully circular by 2050. Its Circular Monitor (2022) tracks materials; social impacts were still to be added as of January 2024 [emf/2024_amsterdam-circular]. DEAL lists portraits for Barcelona, Brussels, Copenhagen, Cornwall and others [deal/2026_data-portraits-in-action]. Copenhagen has produced one yearly since 2023 to inform budget priorities [deal/2026_data-portraits-in-action]. I found no outcome evaluation for Amsterdam, and did not verify any national adoption.

**UN Beyond GDP.** An expert group presented "Counting What Counts" to member states on 7 May 2026 [un/2026_beyond-gdp-hleg-report]. It proposes 31 indicators, with "Respect for the planet, including for planetary boundaries" as a founding principle [un/2026_beyond-gdp-hleg-report]. Only two indicators cover boundaries: greenhouse gas emissions and biodiversity intactness [un/2026_beyond-gdp-hleg-report]. Raworth and Fanning are among those thanked [un/2026_beyond-gdp-hleg-report]. Member states are still negotiating, with Spain and Guyana as co-facilitators since April 2026; I found no adopted outcome [un/2026_beyond-gdp-intergovernmental].

**For the Charter:** "How far we stand from each boundary" is measurable now. A per-person count is not, and building one would mean tracking consumption, which collides with Part VII.6 on privacy. Every real doughnut is advisory; Part VII.7 would be the first binding one.

## 5. Can everyone be inside at once?

Raworth's 2012 arithmetic used low floors. Feeding the 13% who were hungry would take 1% of the food supply; ending $1.25-a-day poverty would take 0.2% of global income [raworth/2012_safe-and-just-space]. Rao and Min (2018) list decent living standards, including clothing, mobility and communication [rao/2018_decent-living-standards]. The Charter's floor omits all three. They call these standards "instrumental (but not sufficient)" for wellbeing [rao/2018_decent-living-standards].

Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) costed decent living for everyone in 2050 at 149 EJ of final energy, 15.3 GJ per person per year [millward-hopkins/2020_decent-living-minimum-energy]. That is over 60% below today's use with about 30% more people [millward-hopkins/2020_decent-living-minimum-energy]. It assumes the best technology everywhere and sufficiency "regardless of income" [millward-hopkins/2020_decent-living-minimum-energy]. The authors "entirely avoided the most difficult question": how to get there [millward-hopkins/2020_decent-living-minimum-energy].

Distribution changes the answer. Even "fair" inequality raises the energy needed by 40% [millward-hopkins/2022_inequality-decent-living-energy]. A super-rich 1% could use as much energy as decent living for 1.7 billion people [millward-hopkins/2022_inequality-decent-living-energy]. Minimum access for all in 2018, with existing inequality and technology, would have added 2–26% to pressure on climate, water, land and nutrients [rammelt/2022_minimum-access-great-inequality]. That equals the impact of the richest 1–4% [rammelt/2022_minimum-access-great-inequality]. Physical needs could likely be met within boundaries. High life satisfaction for all would need 2–6 times the sustainable level, at current relationships [oneill/2018_good-life-for-all].

**For the Charter:** A material floor fits under the ceiling only on four conditions. They are deep cuts at the top, best technology, sufficiency-based public services and low inequality. Part IV's wealth limit helps; the Charter says nothing on provisioning or technology. Its floor reaches past material needs ("thrive", "meaningful work"), where the evidence is weakest.

## 6. Critiques

**Of the boundaries.** Brook et al. (2013) found planetary tipping points in the land biosphere "unconfirmed" and single global aggregates "inapt" [brook/2013_terrestrial-tipping-points]. Montoya, Donohue and Pimm said a safe space for biodiversity "encourages harmful policies" [montoya/2018_implausible-science]. Rockström and colleagues replied that a boundary "is not equivalent to a global threshold or tipping point" [rockstrom/2018_separating-fact-from-fiction]. They added that downscaling "has never been either proposed or encouraged" [rockstrom/2018_separating-fact-from-fiction]. The rejoinder asked: "Why a Planetary Boundary, If It Is Not Planetary, and the Boundary Is Undefined?" [montoya/2018_reply-to-rockstrom].

**Of the doughnut.** Schokkaert (2019), a sympathetic reviewer, says the book skips how to secure decent consumption for all within Earth's limits [schokkaert/2019_review-doughnut-economics]. Teicher (2021) says it "fails to confront the power relations that stop the economy from serving most people's needs" [teicher/2021_doughnut-hole-at-core]. Fanning and Raworth concede "no directly traceable link" between the two rings [fanning/2025_doughnut-world-out-of-balance]. Acharya, Geloso and Psurek (2026) found "little support" for a capitalism-causes-imbalance version of the model, "as here formalized" [acharya/2026_hole-in-the-doughnut].

**On growth.** Raworth asks readers to become "agnostic about growth" (book p. 287, quoted by Schokkaert) [schokkaert/2019_review-doughnut-economics]. Milanovic said the book does not acknowledge that global GDP "must rise significantly to end poverty" [alternative-uk/2018_raworth-reply-to-milanovic]. Raworth replied that growth "is very much needed" in low- and middle-income countries, home to 80% of people [alternative-uk/2018_raworth-reply-to-milanovic]. Vogel and Hickel (2023) found 11 rich countries cut CO₂ while growing in 2013–2019. At those rates, a 95% cut would take over 220 years [vogel/2023_is-green-growth-happening].

**For the Charter:** The Charter answers part of the "missing mechanisms" critique with a wealth limit, worker ownership and courts. It is silent on growth. The boundary critiques bite hardest where it binds every "terran, community, or institution" to global limits.

## 7. Who sets the ceiling

The 2009 group had 29 experts, almost all natural scientists. Input from civil society or governments was "not systematically sought after" [biermann/2020_boundaries-of-pb-framework]. PIK's lab, a partnership formed at the end of 2023, now updates values yearly [pik/2024_planetary-health-check]. The 2026 check has more than 60 authors [pik/2026_phc-press-release]. It sets boundaries "through a broad scientific assessment", drawing on IPCC and IPBES reports and expert judgement [pik/2026_planetary-health-check-summary]. The lab works closely with the "Planetary Guardians", who want boundaries to be "a measurement and operating framework for the world" [pik/2024_planetary-health-check] [pik/2026_phc-press-release].

The Earth Commission offers its numbers as "a transparent proposal for further debate and refinement by scholars and wider society" [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. Biermann and Kim call it "in no way legitimatized by the United Nations" [biermann/2020_boundaries-of-pb-framework]. They warn that humanity's operating space could be "decided by professors, not by the people" [biermann/2020_boundaries-of-pb-framework]. They cite Pickering and Persson's call for "an iterative, dialogical process" to set targets [biermann/2020_boundaries-of-pb-framework] [pickering/2019_democratising-planetary-boundaries].

The IPCC works differently. Governments and observer bodies nominate experts, and drafts pass expert and then government review [ipcc/2026_preparing-reports]. A plenary of governments approves the summary "line by line", with authors present [ipcc/2026_preparing-reports]. The IPCC assesses; governments set the 1.5°C goal in the Paris Agreement [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs]. For firms and cities, the Science Based Targets Network (founded 2019) turns Earth's limits into targets [sbtn/2026_who-we-are].

**For the Charter:** "Follows the science" hides a choice: where to set a limit inside the zone of increasing risk. Part VII.4 gives that choice to all terrans; Parts III and XI.4 give it to "the science". The draft should pick, or split the job. The PIK partner's name, "Planetary Guardians", could be confused with the Charter's Guardians (Part X).

## Tensions and open problems

- **Outdated floor.** It is two versions behind Raworth and drops connectivity and peace and justice unexplained. Part XI.4 makes fixes look like "lowering".
- **Resilience has no measure.** Raworth had no data for it in 2012 and dropped it afterwards [raworth/2025_evolving-doughnut].
- **Ceiling labels.** "Freshwater use" is retired and its verdict flips. Three other labels narrow or shift meaning.
- **"Not negotiable."** The physics is fixed; the boundary values are precautionary judgements [rockstrom/2009_planetary-boundaries-ecology-society].
- **A moving ceiling with no owner.** Values changed in 2025 and 2026. Two expert groups use different nitrogen measures. Parts III and XI.4 conflict with VII.4.
- **Already past the limits.** With seven boundaries crossed, the no-transgression rule cannot be met literally. It needs shares and timelines.
- **The count cannot be computed.** No data count people inside on every item. Per-person footprints would clash with Part VII.6.
- **Feasibility is conditional.** It needs deep cuts at the top. The just climate limit is already exceeded [rockstrom/2023_safe-and-just-esbs].
- **The doughnut test.** A small firm cannot measure its effect on a global headcount.
- **Legitimacy.** Boundary-setting is expert-led, with weak Global South support [biermann/2020_boundaries-of-pb-framework].
- **Preamble wording.** "Toward collapse" outruns the sources, which speak of rising risk of irreversible change [pik/2026_phc-press-release].

## Options for the next draft

- Adopt Raworth's 2025 list, or keep the Charter's and give reasons for each difference. Consider adding mobility and connectivity.
- Move both lists into a revisable schedule. Items may be renamed or merged; the needs covered may not shrink.
- Use official process names with plain glosses, e.g. "Freshwater change (rivers and soil moisture)".
- Split the job. Scientists publish each boundary and high-risk line. Terrans set the operative limit between them, defaulting to the boundary.
- Create an IPCC-style panel: experts nominated by scientific bodies, lay reviewers drawn by lot, open review. An Assembly approves the summary line by line. Part X's Guardians could host it.
- Replace the no-transgression rule with fair shares: each actor moves toward an allocated share on a published timeline. Name the sharing principle.
- Adopt the Earth Commission's "stricter limit governs" rule. Add a duty to protect and compensate people harmed while just limits are breached.
- Report two headlines: the share of terrans above every floor item, and distance to each boundary. Report footprints by income group, not by person.
- Tier the doughnut test. Large bodies publish portraits; firms meet design tests [deal/2026_about-doughnut-economics] and science-based targets [sbtn/2026_who-we-are].
- Add a line on growth: output is a means, judged by the doughnut; poorer regions may need more, richer ones less.
