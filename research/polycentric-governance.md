# Polycentric and non-territorial government

*Research note · planetary government, round 2 · first pass, 11 October 2026 · compiled by Claude, not yet reviewed by Kara*

Consolidation rarely saves money and costs citizens their sense of voice; but piling up overlapping special-purpose bodies raises taxes and dodges oversight. The best planetary precedents decide few things, take scientific or engineering advice, and use double-majority votes or verifiable lotteries rather than universal ballots. Where one member can block appointments, as at the WTO since 2019, the system stalls. Personal-jurisdiction bodies work for culture and schooling but fail on turnout quorums, citizenship limits and gameable registers.

## What the Charter says now

The Commonwealth is defined "by shared commitments, not by territory or borders" (I.4). Terrans may form communities around any vision and leave any of them (VI.2–3). Every terran votes on the questions before the Commonwealth (VII.3). Decisions sit "as close as possible to the people they affect", and planetary matters, "including the ceiling", go to all terrans (VII.4). A lot-drawn assembly writes each ballot (VIII.3), an elected Council of Stewards administers (VIII.7), and no body holds two powers (VIII.8). Community courts sit under a Planetary Court (IX.1). Nations end as "self-governing communities" (XII.4).

The first round found no list of planetary matters and no umpire for VII.4 ([deliberation](deliberation.md)). On 11 October 2026 Kara set universal voting on every question aside as "probably an impossible requirement".

## Polycentric or consolidated?

- Ostrom, Tiebout and Warren (1961) attacked the "Gargantua" ideal of one metropolitan government [ostrom-tiebout-warren/1961_organization-of-government-metropolitan-areas]. They argued that "various scales of organization may be appropriate for different public services" [ostrom-tiebout-warren/1961_organization-of-government-metropolitan-areas].
- In the 1970s Elinor Ostrom's team studied police in 80 US metropolitan areas with 1,159 direct-service producers [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, p. 51]. Small and medium departments performed better, often at lower cost, than large ones serving similar neighbourhoods [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, p. 51]. Few produced their own crime labs or training; larger agencies supplied them [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, p. 51].
- The most consolidated areas needed one-third more officers to put the same number on patrol [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, p. 52]. Duplication rarely occurred, and areas with a few producers of indirect services, such as radio and crime labs, were more efficient [ostrom/2009_beyond-markets-and-states, pp. 411–412]. These are observational studies by the theory's own authors.
- Denmark's 2007 merger reform gives cleaner evidence. Net savings were null: gains in some services were offset by losses in others [blom-hansen/2016_jurisdiction-size-expenditure]. Size had "a causal and sizeable detrimental effect" on citizens' confidence that they can take part in politics [lassen-serritzlew/2011_jurisdiction-size-efficacy].
- Reviews agree. Merger savings are mostly in general administration, and local democracy suffers [tavares/2018_municipal-amalgamations-review]. A 2026 review finds amalgamation "often fails to achieve economies of scale" [zheng-sun-de-witte/2026_do-municipal-amalgamations-work].
- Fragmentation has its own costs. US counties with more overlapping governments have larger public sectors; the overlap effect is about 10% of local revenue [berry/2008_piling-on]. More school districts mean more racial segregation between districts [bischoff/2008_school-district-fragmentation].
- Ferguson is one of 89 municipalities in St. Louis County [us-doj/2015_ferguson-police-department, p. 6]. The St. Louis area was one of Ostrom's police study sites [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, p. 50]. Ferguson's 2015 budget expected fines and fees to bring $3.09 million of $13.26 million general revenue, about 23% (own calculation) [us-doj/2015_ferguson-police-department, p. 10]. The Justice Department found its policing "shaped by the City's focus on revenue rather than by public safety needs" [us-doj/2015_ferguson-police-department, p. 2].
- Ostrom herself lists the failure modes of action below the global scale: leakage, inconsistent policies, free riding and weak certification [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, pp. 29–30].

**Design implication:** Do not centralise to save money, because mergers rarely do. Do pool back-office services at larger scales, and protect small units from predatory revenue and sorting with floors, equalisation and appeals.

## Functional, overlapping jurisdictions

Frey and Eichenberger's FOCJ are functional, overlapping and competing jurisdictions with the power to tax [frey/2009_new-concept-european-federalism, p. 9]. Members may exit freely or use initiatives and referenda; entry may carry a price [frey/2009_new-concept-european-federalism, pp. 10–11]. To limit voting load, Frey would bundle votes on "three or four dates per year" [frey/2009_new-concept-european-federalism, p. 13]. I could not open their 1999 book [frey-eichenberger/1999_new-democratic-federalism-for-europe].

**Design implication:** Functional bodies deliver scale without merging communities, but they multiply, overspend and slip from view. Give each its own revenue, a shared election day, a sunset date and an overseer able to dissolve it.

## Personal (non-territorial) jurisdiction

| Case | Who belongs | Powers and money | What went wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Estonia, 1925 law re-enacted 1993 | Citizens only. German, Russian, Swedish and Jewish minorities, or any over 3,000. Members self-register on lists kept by cultural associations [estonia/1993_national-minorities-cultural-autonomy-act] | Mother-tongue schools, cultural bodies, grants; state and local funds plus member levies (§§5, 27) [estonia/1993_national-minorities-cultural-autonomy-act] | Councils count only if over half the roll votes (§19). The Finns' autonomy closed after its 11 October 2023 vote missed that bar [err/2023_finns-cultural-autonomy-closed] |
| Ottoman millets | Religious communities under their own leaders [britannica/2026_millet] | Own law and councils; leaders answered for taxes and order [britannica/2026_millet] | Built by "ad-hoc negotiations", it kept minorities "under the control of the state" [barkey-gavrilis/2015_ottoman-millet-system]. Reforms from 1856 eroded it [britannica/2026_millet] |
| Belgium's language communities | Territorial, except in Brussels, where community law reaches institutions that belong to one community (Art. 127 §2) [belgium/2021_constitution-english] | Culture, education and "person-related matters" (Arts. 127–128) [belgium/2021_constitution-english] | Amid Flemish–Walloon tensions, forming a federal government took 541 days in 2010–11, with no 2011 budget [wikipedia/2026_belgian-government-formation-2010-2011] |
| Sámi parliaments | Norway: self-declaration plus Sámi as a home language within three generations, or a parent on the roll (§2-6) [norway/1987_sami-act] | Matters it judges to affect the Sámi; may manage state grants (§2-1) [norway/1987_sami-act] | Finland's roll was disputed until the UN Human Rights Committee found a violation in 2019; a June 2025 reform passed 150–27 [idea/2025_finland-sami-parliament-act] |
| Hungary's nationality self-governments | 13 nationalities; voters self-declare onto a register; local bodies only where the census shows 30 members [venice-commission/2012_opinion-hungary-nationalities-act] | "Strategic partners" of central and local authorities [venice-commission/2012_opinion-hungary-nationalities-act] | In 2002 four of the five members of Jászladány's Roma self-government were non-Roma [errc/2004_jaszladany] |

- **Registers are the hinge.** Norway may record roll membership in the population register, visible only to the election authority [norway/1987_sami-act]. Registration is continuous: 6,100 of 2025's voters were not on the 2021 roll [ssb/2025_sametingsvalg-valgdeltakelse]. The 2021 roll held 20,500 voters [ssb/2025_sametingets-valgmanntall]. Turnout in 2025 was 73.5%, the highest since 1993 [ssb/2025_sametingsvalg-valgdeltakelse].
- **Finland moved roll disputes to an outside body.** The 2025 reform created an independent appeals board for roll decisions [idea/2025_finland-sami-parliament-act]. It was in force by February 2026 [samediggi-fi/2026_reformed-act-celebrated].
- **Quorums kill small bodies.** The Estonian Finns' council chair called the bodies "municipalities without municipality borders" [err/2023_finnish-cultural-autonomy-bleak]. He called the 50% bar too high and three-yearly postal elections a burden [err/2023_finnish-cultural-autonomy-bleak]. ERR calls the status "merely symbolic or declarative" [err/2023_finns-cultural-autonomy-closed]. It links the ministry's refusal to amend partly to the 24% Russian minority [err/2023_finns-cultural-autonomy-closed].
- **Loose registers invite gaming.** For the 2024 elections the Hungarian Helsinki Committee counted 230,000 registered Roma voters, against 210,000 Roma in the 2022 census [hhc/2024_franet-elections-reply]. Hungary's Act requires candidates to speak the language but names no one to check [venice-commission/2012_opinion-hungary-nationalities-act]. An NGO told the OSCE that registered voters lose their party-list vote in national elections [ferkovics/2015_roma-political-representation].

**Design implication:** Personal jurisdiction suits culture, language and schooling; no case found used it for land, water or air. It needs self-declared registers with a light objective test, private membership data, independent appeals and no turnout quorum.

## Bottom-up confederations

**Switzerland.** Communal autonomy is guaranteed "in accordance with cantonal law" (Art. 50) [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution]. Whoever benefits from a service pays, and whoever pays decides (Art. 43a) [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution]. Cantons may run joint bodies (Art. 48) [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution]. The federation may compel participation in nine fields, such as waste water and urban transport (Art. 48a) [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution]. Equalisation guarantees cantons minimum resources (Art. 135) [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution].

Communes fell from 2,899 in 2000 to 2,110 in October 2026, a drop of 27% [bfs/2026_commune-register-snapshots]. The median commune has 1,693 residents; 65% draw equalisation money; 17% say they have reached their limits [zhaw/2024_nationales-gemeindemonitoring]. Merged communes report weaker identification and participation, and more debt [zhaw/2024_nationales-gemeindemonitoring]. The volunteer model strains: presidents give 18.7 hours a week, other executive members 9.9, and 63% say the time load is rising [zhaw/2024_nationales-gemeindemonitoring]. Turnout for communal executives averages 44% [zhaw/2024_nationales-gemeindemonitoring]. There are 12,421 executive seats, one per 725 residents (own calculation) [zhaw/2024_nationales-gemeindemonitoring] [un-desa/2024_world-population-prospects]. At that ratio humanity would need about 11 million local executives (own estimate).

**North and East Syria.** The 2023 Social Contract made the commune "the basic grassroots organization form of direct democracy" (Art. 75) [aanes/2023_social-contract]. Every field had co-chairs of both sexes (Art. 24), and residents could join more than one commune (Art. 31) [aanes/2023_social-contract]. Crisis Group found in 2017 that a few PKK-trained cadres decided budgets and appointments behind closed doors [crisis-group/2017_pkk-fateful-choice]. It called the arrangement "one-party" [crisis-group/2017_pkk-fateful-choice]. A government offensive took Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor in January 2026 [wikipedia/2026_daanes]. On 25 August 2026 the SDF's commander announced the dissolution of the SDF and the Autonomous Administration [enab-baladi/2026_sdf-dissolution]. I found no independent evaluation of the communes I could verify.

**Mondragon.** Each member has one vote in a cooperative's assembly, and each cooperative sits in the Cooperative Congress [kasmir/2016_mondragon-successes-challenges]. Members paid 10% of profits into a common fund [co-op-news/2023_ulma-orona-leave-mondragon]. In December 2022 Ulma and Orona, with 11,000 of 80,000 workers, voted to leave for more autonomy [co-op-news/2023_ulma-orona-leave-mondragon]. In 2013 the General Council refused Fagor's €170 million request as "not viable" [geo/2013_mondragon-cooperative-faltering].

**Bookchin.** "Policy is made by a community or neighborhood assembly of free citizens; administration is performed by confederal councils composed of mandated, recallable deputies" [bookchin/1991_libertarian-municipalism]. A confederal majority may stop a member's rights abuses or "ecological mayhem" [bookchin/1991_libertarian-municipalism]. I found no polity that has run on it at scale.

**Design implication:** Local autonomy scales inside compulsory frames: benefit-pays rules, equalisation and mandatory cooperation in listed fields. Voluntary solidarity breaks when strong members can leave, and assemblies drift to whoever organises full time (inference from Mondragon and Syria).

## Functional global governance without a world state

| WTO dispute settlement | Panel reports adopted unless appealed; appointments need consensus [wto/1994_dispute-settlement-understanding] | Appeals body below quorum since 10 December 2019 [wto/2019_dsb-18dec19]; empty since 30 November 2020 [wto/2026_appellate-body] |

- **Montreal binds fast and pays for compliance.** Adjustments bind all parties six months after circulation [unep/1987_montreal-protocol-article-2]. Richer parties fund developing countries' "agreed incremental costs" on the UN scale [unep/1987_montreal-protocol-article-10]. The fund has granted $4.3 billion to 10,198 projects in 144 countries [mlf/2026_home]. Its 2024–26 budget is $965 million [mlf/2026_about], about 4 US cents per terran a year (own estimate) [un-desa/2024_world-population-prospects]. The Kigali amendment on HFCs has 175 parties [untc/2026_kigali-amendment-status].
- **Monitoring catches cheats.** CFC-11 emissions rose about 25% after 2012 from unreported production [montzka/2018_cfc-11-increase]. They fell 26% from 2018 to 2019 [montzka/2021_cfc-11-decline].
- **The IETF lottery resists capture.** The draw uses public random data unknown in advance, anyone can verify it, and no more than two members may share an employer [ietf/2020_rfc-8713]. Twenty qualified participants can petition to recall a leader [ietf/2020_rfc-8713]. Against 300 genuine volunteers, an attacker needs about 365 of its own for an even chance of a majority [ietf/2023_rfc-9389]. Counting online attendance cut the cost of eligibility from three trips to no money at all [ietf/2023_rfc-9389]. RFC 7282 admits many decisions "are now indistinguishable from voting" [ietf/2014_rfc-7282].
- **One veto stalls the whole.** On 26 September 2025, 130 members proposed starting appointments for the 90th time; the US again declined [wto/2025_dsb-26sep25]. Sixty-one members use a stopgap appeal arbitration that had settled two appeals by March 2026 [wto/2026_mc14-mpia]. Reform talks continued on 5 October 2026 [wto/2026_general-council-5oct26].
- **Redundancy works where tasks allow it.** On 11 October 2026 the DNS root ran on 2,047 instances, operated by 12 independent organizations [root-servers/2026_root-server-system]. IANA says only "a limited set" of Internet elements needs global coordination [iana/2026_about].

**Design implication:** Planetary functional bodies are efficient when scope is narrow, advice is scientific or technical, compliance is funded and fallback votes need two majorities. They fail when one member can block appointments or a bare majority overrides strong dissent.

## Design patterns that work

Following Kara's steer, patterns using modern capabilities come first. Open-source governance, rules as code and cybernetic feedback belong to the seventh strand.

- **Verifiable sortition for appointments and oversight.** The IETF draws its nominating committee by a public, repeatable lottery with employer caps and recall by petition [ietf/2020_rfc-8713]. *Needs* a large eligible pool and an unpredictable public seed. *Fails* when pools are small, self-selected or cheap to flood [ietf/2023_rfc-9389].
- **Continuous monitoring tied to rule changes.** Montreal pairs science assessments with binding adjustments [unep/1987_montreal-protocol-article-2], and measurement exposed hidden CFC-11 production [montzka/2018_cfc-11-increase]. *Needs* a measurable target and independent monitors. *Fails* where outcomes cannot be measured or attributed.
- **Open registries with published performance.** IANA publishes its registries and monthly performance against service levels [iana/2026_about]. *Needs* a narrow mandate set by others.
- **Funded experiments at many scales.** Ostrom: "without trial and error, learning cannot occur" [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, p. 31]. *Needs* money for monitoring and evaluation [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, p. 31]. *Fails* through leakage, clashing rules and free riding [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, pp. 29–30].
- **Small units for direct services, large ones for shared services** [ostrom/2009_polycentric-approach-climate-change, pp. 51–52]. *Fails* when small units live on fines [us-doj/2015_ferguson-police-department] or sort people by wealth and race [bischoff/2008_school-district-fragmentation].
- **Benefit-pays rules, equalisation and compulsory cooperation in listed fields** [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution]. *Fails* through bargaining costs and boards captured by their service [zurich/2008_kr-378-2007-intercommunal-cooperation].
- **Paying the cost of compliance** [unep/1987_montreal-protocol-article-10] [mlf/2026_home]. *Fails* if donors stop paying, which was not tested here.
- **Double-majority fallback after consensus fails** [unep/1987_montreal-protocol-article-2]. *Fails* as a bare majority: Codex's 69–67 vote left major members with reservations [usda/2012_codex-35th-session].
- **Shared election days.** Water-board turnout roughly doubled once votes joined provincial elections [tweede-kamer/2014_kst-33719-15] [kiesraad/2026_verkiezingsuitslagen-waterschap]. *Fails* to match general turnout: 53.61% against 58.64% on the same day in 2023 [kiesraad/2026_verkiezingsuitslagen-waterschap].
- **Sunset and dissolution review.** California's overseers were "not uniformly effective" at dissolving districts [little-hoover/2017_special-districts-oversight]. This shows the gap, not a tested cure.
- **Self-declared registers with objective tests and outside appeals** [norway/1987_sami-act] [idea/2025_finland-sami-parliament-act]. *Fails* with turnout quorums [err/2023_finns-cultural-autonomy-closed] or unchecked rolls [hhc/2024_franet-elections-reply].
- **Independent operators for critical systems** [root-servers/2026_root-server-system]. *Needs* a task that can be replicated.

## Tensions and open problems

- **Exit against solidarity.** FOCJ depend on easy exit [frey/2009_new-concept-european-federalism, p. 10], but exit lets strong members escape transfers, as at Mondragon [co-op-news/2023_ulma-orona-leave-mondragon].
- **Voice against cost.** Bigger units weaken citizens' confidence [lassen-serritzlew/2011_jurisdiction-size-efficacy]; more overlapping units raise revenue [berry/2008_piling-on].
- **Attention.** A Zurich commune sat in about eight purpose bodies [zurich/2008_kr-378-2007-intercommunal-cooperation]. Members cannot vote on all of them, so delegation or sortition must carry the load (inference).
- **Who volunteers.** IETF's lottery draws from self-selected insiders [ietf/2023_rfc-9389]; a planetary pool would face the skews the first round found.
- **Territory still matters.** Water, air and land are territorial, which strains I.4's Commonwealth "not by territory".
- **Who umpires overlaps?** I found no court that resolves disputes between functional bodies at planetary scale; the WTO's appeals body stalled.
- **Capture by the organised.** Syria's communes coexisted with cadre rule [crisis-group/2017_pkk-fateful-choice], and Hungary's rolls were gamed [errc/2004_jaszladany].
- **Not verified:** the 1999 FOCJ book; water-board outcome data; independent studies of Syria's communes; ICANN's 2000 turnout; Estonia's act as amended from 1 September 2025.

## Options for the next draft

- **Replace VII.3's universal vote** with a schedule of planetary functions, each run by a functional body. Keep all-terran votes for Charter amendments (XI.3) and recalls.
- **List those functions in VII.4.** Candidates: ceiling budgets per boundary, WMD verification (XII.2), identifiers and standards, floor equalisation, and disputes between communities. Leave the rest to communities.
- **Make the ceiling Montreal-style (III, X.4).** A science panel reports every four years. Adjustments pass by consensus, or else two-thirds plus majorities of high- and low-footprint communities, binding after six months.
- **Pair each cap with a compliance fund** paying agreed incremental costs, financed on an ability-to-pay scale (IV.9, XII.3).
- **Pick Stewards and functional boards by lot-based nomination (VIII.7).** A lot-drawn committee from a broad pool, with a public seed, affiliation caps and recall petitions, replaces a planetary election.
- **Add Swiss rules to VII.4:** whoever benefits pays and decides; equalisation guarantees a minimum; the Commonwealth may compel cooperation in listed fields.
- **Give every functional body a sunset date** and a dissolution review by an independent auditor.
- **Hold all votes on three or four fixed days a year.**
- **Define personal communities in I.4 and VI.2:** culture, language and schooling; self-declared registers with an objective test; private data; outside appeals; no turnout quorum.
- **Protect appointments (VIII.7, IX.2)** with a default rule that fills seats if the appointing body deadlocks.
- **Require several independent operators** for identity, ledgers and voting systems (VII.2, VII.5, VIII.6).

### Design sketch: a polycentric Terran Commonwealth

- **What nests.** Person, then territorial commune for direct services, then regional federations for watersheds, grids and transport, then a few planetary functional bodies. Each level holds listed tasks; residual powers stay below.
- **What overlaps.** Functional districts cross commune lines, as water boards do. Personal communities of faith, language and culture overlap all territory and run schools and culture by register. None governs land, water or air.
- **How it decides.** Communes use assemblies or councils. Functional bodies use expert drafting, lot-drawn oversight panels and consensus with double-majority fallbacks.
- **How disputes are resolved.** Cheap local arenas first, as Ostrom's design principles advise [ostrom/2009_beyond-markets-and-states, p. 422]; regional courts next; the Planetary Court only for conflicts between bodies and Charter breaches. No single member can block an appointment.
- **What it costs.** Mergers save little [blom-hansen/2016_jurisdiction-size-expenditure], while overlap adds about 10% to revenue [berry/2008_piling-on]. Dutch water boards levy about €250 per resident a year (own estimate) [cbs/2026_opbrengsten-waterschapsheffingen]. Planetary compliance funding can be cheap: Montreal's fund costs about 4 US cents per terran a year (own estimate) [mlf/2026_about].
