# One people, no nations: the Commonwealth and the transition

*Research note · Charter Parts I and XII, and the "Transition" open question · first pass, 10 October 2026 · compiled by Claude, not yet reviewed by Kara*

The Charter's stage triggers copy the Earth Constitution's population thresholds of 50% and 90%. They drop its matching count of nations and its referendum rules [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]. On UN figures for mid-2026, stage three could begin while five nuclear-armed states, with 9.5% of humanity, stay outside [un-desa/2024_world-population-prospects] [aca/2026_nuclear-who-has-what]. The "speed of a horse" claim in I.2 runs against mainstream scholarship, which ties nations to print and literacy [miller/2025_sep-nationalism]. Authority that survives leaving works in ICC law, but trials depend on arrests by states [icc/2026_duterte-case].

## What the Charter says

Part I makes every person a terran "from birth" and says no one can grant or revoke that standing (I.1). It calls nations obsolete because communication is now instant (I.2). The Commonwealth is defined by shared commitments, "not by territory or borders" (I.4).

Part XII sets a voluntary, staged path. Individuals may take up digital citizenship from anywhere; communities, cities, organizations and nations join by adopting the Charter (XII.1). Joining nations hand over weapons of mass destruction (WMD), commit to the ceiling and accept the Planetary Court (XII.2). When "half of humanity has joined", the Commonwealth takes on the floor, the wealth limit and planetary revenue (XII.3). At nine in ten, national armies are dismantled and nations become communities (XII.4). Members may leave until stage three; the Court keeps authority over acts done during membership (XII.5). Growth is "never by force" (XII.6).

## The Constitution for the Federation of Earth

**History.** A World Constituent Assembly adopted it at Innsbruck in June 1977 and amended it at Troia, Portugal, in May 1991 [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]. Its sponsor began as a US committee for a world constitutional convention in 1958. It became the World Constitution and Parliament Association (WCPA) in 1966, with Philip Isely as Secretary-General [wikipedia/2026_wcpa]. That article carries a neutrality warning dated August 2026.

**Structure** [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]:

- A World Parliament of three houses. The House of Peoples has one delegate per district, up to 1,000 districts. The House of Nations has one to three per nation; the House of Counsellors has 200 (Arts. 2.4, 5).
- A World Executive: a five-member Presidium with a yearly rotating president, and an Executive Cabinet of 20–30 (Art. 6). Parliament elects both. The executive has no veto and cannot dissolve parliament (6.6).
- A World Ombudsmus, led by five World Ombudsmen. It guards rights against officials and may start court cases itself (Art. 11).
- An Enforcement System that applies world law to individuals, officials included (10.1.1). Arrests need warrants (10.3.4). Art. 10.3.5: "World Police shall be armed only with weapons appropriate for the apprehension of the individuals responsible for violation of world law."

**Stages and disarmament** [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]:

| Stage | Trigger | Military consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Provisional (Art. 19) | WCPA convenes a Provisional World Parliament | Compliance is voluntary |
| First operative (17.3) | 25 nations over 100,000 people; or 10 nations plus 50 districts; or 100 districts | Members hand all WMD to a World Disarmament Agency and pay half their military savings to the world treasury |
| Second operative (17.4) | 50% of nations, covering 50% of world population | Joining nations surrender all weapons; member forces "completely disarmed" |
| Full operative (17.5) | 80% of nations with 90% of population, or 90% of population | All armies disbanded or turned into a non-military World Service Corps |

Three rules have no Charter equivalent. Ratification by referendum needs a majority with 25% turnout (17.1.03). Rival states may ratify as pairs (17.1.09). Every ratifier is bound never to use force or WMD against another member (17.1.10). No clause lets a member nation leave. Once the Federation covers 90% of Earth's territory, individuals and groups may leave. They go to a disarmed zone of at most 5% of habitable land (14.1.4).

Differs from the drafting-session research: it says the Earth Constitution dismantles national militaries "only at the final stage". The text disarms members' forces during the second stage (17.4.06); only non-members wait for the full stage [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution].

**Adoption.** No state has ratified; the WCPA's "Member Nations" table is empty (updated 3 October 2026) [wcpa/2026_member-nations]. A Provisional World Parliament of unofficial delegates has met irregularly since 1982. Wikipedia lists 15 sessions to 2021, which adopted 72 acts binding no state [wikipedia/2026_provisional-world-parliament]. It also lists a 16th session in Pondicherry in December 2025. The WCPA's site says no official notice of a 16th session exists and calls such claims fraudulent [wcpa/2026_pwp-16th-session].

**For the Charter:** The Charter takes the population thresholds almost exactly but drops the safeguards around them. It is more cautious about armies, which members keep until 90%. It is more radical about nations, which the Earth Constitution keeps. Forty-nine years without one ratifying state is the main warning: the first states need a reason to move.

## World federalism and its critics

- **World Federalist Movement.** Its 1947 Montreux declaration called for world law enforced on individuals, "supranational armed forces", and member disarmament to policing levels [wfm/1947_montreux-declaration].
- **Clark and Sohn (1958).** They proposed an Assembly representative of the world's peoples, and an Executive Council replacing the vetoed Security Council [baratta/2023_clark-sohn-plan]. A 10-year staged disarmament would leave a World Peace Force as the only armed force. I could not open the book; the Internet Archive copy is restricted [clark-sohn/1958_world-peace-through-world-law].
- **Garry Davis.** He renounced US citizenship in Paris on 25 May 1948 and declared himself a "citizen of the world" [wsa/2024_our-history]. In January 1954 he founded the World Service Authority to issue World Passports. By its own account he was imprisoned 16 times for lacking "valid" papers. Wikipedia reports that ICAO and the EU Council class the passport as a fantasy document [wikipedia/2026_world-passport]. Davis died in 2013, aged 91 [ap/2013_garry-davis-dies].
- **Kant (1795).** "The law of nations shall be founded on a federation of free states" [kant/1795_perpetual-peace]. Under a universal monarchy, "soulless despotism, when it has choked the seeds of good, at last sinks into anarchy." Yet reason, Kant says, points to a "State of nations" of all peoples. Since states refuse it, a federation is its "negative substitute".
- **Wendt (2003).** He argues a world state is inevitable: "My guess is that a world state will emerge within 100 years" [wendt/2003_world-state-inevitable]. His world state need not end local autonomy and could keep national armies under common command. He argues that a looser union cannot stop a member seceding and rearming.

**For the Charter:** The Charter answers Kant partly: local decisions (VII.4), free movement (VIII.10), an Ombudsman, and exit until stage three. After stage three it removes his two safeguards, rival states and an outside to go to. Unlike Montreux and Clark–Sohn, it has no world force. That avoids a planetary army but leaves Wendt's problem of members who leave and rearm.

Adds to the drafting-session research: Kant's text is more mixed than a flat rejection. He calls a world republic right in principle but unreachable while states refuse it [kant/1795_perpetual-peace].

## "News moved at the speed of a horse" (I.2)

- The mainstream "modernist" view holds that nations are modern. For Gellner (1983) they are products of modernization and industrialization; Hobsbawm (1990) is another modernist work [miscevic/2025_sep-nationalism-archived]. I reached only the publishers' pages, not the books [gellner/1983_nations-and-nationalism] [hobsbawm/1990_nations-and-nationalism].
- Miller (2025): "nations could not exist without widespread literacy and the communication media, originally the printed word" [miller/2025_sep-nationalism]. Hence Anderson's "imagined communities" [anderson/1983_imagined-communities]. A dissenting view stresses nations' deep historical roots; on that view I.2 is half right.
- The number of states tripled long after the telegraph and radio. UN membership grew from 51 in 1945 to 154 in 1980 and 193 in 2011 [un/2011_growth-in-membership].
- Identity lags technology. In a 2016 poll in 18 countries, 51% saw themselves more as global citizens than national ones [globescan/2016_global-citizenship-poll]. The figure was 42% in seven OECD countries and 30% in Germany.

**For the Charter:** I.2 inverts the scholarship: faster communication built nations. Wording that survives scrutiny: "Nations grew with the printing press and mass literacy, which taught strangers who would never meet to see themselves as one people. Today's networks reach every terran, so that bond can now span the planet." "Has made us one people" should become "can make us one people".

## Standing that no one can revoke (I.1)

- The UDHR says: "No one shall be arbitrarily deprived of his nationality" [un/1948_udhr]. The Charter bars any revocation, arbitrary or not.
- UNHCR counted 4.5 million stateless people at end-2025, up 3% in a year [unhcr/2026_global-trends-2025]. "The true number is likely to be much higher"; only 100 countries report data.
- UK law lets the Home Secretary strip citizenship when "conducive to the public good" (British Nationality Act 1981, s. 40(2)) [uk/1981_british-nationality-act-s40]. For naturalised citizens judged to have seriously harmed vital UK interests, statelessness is no bar if the Home Secretary believes they could gain another nationality (s. 40(4A)). Such orders rose from 14 in 2016 to 104 in 2017 [hm-government/2018_transparency-report].
- On 30 June 2026 the US Supreme Court held, in Trump v. Barbara, that US-born children of temporary or unlawful residents are citizens [crs/2026_trump-v-barbara]. Birthright standing held, but only after a fight in the highest court.
- A state can also close a digital status. In March 2022 Estonia stopped e-Residency applications from Russians and Belarusians, ended pending ones, and put existing holders under extra scrutiny [err/2022_e-residency-russia-belarus].

**For the Charter:** "No one can revoke it" is a moral claim the Commonwealth can honour only inside its own system. Its voting identity still needs rules for fraud, duplicates and death that never amount to revocation. Principle IV denies "the Charter's protection" to those who take others' freedom. That reads as partial forfeiture and should be squared with I.1.

## Joining from anywhere (XII.1)

- **e-Residency** is a government-issued digital identity for non-residents, mainly for running an EU company online [estonia/2026_what-is-e-residency]. It "does not confer citizenship, tax residency, physical residency or right of entry" and is not a travel document. Estonia counted over 144,000 e-residents at 22 September 2026, about 0.002% of humanity [estonia/2026_e-residency-dashboard].
- **Bitnation**, a blockchain "virtual nation", had nearly 4,000 citizens in 2016 [souli/2016_bitnation]. Its founder: "We don't need to improve existing nation-states, we need to make them redundant." Its old domain now hosts a crypto price-prediction and review site, not the platform [bitnation-co/2026_domain-snapshot].
- **Network states.** Srinivasan's model is "a highly aligned online community with a capacity for collective action that crowdfunds territory around the world" [srinivasan/2022_network-state]. It seeks recognition from existing states. It is selective and territorial; the Charter is universal and claims no territory.

**For the Charter:** e-Residency is a precedent for digital identity across borders, not for political membership. It works because Estonia stands behind it, and Estonia can close it. Early terran citizens would have no state behind them. Individual sign-ups at e-Residency's pace will never move a population threshold.

## Stage one: handing over weapons of mass destruction (XII.2)

- **TPNW.** In force since January 2021 [un/2017_tpnw]. At 10 October 2026 it had 75 parties; none of the nine nuclear-armed states takes part [untc/2026_tpnw-status] [aca/2026_nuclear-who-has-what]. Article 4 offers two paths: destroy, then join; or join, de-alert at once, then destroy under a time-bound plan. In 2022 the parties set a 10-year deadline, extendable by five years [sipri/2023_tpnw-first-msp]. No verifying authority need be named until a nuclear-armed state joins, so the parties named none; I did not check later status.
- **Chemical Weapons Convention.** The US destroyed its last declared chemical weapon on 7 July 2023 [opcw/2023_stockpiles-destroyed]. The OPCW has verified destruction of 72,304.34 tonnes since 1997. "Declared" matters: in May 2026 inspectors found undeclared chemical weapons in Syria [opcw/2026_syria-undeclared]. They included munitions of types used in the 2013 Ghouta and 2017 Khan Shaykhun attacks.
- **South Africa.** It decided to end its programme in November 1989 and joined the NPT on 10 July 1991 [iaea/1995_south-africa-verification]. Six finished devices and a partial seventh were destroyed. Weapons documents were destroyed on 17–23 March 1993, just before disclosure. The IAEA found "no indications to suggest that the initial inventory is incomplete".
- **Ukraine.** In the 1994 Budapest Memorandum, Russia, the UK and the US reaffirmed commitments to respect Ukraine's "existing borders" [un/1994_budapest-memorandum]. Beyond their own restraint, they promised only to consult, and to seek Security Council action if Ukraine faced aggression with nuclear weapons. In 2022 the General Assembly deplored Russia's aggression by 141 votes to 5 [un/2022_ga-es-11-1].
- **Libya.** It renounced WMD on 19 December 2003 [aca/2018_libya-chronology]. Gaddafi was killed in 2011, during civil war and a NATO-enforced no-fly zone. The new authorities then reported two chemical weapons stockpiles the old regime had not declared.

**For the Charter:** The CWC shows verified handovers can work at scale. The record also shows hidden stockpiles (Syria, Libya), destroyed records (South Africa) and failed assurances (Ukraine). XII.2 names no verifier, deadline or penalty. With exit allowed, a state could hand over weapons, leave and rebuild.

## Authority that survives leaving (XII.5)

- **Rome Statute.** Withdrawal takes effect after one year (Art. 127(1)) [icc/1998_rome-statute]. A state "shall not be discharged, by reason of its withdrawal, from the obligations" it had as a party (127(2)). Its duty to cooperate in investigations begun before the exit continues.
- **Burundi.** Its exit took effect on 27 October 2017; judges authorised an investigation two days earlier [icc/2026_burundi-situation]. The situation page lists no public case.
- **Philippines.** Its exit took effect on 17 March 2019, and the investigation was authorised in September 2021 [icc/2026_philippines-situation]. The Court kept jurisdiction over crimes during membership. It notes the crimes "appear to have continued" after the exit.
- **Duterte.** Philippine authorities arrested him and surrendered him to the Court on 12 March 2025 [icc/2026_duterte-case]. Judges confirmed three counts of crimes against humanity on 23 April 2026. Trial is set for 30 November 2026.
- **Withdrawals** [untc/2026_rome-statute-status]. The Statute has 125 parties. Hungary gave notice on 2 June 2025 but rescinded it on 29 May 2026. Six exits take effect in 2027: Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali, Venezuela, Chad and Nauru.

Differs from the drafting-session research: 125 members is still right, but six will leave between June and September 2027 unless they rescind [untc/2026_rome-statute-status]. Its line that surviving jurisdiction "blocks joining, then leaving to escape justice" holds for past acts only, not conduct after exit [icc/2026_philippines-situation].

**For the Charter:** XII.5 matches Article 127 in spirit. Practice suggests adding a notice period and a duty to cooperate that survives exit. Neither makes arrests happen: Duterte reached court only after his own state's police acted.

## Transition thresholds (open question)

| Rule | Threshold |
|---|---|
| US Constitution Art. VII (1787) | 9 of 13 states, binding only "the States so ratifying" [us/1787_constitution] |
| US Constitution Art. V | Amendments: three fourths of states [us/1787_constitution] |
| UN Charter Arts. 110 and 108 | Five named powers plus a majority of others; amendments need two thirds of members, including all permanent Council members [un/1945_un-charter] |
| Rome Statute Art. 126 | 60 ratifications [icc/1998_rome-statute] |
| Paris Agreement Art. 21 | 55 parties with at least 55% of global emissions [un/2015_paris-agreement] |
| TPNW Art. 15 | 50 ratifications [un/2017_tpnw] |
| EU qualified majority | 55% of states (at least 15 of 27) with 65% of population [ep/2026_council-factsheet] |
| Earth Constitution | 50% of nations and of population; then 90% of population, with or without 80% of nations [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution] |

**Population arithmetic** [un-desa/2024_world-population-prospects]. The UN projects 8,300.7 million people in mid-2026, so a tenth is 830 million. India and China together hold 34.8%. The seven largest states hold 51.1%, so stage two could start with seven members. The US, Pakistan, Russia, North Korea and Israel together hold 787.9 million, or 9.5%. All five are nuclear-armed, Israel undeclared [aca/2026_nuclear-who-has-what]. All five could stay outside at stage three.

**The counting ambiguity.** "Half of humanity has joined" could count digital citizens, residents of member nations and communities, or both. Counting both risks double counting. The Earth Constitution counts only residents of ratifying nations and districts, never individual sign-ups [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution].

**For the Charter:** Population-only triggers let a few large states decide for everyone. Comparators add a second key, such as states plus population, or name the states that must join. At stage three the risk is concrete: members disarm while nuclear-armed outsiders keep their arsenals.

## Tensions and open problems

- **I.2 is factually backwards.** Scholarship ties nations to print, literacy and industrialization, and the number of states tripled after 1945.
- **I.1 against Principle IV.** Irrevocable standing sits uneasily with losing "the Charter's protection".
- **I.4 against Part XII.** A Commonwealth "not by territory" is triggered by territorial nations. The Court needs a rule: personal jurisdiction, territorial, or both.
- **Stage one has weak incentives.** Early members disarm while neighbours stay armed; there is no paired joining or non-aggression pledge.
- **No verifier.** Syria, Libya and South Africa show that declarations alone are incomplete.
- **Exit and rearmament.** Before stage three a member can hand over weapons, leave and rebuild. Nothing delays exit during war.
- **Stage-three security gap.** Members disarm while states with 9.5% of humanity and nuclear arsenals could remain outside. Part XII says nothing about defending members.
- **"Half of humanity" is undefined**, and individual sign-ups barely register.
- **Risk to individuals.** People whose states ban foreign political allegiance would join at their own risk.
- **The precedent's record.** No state has ratified the Earth Constitution in 49 years, and its custodians dispute who convenes its parliament.

## Options for the next draft

On the "Transition" open question:

- **Define "joined".** Count residents of member nations and communities, plus digital citizens living elsewhere, without double counting.
- **Add a second key.** Stage two could need half of humanity and half of nations, as in Earth Constitution 17.4.01. An EU-style 55%/65% rule is an alternative.
- **Gate stage three on security.** Require every nuclear-armed state to join and finish a verified handover first. UN Charter Art. 110 shows how to name required states.
- **Gate every stage on verification.** An independent verifier certifies earlier commitments, followed by a fixed waiting period.
- **Or keep 50% and 90%, and justify them.** They match the Earth Constitution; most treaties start lower because they bind parties alone.
- **Add a stall review** if a stage is not reached by a set date, as Earth Constitution Art. 18.5 did.

On stage one and leaving:

- Adopt TPNW-style terms: de-alert on joining, and destroy within 10 years under a named verifier.
- Add the Earth Constitution's non-aggression pledge (17.1.10) and paired joining for rivals (17.1.09).
- Consider its rule that members pay half their military savings into a common treasury (17.3.09), linking disarmament to revenue.
- Make exit take a year's notice, as in Rome Art. 127 and TPNW Art. 17. Delay exit during armed conflict, as TPNW Art. 17(3) does.
- Keep cooperation duties after exit, never return handed-over weapons, and let individuals keep digital citizenship.

On Part I:

- Rewrite I.2 as suggested above.
- Reconcile I.1 with Principle IV: standing never lapses, even when a court limits particular protections.
- For the separate "Leaving after stage three" question, note Earth Constitution 14.1.4: exit to a disarmed zone of at most 5% of habitable land.
