# Research for the next draft

Oct 9, 2026 · @kara Zajac

## At a glance

The research mostly supports your direction, with two big cautions: remote online voting is not yet secure, and a world without exit needs much stronger checks on power.

| Open question | What the research suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Wealth limit | Pay-ratio limits work best as a steeply rising tax measured against the lowest-paid worker, contractors included. For wealth, a tax that rises steeply and reaches 100% at the limit. With no other country to flee to, the main risks left are valuing private companies, cash-poor founders, and hidden assets. |
| Business | Keep companies' legal standing so they can be sued and fined, but deny them political rights. Bar organizations from spending on votes, protect the press, and cap individuals too. |
| Direct digital votes | Digital ID works at national scale. Remote online voting cannot yet be secured, so votes need deliberation first, audits, and a non-digital fallback. |
| Transition and enforcement | No global body enforces on its own. What works is law run through courts plus the power to withhold money and benefits, with no vetoes. |
| Checks on power | With no other country to flee to, voice must be stronger: an independent ombudsman, free movement between communities, and rights no majority can override. |
| Guardians | They fail when advisory-only, underfunded, or appointed by those they check. They last with mandatory referral, automatic delay powers, court standing, and protected funding. |

## Wealth limits

Pay-ratio limits have mostly failed as hard caps, but can work as a steeply rising tax if the ratio counts every worker an organization depends on.

### Pay ratios today

- Large US companies paid CEOs about 220 to 325 times typical or median worker pay in 2025, depending on the measure ([EPI](https://www.epi.org/blog/ceo-pay-surged-in-2025-ceos-are-paid-325-times-as-much-as-the-typical-worker/), [AFL-CIO](https://aflcio.org/2026/8/13/12-things-you-need-know-afl-cios-2026-executive-paywatch-report), [Equilar](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/06/04/ceo-pay-trends-a-post-proxy-season-recap-2/)). In 1965 it was about 21 times.
- At least 20 US-listed companies reported ratios above 1,000:1 for 2025, including Starbucks at 1,794:1 and Coca-Cola at 1,739:1 ([AFL-CIO database](https://aflcio.org/paywatch/company-pay-ratios)).
- Ratios reach 1,000:1 two ways: a low-paid part-time or overseas median worker, or a one-off giant stock grant.

### What has been tried

| Rule | Limit | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mondragon cooperatives](https://www.thenews.coop/co-operative-approaches-to-pay/), Spain | 6:1 since 1988 (3:1 at founding in 1956) | Still in force; one sociologist reports about 9:1 in practice ([Kasmir](https://globaldialogue.isa-sociology.org/articles/the-mondragon-cooperatives-successes-and-challenges)) |
| [Swiss 1:12 vote](https://swissvotes.ch/vote/575.00/kurzbeschreibung-de.pdf), 2013 | 12:1 in every company | Rejected 65.3% to 34.7%, in every canton |
| [Israel's financial firms](https://boi.org.il/media/ijld4zdd/dp202407en.pdf), 2016 | 35:1 to the lowest-paid, outsourced staff included; no tax deduction above NIS 2.5M | Affected executives' pay fell about 27%; turnover rose; stock returns no worse |
| [France's state-owned firms](https://www.thelocal.fr/20130524/france-scraps-plan-to-limit-fat-cat-salaries) | 20:1, or €450,000 | Extension to private firms dropped in 2013 |
| [Portland, Oregon](https://efiles.portlandoregon.gov/record/16627461/file/document), since 2017 | Business tax +10% at 100:1, +25% at 250:1 | Raised $3.5–5.2M a year in 2017–19; no measured effect on pay ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/can-ceo-tax-strike-blow-inequality-portland-answer-elusive-n1248484)) |
| [San Francisco](https://sfstandard.com/2026/05/14/sf-overpaid-ceo-tax-city-report-economy/), since 2022 | 0.1–0.6% of gross receipts above 100:1 | About $140M a year at peak; cut 80% in 2025 ([SF Treasurer](https://sftreasurer.org/prop-m-2024-business-tax-reform)); an increase failed with 47.2% in June 2026 ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2026/06/sf-overpaid-ceo-tax-fails-union-prop-d/)) |
| [US Senate bill S. 2818](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119s2818is/html/BILLS-119s2818is.htm), 2025 | Corporate tax +0.5 to +5 points, from 50:1 to above 500:1 | Pending; tells the Treasury to stop firms shifting work to contractors |

### Lessons

- **Caps get loosened.** San Francisco, Ireland's bank cap, the UK's bonus cap and Ben & Jerry's all loosened or dropped their limits.
- **Ratios are easy to game.** Outsourcing, franchising, part-time hiring and offshoring lower the ratio without reducing inequality ([LSE](https://www.lse.ac.uk/News/Latest-news-from-LSE/2020/L-December/Outsourcing-low-paid-work-disguises-true-extent-of-growing-inequality-within-companies), [Edmans](https://www.london.edu/news/disclosing-ceo-worker-pay-gap-could-hurt-employees-1276)).
- **Teeth matter.** Israel's hard ratio plus lost tax deductions changed pay; small local surcharges did not.
- **Critics' strongest case:** a ratio punishes low-wage industries more than high-wage ones at the same CEO pay, and some studies link wide pay gaps to stronger firm performance.

### What this means for the Charter

- Measure against the lowest full-time-equivalent pay, counting contractors, franchise and outsourced workers.
- Measure the top as total pay, stock included, averaged over several years.
- A 1,000:1 line is far looser than any precedent: binding caps run 6:1 to 35:1, and tax schemes start at 50:1 to 100:1. At a lowest wage of $15,000–35,000, it would allow $15–35M a year.
- A graduated tax starting near 50:1 and rising steeply fits your softer-limits idea better than a single cutoff.

### Income and wealth taxes, and hard caps

Very high taxes on the rich have mostly been undone by avoidance and by the rich moving away. One planetary jurisdiction removes the moving away, but not the hard parts: valuing private companies, cash-poor founders, and hidden assets.

| Approach | Precedent | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Very high top income tax | US 91–94% above $200,000 for single filers, 1944–63 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-soi/02inpetr.pdf)); UK up to 98% in 1974–79 | Few people paid it, and loopholes kept real payments low. The UK's top 1% paid 11% of income tax in 1978/79, against about 29% now ([Tax Policy Associates](https://taxpolicy.org.uk/2025/05/08/tax-rich-1970s-loopholes/)) |
| Annual wealth tax | Norway 1–1.1%; Spain 0.16–3.5%, plus 1.7–3.5% above €3M; Swiss cantons ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/business/2025/09/29/wealth-taxes-in-europe-who-collects-them-and-how-much-do-they-raise)) | OECD countries with one fell from 12 in 1990 to 4 in 2017; survivors raise 0.2–1.2% of GDP |
| Billionaire minimum tax | Zucman's 2% on fortunes above $1B, proposed to the G20 in 2024 ([Zucman report](https://taxobservatory.world//www-site/uploads/2024/06/Zucman-Report-Executive-Summary-EN.pdf)) | Only a soft G20 pledge; France's Assembly rejected its version 228–172 in October 2025 ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.fr/20251101/french-lawmakers-reject-wealth-tax-as-budget-tensions-mount)) |
| Glide path to a hard cap | Huey Long's 1933 bill: 1% above $1M, rising 1 point per extra $1M to 100% above $100M ([Share Our Wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_Our_Wealth)) | Never enacted, but a working template |
| Hard cap | Ingrid Robeyns' *Limitarianism*: a €10M political limit and a €1M personal ethical limit ([Utrecht University](https://www.uu.nl/en/in-the-media/what-is-limitarianism-professor-ingrid-robeyns-explains)) | No modern democracy has enacted one |

**What the evidence shows**

- In Switzerland, a 1-point wealth-tax cut raises reported wealth by at least 43%. About a quarter is people moving; most of the rest looks like hidden assets ([Brülhart et al.](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.20200258)).
- In Spain, when the rich moved to zero-tax Madrid, the lost income tax was about six times the lost wealth tax ([Agrawal et al.](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/app.20220615)).
- In Norway, departures of the wealthy roughly doubled after 2022 ([Reuters](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/norways-wealth-tax-trades-millionaires-for-equality)), yet households there save more under the tax, not less ([Ring](https://www.restud.com/wealth-taxation-and-household-saving-evidence-from-assessment-discontinuities-in-norway/)).
- Critics' strongest case: a 2% wealth tax on a 5% return equals a 40% income tax, and on low-return assets the rate can pass 100% ([Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/eu/wealth-tax-impact/)). Founders rich on paper but short of cash may have to sell or shut down ([Presse-citron](https://www.presse-citron.net/les-startups-comme-mistral-ai-mettront-la-cle-sous-la-porte-le-prix-nobel-deconomie-tacle-la-taxe-zucman/)).

**What one planetary jurisdiction changes**

- Gone: emigration, tax havens, competition between countries, and the need for exit taxes.
- Still there: valuing private companies, cash-poor founders, effects on incentives, splitting wealth among relatives, and hiding unreported assets.

**What this means for the Charter**

- Use a glide path, not a cliff: rates that rise with wealth and reach 100% at the limit. That makes the limit both soft and hard.
- Reference points for where the limit sits: €1M (Robeyns' personal limit), €2.2M (what most Dutch survey respondents called more than enough, at 2018 prices), €10M (Robeyns' political limit), and $32–50M (where US wealth-tax plans began: [Sanders](https://www.wbur.org/news/2019/09/24/bernie-sanders-wealth-tax), [Warren](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2024/03/19/elizabeth-warren-others-reintroduce-ultra-millionaires-tax-3/)).
- Report every asset class to the tax system, and look through companies, trusts and family members.
- Let founders pay in shares to a commons fund, or defer with interest, instead of forcing fire sales.
- Cap the combined tax on people below the limit, so taxes never take more than their assets earn.

## Business

"Corporations are not people" works best as "organizations have no political rights": companies keep the legal standing that lets them be sued and fined, but cannot spend to sway votes.

### Where things stand

- [Citizens United v. FEC](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/08-205) (2010, 5–4) let corporations and unions spend without limit on elections. [SpeechNow.org v. FEC](https://www.fec.gov/legal-resources/court-cases/speechnoworg-v-fec) created super PACs two months later.
- Outside spending hit a record $4.5B in 2024 ([OpenSecrets](https://wausaupilotandreview.com/2025/05/31/outside-spending-on-2024-elections-shatters-records-fueled-by-billion-dollar-dark-money-infusion/)), and undisclosed dark money a record $1.9B ([Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/dark-money-hit-record-high-19-billion-2024-federal-races)).
- In [NRSC v. FEC](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/supreme-court-strikes-down-limits-on-political-party-coordinated-expenditures) (June 30, 2026), the Supreme Court also struck limits on party spending coordinated with candidates.
- A constitutional amendment is stalled. The live route is state laws denying corporations the power to spend politically: [Hawaii](https://dailymontanan.com/2026/06/17/hawaii-enacts-law-modeled-on-montana-plan/) passed one in May 2026, and [Montana's I-194](https://news.ballotpedia.org/?p=50534) is on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

### Three meanings of "not people"

| Meaning | What changes | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No legal personhood | Firms could not own property, sign contracts, or be sued | Breaks firms, unions and nonprofits, and makes them harder to fine or prosecute ([Hansmann & Kraakman](https://www.yalelawjournal.org/pdf/386_hxm176pg.pdf)) |
| No constitutional rights | Speech and property protections become revocable privileges ([Move to Amend](https://www.movetoamend.org/amendment)) | Also strips groups like the NAACP and newspapers of protection |
| No political rights | No spending to influence votes | Closest to what Canada and France do |

### How other democracies limit organizational money

- 47 of 181 countries ban corporate donations to parties ([International IDEA](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/question-view/529)).
- [Canada](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx?section=pol&dir=lim&document=lim2026&lang=e) and [France](https://transparency-france.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Juin-2015_Financement-de-la-vie-politique_Etat-des-lieux-recommandations.pdf) ban corporate and union donations, cap individual giving, and fund parties publicly.
- None bans all spending by organizations; campaign groups are capped, not banned.

### Alternatives to shareholder-first companies

| Model | Example | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worker cooperatives | [Mondragon](https://www.thenews.coop/mondragon-posts-positive-2025-revenue-and-job-figures/): €11.3B sales, 71,415 workers (2025) | Productivity and survival at least as good; cut pay before jobs; few are founded ([Pérotin](https://files.uniteddiversity.coop/Cooperatives/Worker_Co-ops/worker_co-op_report.pdf)) |
| Worker seats on boards | Germany: half the supervisory board above 2,000 staff | Small effects either way; shareholders keep the tie-break ([CEPR](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP16261.pdf)) |
| Employee ownership | US ESOPs: about 6,600 plans, 15M participants ([NCEO](https://www.nceo.org/articles/esops-by-the-numbers)) | About double the retirement wealth of similar workers ([Blasi & Kruse](https://cleo.rutgers.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/April-2025-FINAL-Employee-Ownership-and-ESOPs-What-We-Know-from-Recent-Research-.pdf)) |
| Foundation or steward ownership | Bosch, Novo Nordisk, [Patagonia](https://www.patagonia.com/ownership/) | As competitive, with better environmental and social scores ([Harvard Law Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2025/05/28/purpose-that-matters-what-foundation-ownership-teaches-us-about-sustainable-capitalism)) |
| Benefit corporations and B Corps | 9,576 certified firms ([B Corp](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_Corporation_(certification)>)) | Weakest option: the standards are not legally enforceable |

### Strongest objections

- A ban on all organizations also hits nonprofits, unions and the press. Citizens United itself was a nonprofit distributing a film. Both US amendment drafts protect the press.
- Banning organizations but not individuals shifts power to the very rich. Canada and France also cap individuals.
- Co-ops can become two-tier: only about a third of Mondragon's workers were members in 2016 ([Kasmir](https://globaldialogue.isa-sociology.org/articles/the-mondragon-cooperatives-successes-and-challenges)).

### What this means for the Charter

- Keep legal personhood as a granted privilege, and deny organizations political rights.
- Bar organizational spending on votes, protect the press, and cap individual spending too.
- If the Charter favors worker ownership, it needs membership paths for long-term workers to prevent two-tier firms.

## Direct digital democracy

Secure digital ID works at national scale, but independent security experts agree remote internet voting cannot yet be protected from malware on voters' devices, coercion or vote-buying.

### Digital ID and online voting today

- Estonia has run binding online voting since 2005. It peaked at 51.1% of voters in 2023, then fell to 41.7% in 2024 and about 45.6% in 2025 ([valimised.ee](https://www.valimised.ee/en/archive/statistics-about-internet-voting-estonia), [ERR](https://news.err.ee/1609843611/supreme-court-declines-to-hear-ekre-local-elections-e-vote-complaint)).
- To blunt coercion, a paper vote cancels an online one, and voters can revote online as often as they like.
- Smartphone voting became legal in 2024, but officials blocked it in 2025 because app-store apps could not be audited ([ERR](https://news.err.ee/1609723092/estonia-s-mobile-voting-delayed-by-auditing-concerns)).
- Every EU country must offer a digital identity wallet by 24 December 2026; rollout is uneven ([B2Trust](https://b2trust.com/en/blog/eidas-2-implementation-status-eu-27-q2-2026)).
- India's Aadhaar covers about 1.38 billion people. It also had a 12% biometric failure rate in 2018 and cost about 3 million voters their place on the rolls ([Aadhaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aadhaar)), and 1.5–2 million people lost welfare benefits during its rollout in one state ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26744)).

### What security research says

| Year | Finding | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2014 | Estonia's system was open to malware on voters' PCs and attacks on the counting server; reviewers urged halting it | [Springall, Halderman et al.](https://estoniaevoting.org/findings/summary/) |
| 2018 | Internet voting should not be used at present | [US National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2018/09/securing-the-vote-new-report) |
| 2020, reaffirmed 2024 | Returning ballots electronically is high-risk even with controls | [CISA, EAC, FBI, NIST](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/risk-management-electronic-ballot-delivery-marking-and-return) |
| 2020 | The Voatz blockchain app let attackers change or learn votes | [MIT](https://www.csail.mit.edu/node/7661) |
| 2021 | Blockchain fixes none of internet voting's problems and adds new ones | [Park, Specter, Narula & Rivest](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/PSNR21.pdf) |
| Jan 2026 | 21 computer scientists: no known technology can secure internet voting | [Princeton CITP](https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2026/01/16/internet-voting-is-insecure-and-should-not-be-used-in-public-elections/) |
| Mar 2026 | Basel-Stadt, Switzerland, could not decrypt 2,048 e-votes, with no attacker involved | [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/11/swiss_evote_usb_snafu/) |

Verification tools can expose some tampering on servers. They cannot stop malware on a voter's own device, coercion or vote-buying, and they cannot settle disputes when a voter spots cheating.

### Direct democracy at scale: Switzerland

- About four federal voting days a year, with turnout around 50% ([Voting in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_in_Switzerland)).
- About 11.5% of citizen initiatives passed between 1893 and 2014 ([Popular initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative_in_Switzerland)).
- Majorities passed a minaret ban (2009, 57.5%) and a face-covering ban (2021, 51.2%), both seen by Muslim groups as targeting them ([swissinfo](https://swissinfo.ch/eng/political-history_the-controversial-minaret-ban--ten-years-on/45399822), [swissinfo](https://swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss--burka-ban--vote-too-close-to-call/46420534)).
- In 2019 a court annulled a national vote for the first time, because the government had given voters wrong figures ([swissinfo](https://swissinfo.ch/eng/politics/wrong-statistics_historic-verdict-forces-swiss-re-vote-on-family-tax-breaks/44887174)).

### Hybrid models that help

- **Citizens' assemblies chosen by lot.** Ireland's 2016–17 assembly led to a 66.4% referendum win in 2018 ([Thirty-sixth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland)). When the government rewrote a later assembly's proposal, voters rejected both 2024 amendments ([2024 referendums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_constitutional_referendums)).
- **Online consensus-finding.** Taiwan's vTaiwan used Pol.is to find common ground on Uber rules. About 80% of its 26 cases led to government action, but results are not binding ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2018/08/21/240284/the-simple-but-ingenious-system-taiwan-uses-to-crowdsource-its-laws/)).
- **Liquid democracy**, where you can delegate your vote, concentrated power in a few super-voters and drew very few participants in Germany's Pirate Party ([Kling et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07723v1), [P2P Foundation](https://blog.p2pfoundation.net/the-failure-of-the-pirate-partys-direct-democracy-based-on-the-liquid-feedback-system/)).

### What this means for the Charter

- Use the digital ID to prove a person may vote, with privacy features like the EU wallet's selective disclosure, and keep ballot secrecy separate from identity.
- Put deliberation before each planetary vote: a randomly chosen assembly reviews the proposal, and the ballot matches what it recommends.
- Keep a non-digital way to vote, audits after every vote, and backup keys, so one failure never voids a decision.
- Entrench the Seven Principles so no majority vote can override them.
- Expect low, selective turnout; binding votes may need a minimum participation rule.

## Global enforcement

No existing global body can enforce on its own: the UN, ICC and Interpol all depend on countries choosing to act, and the EU works mainly through national courts and control of money.

| Body | Power on paper | What happens in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [UN Security Council](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-5) | Its decisions bind every member | No army of its own, and five permanent members can veto anything, including reform of the veto. 320 vetoes from 1946 to 2025 ([SCR](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/atf/cf/%7B65BFCF9B-6D27-4E9C-8CD3-CF6E4FF96FF9%7D/Veto_report_2026_F.pdf)), and a record $1.57B in unpaid dues by end of 2025 ([Reuters via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/01/30/guterres-warns-of-uns-imminent-financial-collapse/)) |
| [International Criminal Court](https://www.icc-cpi.int/about/the-court) | Tries individuals, including heads of state; 125 member states | No police and no trials in absentia; 20 of 49 people with warrants remain at large. Duterte reached The Hague in 2025 only because his own country's police arrested him ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/04/icc-confirms-charges-against-philippines-ex-president-duterte/)) |
| [Interpol](https://www.interpol.int/en/How-we-work/Notices/Red-Notices) | 196 members share wanted-person alerts (Red Notices) | A Red Notice is not an arrest warrant. Leaked files show about 86,000 notices, with authoritarian states the top filers ([Disclose](https://disclose.ngo/en/article/revelations-on-the-misuse-of-interpol-by-the-worlds-most-repressive-regimes)) |
| European Union | EU law overrides national law, and its court can fine states | Collected an unpaid fine from Hungary by deducting it from EU payments ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2024/09/18/brussels-moves-to-deduct-200-million-fine-from-hungarys-eu-funds-as-country-refuses-to-pay)), and froze about €35–36B ([Verfassungsblog](https://verfassungsblog.de/unfreezing-eu-funds-without-melting-the-rule-of-law/)). Its Article 7 sanctions have never reached the penalty stage ([Article 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_7_of_the_Treaty_on_European_Union)) |

### What makes enforcement work

- **Courts and money, not force.** Supranational law has worked where it runs through local courts and where breaking it costs the benefits of membership ([Helfer & Slaughter](https://scholarship.law.duke.edu/faculty_scholarship/2031), [Hathaway & Shapiro](https://yalelawjournal.org/pdf/1020_z6ibu4n2.pdf)).
- **Target individuals, not populations.** That is the ICC model, and the Earth Constitution arms its police only to arrest individuals ([WFM Canada](https://wfmcanada.org/blogs/news/constitution-for-the-federation-of-earth-structure-and-function)).
- **No vetoes, and no unanimity for sanctions.** Vetoes paralyse the UN, and unanimity let Hungary and Poland shield each other in the EU.
- **Independent infrastructure and revenue.** Under US sanctions, ICC officials lost Microsoft email and bank cards ([Karim Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Ahmad_Khan)), and US non-payment forced UN peacekeeping cuts.

### Transition: how countries have given up power

- The EU pooled sovereignty treaty by treaty, voluntarily, with a legal exit; the UK left in 2020.
- The Earth Constitution proposes stages: joining nations hand over weapons of mass destruction first, and national militaries are dismantled only at the final stage ([TRANSCEND](https://www.transcend.org/tms/?p=304701)).
- The ICC keeps jurisdiction over crimes committed while a country was a member, even after it leaves. That blocks joining, then leaving to escape justice.

### The no-exit problem

- Critics from Kant and Rawls to Ilya Somin and Bryan Caplan argue one world authority removes the two historical checks on tyranny: somewhere to flee, and rival states ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/world-government/), [Somin](https://volokh.com/?p=15118)).
- Caplan estimates a 5% chance of lasting global totalitarianism over the next thousand years with many states, rising to 25% with one ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/risks-of-stable-totalitarianism)).
- Albert Hirschman's *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* implies that when exit disappears, voice must become much stronger ([Exit, Voice, and Loyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit,_Voice,_and_Loyalty)).

### What this means for the Charter

- Enforce through a planetary court open to individuals, plus the power to withhold funds and benefits from people and institutions that break the Charter.
- Give the Commonwealth independent revenue, and cap any single region's share of it.
- Put an independent ombudsman over every agency, including the enforcers.
- Build internal exits: free movement between self-governing communities, and possibly a lawful opt-out procedure.
- Make jurisdiction over past acts survive any departure.

## Guardians

Guardians for the future and for nature mostly fail by being advisory only, underfunded, or abolished once they bite. The ones that last combine procedural powers with protected funding and independent appointment.

| Body | How chosen | Powers | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Wales Future Generations Commissioner](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2015/2/enacted) (2015) | Ministers appoint, 7-year term | Reviews; public bodies must follow advice or publicly explain why not | Credited with stopping a motorway ([Reasons to be Cheerful](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/meet-the-future-generations-commissioner-of-wales/)); Wales' auditor said in 2025 the law is not driving system-wide change ([Business News Wales](https://businessnewswales.com/ten-years-on-ambitions-of-the-well-being-of-future-generations-act-remain-unfulfilled)) |
| [Hungary's Ombudsman for Future Generations](https://fdsd.org/ideas/the-hungarian-parliaments-ombudsman-for-future-generations/) (2008) | Parliament | Opinions on draft laws; can trigger constitutional review | Downgraded to a deputy post in 2011–12 |

### What gives guardians real weight

- **A ladder of powers instead of a veto:** every major decision referred to them, a duty to follow their advice or explain, an automatic delay when long-term harm crosses a threshold, and standing in court.
- **Precaution and burden of proof.** Ecuador's courts stopped mining at Los Cedros by putting the burden on the state and developers ([Verfassungsblog](https://verfassungsblog.de/rights-of-nature-implementation/)). Germany's top court ordered lawmakers to set climate targets beyond 2030 to protect future generations' freedom ([BVerfG](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2021/bvg21-031.html)).
- **Funding and appointments out of reach of those they check:** endowments, default appointments, and holdover rules so the post never sits empty.
- **Duties that bind the guardians too.** In 2023 a New Zealand court found Te Urewera's own board acted unlawfully when 29 huts that supported predator control were burned ([Te Ao Māori News](https://www.teaonews.co.nz/2023/12/14/high-court-condemns-29-te-urewera-huts-burnt-down-as-unlawful/)).
- **Fact-finding kept separate from advocacy.** Israel's commissioner was attacked as an unelected moral arbiter before it was abolished.

### What this means for the Charter

Guardians fit naturally into direct democracy:

- Every planetary vote carries a Guardians' long-term impact statement on the ballot.
- A proposal that fails the long-view test triggers an automatic waiting period and a second vote.
- Guardians could be chosen by a mix of lot and nomination by scientific bodies, with long, single terms.
- Their budget is a fixed share of planetary revenue that no single vote or official can cut.
