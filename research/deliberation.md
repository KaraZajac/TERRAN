# Deciding together: assemblies, initiatives and direct democracy

*Research note · Charter Part VII.1, VII.3–4 and Part VIII.1–4, VIII.7–10 · first pass, 10 October 2026 · compiled by Claude, not yet reviewed by Kara*

Every precedent credited in Part VIII was advisory or indirect: Ireland's assemblies, vTaiwan, and the Earth Constitution's parliament-elected executive. Putting the assembly's own wording on the ballot (VIII.4) answers a documented Irish problem from 2024. But no precedent pairs lot-chosen drafting with binding votes near humanity's scale, and the evidence on quorums cuts against a minimum-turnout rule. The next draft needs an initiative threshold, assembly rules, the Stewards' term and removal, the Ombudsman's appointment, and a test for "planetary".

## What the Charter says

Part VII puts cooperation first (VII.1) and has every terran vote directly (VII.3). Decisions sit "as close as possible" to those affected; planetary matters, "including the ceiling", go to all (VII.4). In Part VIII, a proposal "backed by enough terrans" goes to an assembly (VIII.1), and online discussion maps agreement (VIII.2). An assembly "drawn by lot to mirror humanity" studies it with experts and Guardians, may improve it, and writes the question (VIII.3). Terrans vote on it "exactly as the assembly wrote it", by majority unless the Charter requires more (VIII.4). A Council of Stewards, "elected by all terrans for one fixed term", administers without veto or army (VIII.7). No body holds two powers (VIII.8). An independent Ombudsman may investigate any institution (VIII.9). Terrans may move to any community and organise peaceful opposition (VIII.10).

## Ireland's assemblies (VIII.3)

The Convention on the Constitution, approved in July 2012, had 66 randomly selected citizens, 33 politicians and a chair [citizens-assembly-ie/2026_convention-on-the-constitution]. Parliament set eight of its ten topics. The government accepted six recommendations for constitutional change and put two to referendums in May 2015 [citizens-assembly-ie/2026_convention-on-the-constitution]. Marriage equality passed with 62.1% of votes; the presidential-age change failed [ireland/2024_referendum-results-1937-2024]. Courant calls this "selective listening" or "cherry picking" [courant/2021_irish-model].

The 2016–18 Citizens' Assembly had 99 citizens and a judge as chair, appointed by the government [citizens-assembly-ie/2026_selection-2016-2018]. A market-research firm, REDC, recruited against quotas for gender, age and social class. Fifty-three members were replaced, and 20 recruits never attended. In January 2018, seven replacement members proved to have been recruited against the agreed method [citizens-assembly-ie/2026_selection-2016-2018]. Courant reports that one recruiter found them by phone and through friends and family [courant/2021_irish-model]. Parliament fixed its five topics [courant/2021_irish-model]. Its abortion recommendations led to the May 2018 referendum, which passed with 66.4% on 64.1% turnout [ireland/2024_referendum-results-1937-2024]. Two other topics had drawn "No reaction to date" by 2021 [courant/2021_irish-model].

| Later assembly | Recommendation | Government follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Gender Equality (2020–21), recruited "as advised by industry experts" [citizens-assembly-ie/2026_gender-equality-assembly] | A care clause that "obliges the State to take reasonable measures" [kildarenow/2024_varadkar-care-wording] | The government wrote "shall strive to support" family care [kildarenow/2024_varadkar-care-wording]. In March 2024 voters rejected the Family (67.7% No) and Care (73.9% No) amendments on 44.4% turnout [ireland/2024_referendum-results-1937-2024] |
| Dublin (2022): 67 residents, 12 councillors and a chair | A plebiscite on an elected mayor [citizens-assembly-ie/2022_dublin-assembly-report] | The 2025 programme will only "consider further plebiscites" [ireland/2025_programme-for-government] |
| Biodiversity Loss (2022–23) | 159 recommendations | October 2024: 15 implemented, 134 "in progress", 10 not [citizens-assembly-ie/2024_biodiversity-government-response] |
| Drugs Use (2023) | "A decriminalised model", among 36 [citizens-assembly-ie/2024_drugs-use-report-launch] | The 2025 programme diverts users to health services; no decriminalisation [ireland/2025_programme-for-government] |

On 10 October 2026 no assembly is running, and six have been held [citizens-assembly-ie/2026_selection-2016-2018]. The 2025 Programme for Government never mentions a citizens' assembly [ireland/2025_programme-for-government]. Courant lists "politicians' control, top-down agenda, constrained mini-public, and unclear public impacts" [courant/2021_irish-model].

Differs from the drafting-session research: the sources read here document the rewrite for the Care amendment. None shows that the rewrite caused the 2024 defeats.

**For the Charter:** Lot-chosen assemblies can produce proposals that win referendums. In Ireland, though, parliament chose the topics and the government chose which outputs reached a ballot. VIII.1 and VIII.4 remove both filters. That fixes the 2024 problem but drops an elected check. The draft needs its own: recruitment audits, replacement rules and review before a vote. Ireland used quota sampling by market-research firms, so "drawn by lot" should name a method.

## Other assemblies chosen by lot (VIII.3)

The OECD counted 289 cases from 1986 to October 2019 [oecd/2020_catching-the-deliberative-wave]. In OECD countries, 52% were local and 3% (eight) international [oecd/2020_catching-the-deliberative-wave]. Its "Citizens' Assembly" model averaged 90 members and 18.8 meeting days over 47 weeks [oecd/2020_catching-the-deliberative-wave].

Ostbelgien, Belgium's German-speaking Community, made sortition permanent in February 2019 [oecd/2020_catching-the-deliberative-wave]. A 24-member Citizens' Council, a third rotating every six months, sets the agenda and convenes panels of 25–50. Proposals backed by 100 citizens may be put to it, and parliament must answer each panel [oecd/2020_catching-the-deliberative-wave]. Its eighth assembly is due to meet in autumn 2026 [buergerdialog/2026_stand-der-dinge]. A 2022 review led to amendments to the founding decree in April 2024 [buergerdialog/2026_auswertung].

The 2021 Global Assembly was led by civil society, not governments [curato/2023_global-assembly-evaluation]. A lottery picked 100 population-weighted points, and 98 members met online for 68 hours [global-assembly/2022_report-2021]. They presented a declaration at COP26. The points fell in 49 of 193 countries. Members with over 12 years of schooling were 13 points above the global share. The assembly cost US$972,247. Its own report names "low impact on policy" as a known weakness of the model [global-assembly/2022_report-2021].

France's Citizens' Convention for Climate drew 150 people by lot and delivered 149 proposals in June 2020 [ccc/2021_avis-reponses-gouvernement]. President Macron had pledged to pass them on "sans filtre" [ccc/2021_site-officiel]. The government's October 2026 tracker says he kept 146 and lists 100 as implemented totally or partially [france-mte/2026_suivi-convention-citoyenne]. Its own filters give different totals, and it never counts proposals adopted unchanged. In February 2021 members graded the government's uptake 3.3 out of 10 [ccc/2021_avis-reponses-gouvernement]. Facilitators, not members, wrote that opinion's debate summaries [ccc/2021_avis-reponses-gouvernement].

**For the Charter:** Only Ostbelgien keeps sortition running with a citizen-set agenda, and it is advisory for one small community. The one global assembly had 100 members and no formal power. France shows that "without filter" erodes when elected bodies keep the last word. A binding ballot fixes that, but raises the stakes on who selects, briefs and facilitates.

## Can 100–1,000 people mirror humanity? (VIII.3)

A random sample of 1,067 has a margin of error of about ±3 points; a subgroup of 160, about ±8 [pew/2016_margin-of-error]. By the same formula, 100 members carry about ±10 points (own calculation). The world had 8.14 billion people in 2024 [worldbank/2026_wdi-population]. With 1,000 seats shared by population, 116 of 217 countries and territories get under one expected seat. With 100 seats, 198 do [worldbank/2026_wdi-population]. Stratification matches only chosen traits; the Global Assembly used five [global-assembly/2022_report-2021]. Attrition skews samples too: Ireland's 2016–18 assembly made 53 replacements across its 99 seats [citizens-assembly-ie/2026_selection-2016-2018].

**For the Charter:** At these sizes an assembly can match a few traits within known error, not every nation or language. The draft should name the traits and the size, and say whether several assemblies feed one decision.

## Mapping common ground: vTaiwan, Pol.is and bridging (VIII.2)

vTaiwan began in 2014, after the Sunflower Movement, as a joint project of the g0v civic-tech community and government [li-vigni/2026_online-participation]. Its founders describe consultation that reaches "rough consensus" on digital legislation [hsiao/2018_vtaiwan]. In Pol.is, people post statements and vote agree, disagree or pass. Principal component analysis and k-means clustering then group the voters [small/2021_polis]. A consensus score multiplies each group's agreement, which the authors say helps "protect from tyranny of the majority" [small/2021_polis]. In the 2015 Uber case, over 4,500 people voted on 145 statements, and the results fed a draft bill [bertelsmann/2022_taiwan-join-vtaiwan]. Other cases included online alcohol sales [small/2021_polis].

Audrey Tang coordinated vTaiwan from 2016 to 2022, then ran the digital ministry until 2024 [li-vigni/2026_online-participation]. No successor took over its government link, and volunteers now run it. It stayed confined to digital policy and was never binding. It handled 28 cases; official figures credit 80% with "decisive government action" [li-vigni/2026_online-participation]. Bertelsmann notes that "the government can refuse to discuss issues", and that tech-savvy people have an edge [bertelsmann/2022_taiwan-join-vtaiwan]. Critics fault Pol.is for "algorithmically-forced separation of participants into in-or-out groups" [li-vigni/2026_online-participation].

Differs from the drafting-session research: Li Vigni counts 28 cases, against 26 in its 2018 source. The 80% is a self-count, and vTaiwan has lacked a government sponsor since 2024.

X's Community Notes ranks notes by agreement across opposed raters. A note needs a score of at least 0.40 and raters from both sides; fewer than 10% qualify [x/2026_community-notes-ranking]. People shown such notes were less likely to reshare posts [wojcik/2022_birdwatch]. But a study of the roll-out found no evidence that it cut engagement with misleading posts; its authors suggest notes come too late [chuai/2024_community-notes-rollout].

**For the Charter:** These tools map opinion; they do not decide. Their evidence comes from thousands of people on narrow topics. Language, manipulation and speed at planetary scale are untested. VIII.2 could say that the map informs the assembly and that code and data are public.

## Initiative thresholds (VIII.1)

| Mechanism | Threshold | Share of electorate | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swiss popular initiative | 100,000 signatures in 18 months [switzerland-bk/2026_volksinitiativen] | 1.8% of 5.59 million voters (2024) [swissvotes/2026_dataset] | Binding vote; 26 of 244 passed, 1893–2026 [swissvotes/2026_dataset] |
| European Citizens' Initiative | 1 million in 12 months, with minimums in 7 states [eci/2026_how-it-works] | 0.28% of 355 million voters (2024) [wikipedia/2024_european-parliament-election] | A Commission reply only; 14 of 135 replied to by June 2026 [eci/2026_results-and-follow-up] |
| Taiwan referendum | 0.01% to propose, then 1.5% within six months [taiwan/2003_referendum-act] | As stated | Binding if yes beats no and reaches 25% of eligible voters |
| Taiwan's Join petitions | 5,000 supporters [bertelsmann/2022_taiwan-join-vtaiwan] | 0.02% of 23.5 million people | An agency reply; 289 of 13,853 qualified by June 2022 |
| Ostbelgien | 100 citizens [oecd/2020_catching-the-deliberative-wave] | Not computed | The Citizens' Council considers it |

The Commission credits legal change to two initiatives, on drinking water and glyphosate studies; five list no outcome [eci/2026_results-and-follow-up]. In Switzerland, Parliament may put a counter-proposal beside an initiative, and voters may back both [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution]. A tie-break question has settled double yeses since 1987 [swissvotes/2026_dataset]. In March 2026 a cash initiative failed (45.6%) while its counter-proposal passed (73.4%) [swissvotes/2026_dataset].

Differs from the drafting-session research: the Swiss initiative success rate through September 2026 is 10.7% (26 of 244). For its 1893–2014 window, the Swissvotes data give 11.2% (22 of 196), not 11.5% [swissvotes/2026_dataset].

At 8.14 billion people, 0.02% is about 1.6 million; 0.28%, 23 million; 1.8%, 146 million [worldbank/2026_wdi-population].

**For the Charter:** Low thresholds suit a step that leads to deliberation rather than straight to a vote. The ECI's spread rule stops one region from filling the agenda. Swiss counter-proposals let an assembly "improve" a proposal while voters keep the original.

## Direct votes at scale (VII.3, VIII.4)

From 2016 to 2025 Switzerland held 86 federal votes on 31 days, with mean turnout of 48.8% (range 34.5–65.7%) [swissvotes/2026_dataset]. From the 1970s to the 1990s, decade averages were 40.6–41.8% [swissvotes/2026_dataset].

Differs from the drafting-session research: about three voting days a year in 2016–2025, not four; turnout near 49%, and lower over the long run.

Majorities have restricted minorities. A minaret ban passed in 2009 with 57.5%, and a face-covering ban in 2021 with 51.2% [swissvotes/2026_dataset]. In municipal votes on naturalisation, "no" votes ran about 40% higher for applicants from (former) Yugoslavia and Turkey. The comparison was with similar applicants from northern and western Europe [hainmueller/2013_swiss-passport]. Swiss checks are thin. Parliament voids initiatives only for breaching mandatory international law or unity of form and subject. Courts must apply federal acts [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution].

The Venice Commission says texts that breach superior law, including human rights, may not go to a vote [venice-commission/2022_code-of-good-practice-referendums]. It advises against turnout and approval quorums, since a turnout quorum rewards abstention. It accepts an approval quorum for fundamental constitutional questions [venice-commission/2022_code-of-good-practice-referendums]. Taiwan shows the cost. In August 2025 a proposal won 74.2% of votes on 29.5% turnout, yet failed the 25% rule [wikipedia/2025_taiwanese-referendum]. The rule survived Taiwan's December 2025 amendments [taiwan/2003_referendum-act].

Differs from the drafting-session research: its minimum-participation rule runs against the Venice Commission's advice and Taiwan's experience.

Oregon's Citizens' Initiative Review is an uncredited precedent for VIII.3. A state commission draws 18–24 voters by survey sampling [oregon/2011_citizens-initiative-review-statute]. They file findings and pro and con statements of up to 250 words for the voters' pamphlet. Each campaign may comment on its side's statement first, and panels run only if funded [oregon/2011_citizens-initiative-review-statute]. Voters valued this neutral information from peers [gastil/2014_vicarious-deliberation].

**For the Charter:** Expect about half of terrans to vote on a question, and expect self-selection. The Seven Principles and the Court are the minority safeguards, but no clause says who checks a proposal before the vote. The evidence favours review before and after a vote, not a quorum.

## Decisions close to home (VII.1, VII.4)

Pius XI's 1931 encyclical called it "a grave evil" to give a higher body what lesser bodies can do [pius-xi/1931_quadragesimo-anno]. Art. 5(3) TEU lets the EU act only where member states cannot sufficiently achieve an aim and the Union can do better [european-parliament/2026_subsidiarity-factsheet]. National parliaments holding a third of votes can force a review. This "yellow card" was used in 2012, 2013 and 2016. According to a 2021 Parliament paper, the Court of Justice has never struck down a measure for breaching subsidiarity [european-parliament/2026_subsidiarity-factsheet]. Switzerland leaves cantons every power not given to the federation. The federation takes only tasks cantons cannot do or that need uniform rules [switzerland/1999_federal-constitution]. Ostrom defines polycentric governance as "many centers of decision making that are formally independent of each other" [ostrom/2009_beyond-markets-and-states]. Her design principles nest governance in layers and favour cheap local conflict resolution [ostrom/2009_beyond-markets-and-states]. That is the closest evidence base for VII.1's cooperation.

**For the Charter:** Existing systems use a list of central powers, a residual rule, a political brake and a court that rarely intervenes. VII.4 has none of these. It also puts "the ceiling" to a vote of all, while Part III and XI.4 tie the ceiling to science. The draft should say which governs.

## The Council of Stewards and planetary elections (VIII.7–8)

The Earth Constitution (1977, amended 1991) has a five-member Presidium and a 20–30 member Executive Cabinet, all drawn from its parliament [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]. Presidium members come one from each continental division. Parliament elects them from nominees, for five-year terms and at most two in a row, and can remove them for cause [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]. The executive has no veto, cannot dissolve parliament and cannot defy courts [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution]. The Charter keeps those limits but elects Stewards directly, for one term, with no removal clause. Asked about a Dublin mayor, the 2022 assembly backed a two-term limit (84%) and a removal mechanism (59%) [citizens-assembly-ie/2022_dublin-assembly-report].

No election has covered humanity. India's 2024 election had over 968 million eligible voters; 642 million voted over 44 days [wikipedia/2024_indian-general-election]. It needed over a million polling stations and 15 million staff and security personnel [wikipedia/2024_indian-general-election]. The 2024 European election had about 355–357 million registered voters [wikipedia/2024_european-parliament-election] and 50.74% turnout [european-parliament/2024_election-turnout]. Both filled hundreds of seats by constituency, not one race. Humanity is over eight times India's electorate [worldbank/2026_wdi-population]. Cost figures were not verified.

On VIII.8, lot-chosen assemblies both write ballot questions (VIII.3) and choose judges (IX.2). The rule should say whether each assembly counts as a separate body.

**For the Charter:** The credited model avoids a planetary popular vote. Its executive is collegial, regionally balanced, elected by parliament and removable. The Charter must say who can remove a Steward and how long a term lasts.

## The Ombudsman (VIII.9)

Sweden created its Parliamentary Ombudsman in 1809 so the Riksdag could control executive power [sweden-jo/2026_history]. The office now reviews courts and public authorities, independent of the executive [sweden-jo/2026_home]. The Riksdag elects the Ombudsmen. Their remit excludes members of the Riksdag, ministers and local councillors, and they may prosecute officials [sweden/2023_act-with-instruction-parliamentary-ombudsmen]. The Earth Constitution has a council of five World Ombudsmen, elected by parliament for ten-year terms, who may start court cases [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution].

**For the Charter:** VIII.9 has one Ombudsman with unlimited reach, and IX.10 adds oversight of courts and enforcers. Sweden shows that court supervision can work, but it leaves elected politicians to political control. The draft omits who chooses the Ombudsman, the term, the powers, and who checks the office. The credit line should name Sweden.

## Free movement and dissent (VIII.10)

The UDHR protects movement within each state and the right to leave any country (Art. 13), and peaceful assembly and association (Art. 20) [un/1948_udhr]. It grants no right to enter any community.

**For the Charter:** VIII.10 goes further, with a right to move into any community. That clashes with Part VI's communities built around a shared vision. The draft should say whether communities may set entry conditions while exit stays free.

## Tensions and open problems

- **Threshold (VIII.1).** "Enough terrans" has no number, time window or spread rule.
- **Ownership (VIII.3).** An assembly may rewrite a citizen's proposal before the vote. Switzerland keeps the original on the ballot.
- **Accountability (VIII.3–4).** Lot-chosen members would write binding text without election or recall. Staff shape outputs too, as French facilitators did.
- **Representation.** At 1,000 seats, 116 of 217 countries and territories get under one expected seat.
- **Recruitment.** Ireland's 2018 breach and the Global Assembly's skews show the need for audits.
- **Quorums.** The drafting session's minimum-turnout idea conflicts with the Venice Commission and with Taiwan's 2025 result.
- **Minorities.** There is no pre-vote review, and no clause says whether the Court can void a vote.
- **Volume.** Switzerland asks about nine questions a year; VII.3 sets no limit.
- **Voting age.** VII.2–3 give an identity from birth but set no voting age.
- **Planetary versus community (VII.4).** There is no list and no umpire, and the ceiling is both voted and set by science.
- **Stewards (VIII.7).** One planetary race has no precedent. "One fixed term" is ambiguous, and there is no removal clause.
- **Separation (VIII.8).** Assemblies both draft questions and choose judges.
- **Ombudsman (VIII.9).** Selection, term, powers and oversight are unstated.
- **Movement (VIII.10).** A right to enter any community conflicts with Part VI.
- **Credits.** Ireland has no assembly running, and vTaiwan has no government sponsor. Oregon, Switzerland, Ostbelgien and Sweden go uncredited.
- **Not verified here.** How many French proposals passed unchanged; election costs; further studies of minorities under direct democracy.

## Options for the next draft

- Set the VIII.1 threshold as a share, a window and a spread rule, such as Taiwan's two steps or the ECI's state minimums.
- Put both the original and the assembly's version on the ballot, with a tie-break question, as Switzerland does.
- Model assembly outputs on Oregon: findings, the case for and against, word limits, and a right of reply for proposers.
- Have the Planetary Court check proposals against the Seven Principles before votes, in line with the Venice Commission's code.
- Use simple majorities without turnout quorums. Keep supermajorities for Charter changes (XI.3), and consider regional double majorities for fundamental planetary matters.
- Set assembly size, traits, a published selection algorithm, audits and replacement rules. Consider regional assemblies feeding a global one.
- Consider a standing council chosen by lot, as in Ostbelgien, to sort proposals and track follow-through.
- Give the Stewards a stated term, a re-election rule and a removal procedure. Consider indirect election or regional seats.
- Specify the Ombudsman's selection, term, size, powers and oversight, and credit Sweden.
- List planetary powers, leave the rest to communities, add a community objection procedure and name an umpire. Settle the ceiling's status.
- Publish VIII.2's algorithms and data, and treat the maps as input only.
- Say whether communities may set entry conditions.
