# Money without nations

*Research note · Charter Part IV (amounts note), the open question on money, and Parts VII, VIII and XII where they touch money · first pass, 10 October 2026 · compiled by Claude, not yet reviewed by Kara*

The draft's yardstick is undefined: "adjusted each year for prices" names no index, and the obvious one, US CPI, has no October 2025 reading. A dollar limit also bites unevenly: in 2021, $10 million at market rates bought about five times more consumption in India than in Switzerland. The euro shows shared money needs a common central bank and crisis backstops; global plans since Keynes produced only the limited SDR. The cheapest sound step is a Commonwealth unit of account, an index rather than a currency, with any issuer left to later stages.

## What the Charter says

- Part IV.4 caps wealth at $10 million. Part IV.5 frees the first $2 million from wealth tax.
- The note under Part IV reads: "Amounts are in 2026 US dollars, adjusted each year for prices, until the Commonwealth has its own money."
- The open question asks what money the Commonwealth uses "in place of US dollars".
- Related clauses: no nations (Part I.2); a Charter that "belongs to no government" (Preamble); digital identity from birth (VII.2).
- Also related: blockchain ledgers "wherever it truly helps" (VII.5), privacy (VII.6), one power per body (VIII.8), and stage-two planetary revenue (XII.3).
- No clause names an issuer, a governor of money, or a price index. Part V.3's pay limits are ratios, so they need no currency.

## The draft's yardstick: US dollars and US prices

- US consumer prices (CPI-U) rose 3.4% in the 12 months to August 2026 [bls/2026_cpi-august-release].
- The 2025 shutdown stopped price collection. BLS "did not issue an October 2025 CPI news release" [bls/2026_shutdown-cpi-faq].
- For most series, BLS will not publish 12-month changes for October 2026 either [bls/2026_shutdown-cpi-faq].
- The World Bank's International Comparison Program sets global price levels. Its latest benchmark, for 2021, came out in May 2024; later years are extrapolated [worldbank/2026_icp-programme].
- In June 2025 the World Bank raised its extreme-poverty line from $2.15 a day (2017 PPP) to $3.00 (2021 PPP). The 2022 count rose from 713 million to 838 million [worldbank/2025_june-2025-poverty-lines].
- The new PPPs raised measured incomes 13% on average, against 11% US inflation; in low-income countries, about 21% [worldbank/2025_june-2025-poverty-lines].

What $10 million means in different places. Price level is household-consumption PPP over the market exchange rate, from World Bank data updated 8 October 2026. Post-2021 values are extrapolations [worldbank/2026_wdi-ppp-price-levels].

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Switzerland | 1.36 | $7.3M | $13.6M | 1.28 |
| United States | 1.00 | $10.0M | $10.0M | 1.00 |
| China | 0.62 | $16.1M | $6.2M | 0.48 |

**For the Charter:** The draft gives no index, base month or method. US CPI is one state's statistic, and the shutdown showed it depends on US budget politics. A market-rate dollar limit is loose where prices are low and tight where they are high. A PPP limit flips this, capping a terran in India at about $2.6 million of tradable assets. PPP suits the floor, which is about consumption; market rates suit the limit, which is about tradable wealth.

## Keynes's Clearing Union and the bancor (1942–44)

- On 28 August 1942 the British embassy sent Washington Keynes's "Proposals for an International Clearing Union" [keynes/1942_clearing-union-proposals].
- Central banks would hold "international bank-money, called (let us say) bancor, fixed (but not unalterably) in terms of gold". Everyone else kept national money [keynes/1942_clearing-union-proposals].
- Quotas followed trade. Balances above a quarter of quota, credit or debit, paid 1% a year to a Reserve Fund; above half, another 1% [keynes/1942_clearing-union-proposals].
- At Bretton Woods in July 1944, 44 nations chose something closer to Harry Dexter White's plan. It pooled national currencies and gold instead of creating a currency [federal-reserve-history/2013_bretton-woods-created].
- White's side feared the United States would end up holding most bancor. The dollar stayed pegged to gold at $35 an ounce until 1971 [federal-reserve-history/2013_bretton-woods-created].

**For the Charter:** This is the closest precedent for money above nations, though its members were states. Charging both surpluses and deficits gives a ready revenue stream for something like the Commons Fund. Cite the 1942 text directly. Zhou's 2009 essay says bancor was "based on the value of 30 representative commodities"; Keynes's text fixes it in gold [zhou/2009_reform-international-monetary-system].

## The SDR, and legal amounts written in SDRs

- The IMF created the SDR in 1969. It is "not a currency", and private parties cannot hold it. The 2021 allocation was about SDR 456 billion, then about $650 billion [imf/2023_sdr-factsheet].
- Since 1 August 2022 the weights are: dollar 43.38%, euro 29.31%, renminbi 12.28%, yen 7.59%, sterling 7.44%. The next review is due by end-July 2027 [imf/2022_sdr-valuation-review].
- On 9 October 2026, SDR 1 was worth $1.353260 [imf/2026_sdr-valuation]. By my arithmetic, the dollar made up 42.7% of its value, and $10 million was about SDR 7.39 million.
- Allocations and quota changes need 85% of IMF voting power, so any bloc above 15% can block them [imf/1944_articles-of-agreement].
- The Montreal Convention writes airline liability limits in SDRs and ICAO reviews them under Article 24 [icao/2024_montreal-convention-revised-limits].
- The Article 21 limit rose from SDR 100,000 to 128,821 (December 2019) and 151,880 (December 2024), up 51.9% since 1999 [icao/2024_montreal-convention-revised-limits].

**For the Charter:** A unit no single state issues can exist and be written into law. But the SDR is a basket of five national currencies, so it inherits their monetary policies and the IMF's weighted votes. The Montreal Convention shows "adjusted for prices" can work in a supranational unit through scheduled reviews.

## Global reserve plans of 2009: the Stiglitz Commission and Zhou Xiaochuan

- A UN commission chaired by Joseph Stiglitz reported on 21 September 2009. It called a global reserve currency "an idea whose time has come" (Chapter 5, para. 7) [un/2009_stiglitz-commission-report].
- The IMF could build it on SDRs, or a new "Global Reserve Bank" could run it. Simple versions would issue $150–300 billion a year (paras. 33, 40) [un/2009_stiglitz-commission-report].
- Allocations could penalise large surpluses, and fuller versions could fund development or global public goods (paras. 39, 41) [un/2009_stiglitz-commission-report].
- On 23 March 2009, China's central bank governor called for a reserve currency "disconnected from individual nations" [zhou/2009_reform-international-monetary-system].
- Zhou proposed widening the SDR basket to all major economies, perhaps weighted by GDP, and letting the IMF manage pooled reserves [zhou/2009_reform-international-monetary-system].

**For the Charter:** Both plans keep states as members and target reserves, not people's money. Two ideas carry over. A basket of all major economies could define a Commonwealth unit, and new issuance could fund global public goods.

## Lietaer's Terra, and the other Terra

- Bernard Lietaer set out the Terra in *The Future of Money* (German 1999, English 2001) [lietaer/2008_terra-interview].
- He called it "a complementary, privately issued, supra-national trade reference currency". About a dozen commodities and services would back it, including oil, wheat, copper and gold [lietaer/2008_terra-interview].
- A demurrage charge of 3.5–4% a year would discourage hoarding. It was meant for corporations, not households, alongside national money [lietaer/2008_terra-interview].
- By 2008 a multinational's board had vetoed a pilot, and an economic model was still being funded [lietaer/2008_terra-interview]. I found no record of a later launch.
- "Terra" is also the blockchain behind TerraUSD and LUNA. They crashed in May 2022, "resulting in over $40 billion worth in investor losses" [doj/2025_kwon-sentenced].
- Founder Do Kwon got 15 years on 11 December 2025. Terraform claimed user governance; prosecutors said a trading firm secretly propped up its peg in 2021 [doj/2025_kwon-sentenced].

**For the Charter:** Demurrage matches Part IV's view of hoarding. But the Terra was private, built for corporations, and had not launched by 2008. My own inference: a basket of oil, metals and grain ties money's value to extraction, which sits awkwardly with the ceiling. The name "terra" is taken twice, once by a failed proposal and once by a fraud.

## The euro: one money, many treasuries

- The euro began on 1 January 1999 as book money; cash followed in 2002. It is legal tender in 21 of 27 EU states, including Bulgaria from 2026, for about 358 million people [ecb/2026_euro-introduction].
- Monetary union came in three stages, starting 1990, 1994 and 1999. Stage one included "free use of the ECU", the euro's forerunner [ecb/2026_emu-history].
- The euro came without significant banking or fiscal union. It relied on deficit and debt limits (3% and 60% of GDP) and a "no bailout" rule [lane/2012_european-sovereign-debt-crisis].
- Greece lost market access in May 2010, Ireland in November 2010, Portugal in April 2011. Lane traces the crisis to "the flawed original design of the euro" [lane/2012_european-sovereign-debt-crisis].
- On 26 July 2012 Mario Draghi said the ECB was "ready to do whatever it takes to preserve the euro" [draghi/2012_global-investment-conference-speech].
- Optimum currency area theory asks for labour mobility or flexible wages, openness, diversified economies and fiscal transfers. A later strand says shared money builds some of these over time [dellas-tavlas/2009_optimum-currency-area-odyssey].

**For the Charter:** The euro shows many states can share one money, introduced in stages as Part XII plans. Its first decade shows the cost of money arriving before fiscal and banking union. The Charter has two conditions in view: free movement (VIII.10) and planetary revenue (XII.3). That argues for any common currency coming after a working Commonwealth treasury.

## Currency competition (Hayek, 1976)

- Hayek argued governments should lose their monopoly of money. Gresham's law, he wrote, applies only where law fixes exchange rates (pp. 41–43, 1990 edition) [hayek/1976_denationalisation-of-money].
- His example was a private "ducat", redeemable for 5 Swiss francs, 5 D-marks or 2 dollars. Its issuer would hold it stable against a commodity basket (pp. 46–48) [hayek/1976_denationalisation-of-money].
- He expected regions "much exceeding present national territories" to settle on standard baskets of wholesale prices (p. 75) [hayek/1976_denationalisation-of-money].
- Today's nearest test is stablecoins. They track fiat money, "overwhelmingly the US dollar", and often trade at varying rates, like 19th-century US free-banking notes [bis/2025_next-generation-monetary-system].

**For the Charter:** Competing monies fit Part VI's "utopia of utopias". But the wealth limit needs one measuring rod, which competition does not supply. Hayek himself expected a few region-wide standards. So far, private money has converged on the dollar, not on new standards.

## Community currencies: WIR, Bristol Pound, Sardex

| | WIR (Switzerland) | Bristol Pound (UK) | Sardex (Sardinia) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | 1934 | 2012 | 2009 or 2010 (sources differ) |
| Form | Bank-run credit money for small firms | Paper and digital; council tax payable | Electronic mutual credit, 1:1 with the euro, not convertible |
| Scale | Over 70,000 customers (2016 paper) | Peak about 1 million Bristol Pounds a year; about 0.02% of residents used it | 1,477 firms, 48,170 transactions (2013–14) |
| Trend | Turnover falling since 1992; deposits down 8.7% in 2025 | Withdrawn 2021; successor closed 2023 | Core user group down from 14,072 to 10,323, 2022–24 |

- WIR turnover rises in recessions, and WIR prices often exceed franc prices [stodder-lietaer/2016_wir-macro-stability]. Lietaer co-wrote that study and promoted such currencies.
- At end-2025, WIR-currency deposits were CHW 352.0 million, against CHF 4.7 billion of total deposits [wir/2026_annual-results-2025].
- The Bristol Pound ran from 2012 to 2021; its successor, Bristol Pay, closed in mid-2023 for lack of funds [bristol-pound/2024_legacy-site].
- The Pound itself ended when its company "failed to secure the funding it needed to survive". Its director said about 80% of users had a degree [bristol-cable/2022_bristol-pound-director].
- Sardex firms trading in payment loops had higher turnover and less debt build-up; a Sardex co-founder co-wrote this study [iosifidis/2018_cyclic-motifs-sardex]. A 2025 preprint reads the network's shrinking core as "declining liquidity recirculation" [ancona/2025_sardex-resilience].

**For the Charter:** These are working models for money inside Part VI communities. They last when they meet a clear need, like WIR's credit for small firms, and fade when official money is cheap. None replaces national money; all measure value in it.

## Cryptocurrencies: volatility, energy, governance

- BIS calls unbacked crypto a speculative asset with "large price gyrations". It finds stablecoins fail the tests of singleness, elasticity and integrity [bis/2025_next-generation-monetary-system].
- From 1 October 2025 to 9 October 2026, bitcoin's annualised volatility was 44.2%, with a 53.1% peak-to-trough fall [coinmetrics/2026_btc-price-series].
- The euro against the dollar had 5.5% volatility and a 6.7% maximum fall over the same dates [ecb/2026_eur-usd-reference-rates]. Both are my calculations from daily data.
- In April 2025, Cambridge researchers put bitcoin's electricity use at 138 TWh a year, about 0.5% of the world's. A linked study puts proof-of-stake Ethereum at 7.87 GWh [cambridge-judge/2025_bitcoin-mining-energy].
- El Salvador made bitcoin legal tender in 2021. A 2022 study found its use low, concentrated and falling, with privacy fears a key barrier [alvarez/2022_bitcoin-legal-tender-el-salvador].
- A December 2024 IMF deal committed El Salvador to voluntary acceptance and dollar-only taxes [imf/2024_el-salvador-eff-agreement]. Congress passed the acceptance change 55–2 in January 2025 [theblock/2025_el-salvador-bitcoin-law].

**For the Charter:** Nothing here supports a cryptocurrency as the Commonwealth's unit. Bitcoin's electricity use sits awkwardly with the ceiling, and its swings would make a wealth limit lurch. On this evidence, Part VII.5's "wherever it truly helps" would not cover issuing money.

## Central bank digital currencies, as of October 2026

- A 2024 BIS survey of 93 central banks found 91% exploring a CBDC. Only three ran live retail ones: the Bahamas, Jamaica and Nigeria [bis/2025_cbdc-survey-2024].
- Many consider holding limits: 56% of advanced and 63% of emerging economies [bis/2025_cbdc-survey-2024].
- The ECB moved the digital euro to its next phase in October 2025, with a pilot in 2027. It aims for possible first issuance in 2029, if EU law passes in 2026 [ecb/2026_digital-euro].
- On 9 July 2026 the European Parliament voted 416–169 to open talks with member states. It wants offline use, privacy, and acceptance by most businesses [eunews/2026_digital-euro-parliament-vote].
- On 21 September 2026, Council officials met to prepare the third round of talks [council-eu/2026_digital-euro-trilogue-notice]. I found no adopted regulation as of 10 October 2026.
- In the US, Executive Order 14178 (23 January 2025) bars agencies from acting "to establish, issue, or promote CBDCs", citing privacy and sovereignty [us/2025_executive-order-14178].

**For the Charter:** Digital identity from birth (VII.2) plus a Commonwealth money would amount to a retail CBDC. The digital euro's choices are the current reference: offline use, holding limits, banks as intermediaries. El Salvador and the US order show the political cost when money and identity merge without strong privacy.

## Who governs money: independence or direct votes?

- EU law bars the ECB from taking instructions (Article 130). Board members serve one eight-year term, removable only for incapacity or serious misconduct [ecb/2026_independence].
- The Eurosystem may not lend to public bodies, and the ECB can adopt binding regulations [ecb/2026_independence].
- Evidence on independence is mixed. In 118 developing countries (1980–2013), more legal independence went with lower inflation [garriga-rodriguez/2020_cbi-inflation-developing].
- An earlier cross-country study found independence "relatively unimportant" next to openness and tax factors [campillo-miron/1997_why-inflation-differs].
- Swiss voters set money's constitutional rules directly. The 2014 "Save our Swiss gold" initiative won 22.7% and no cantons [switzerland/2014_gold-initiative-results].
- The 2018 initiative for money creation by the central bank alone won 24.3% and no cantons [switzerland/2018_vollgeld-initiative-results].
- On 8 March 2026 a "cash is freedom" initiative failed at 45.6%. A counter-proposal on the currency and cash supply passed at 73.4% [switzerland/2026_cash-initiative-results].
- The Earth Constitution lets world government "establish, issue and regulate world currency, credit and exchange" (Article IV.17), moving to "a common global currency" [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution].
- Its financial agency would fund government with credit "without requiring the payment of interest" (Article VIII, Sec. G-1-f) [wcpa/1977_earth-constitution].

**For the Charter:** A central bank makes rules, runs operations and imposes sanctions, a mix Part VIII.8 forbids in one body. The Charter already gives judges and Guardians the central-bank template: one long term, removal only for misconduct. Swiss voters set money's rules, and rejected both radical redesigns put to them here. If the Charter borrows the Earth Constitution's money powers, it need not borrow its self-financing credit, which EU rules forbid.

## Tensions and open problems

- **Undefined index.** "Adjusted each year for prices" needs an index, base period and method. US CPI-U lacks an October 2026 12-month change for most series.
- **A national yardstick in a nationless charter.** Dollars and BLS data tie Charter thresholds to one state's currency, statistics agency and budget fights.
- **Market rates or PPP.** Market-rate dollars favour the rich in low-price countries; PPP dollars cap them at a fraction of tradable value. Nigeria's price level more than halved from 2021 to 2024.
- **Exchange-rate noise.** Wealth above the limit passes on "each year" (IV.4). The euro's dollar value ranged about 7% last year, moving euro holders toward or away from a dollar limit.
- **Nominal or real earnings.** Part IV.6 caps taxes at what assets "earn in a year". At 3.4% inflation, a 3.4% nominal return earns nothing real, so a 1% wealth tax exceeds real earnings.
- **"Its own money" assumes one currency.** The euro needed a central bank, then emergency backstops. The Commons Fund is not a treasury, and planetary revenue is undesigned.
- **No issuer, no governor.** Nobody is named to create money, set its supply, or rescue banks in a panic. Any answer collides with Part VIII.8.
- **Direct votes on money.** Swiss voters rejected the gold and sovereign-money initiatives about 3 to 1. A new currency put to a planetary vote could fail; this is an inference.
- **Privacy and blockchain.** Digital identity plus digital money risks surveillance (Principle III, VII.6). Bitcoin's energy use conflicts with the ceiling, and Terra/LUNA's "user governance" hid manipulation.
- **The SDR is not neutral.** It is 43% dollar by weight and changes only with IMF supermajorities.
- **Naming and credits.** "Terra" is taken by a 2001 proposal and a collapsed stablecoin. If the Charter credits Keynes, it should cite the 1942 text, not Zhou's commodity account.

## Options for the next draft

These are options, not decisions. Kara decides.

- **Split the open question.** One: what unit measures Charter amounts? Two: what money do terrans use, and who may issue it? The first needs an answer now.
- **Minimal fix.** Name the index, for example "US CPI-U, annual average", with a published fallback when data are missing. Simple, but keeps the national tie.
- **SDR amounts, Montreal-style.** State amounts in SDRs (the limit is about SDR 7.4 million). Revise them for inflation on a fixed schedule, as ICAO does.
- **PPP amounts.** Define amounts in 2021-PPP dollars, as the World Bank does for poverty. Fairer for consumption, harsher on wealth in low-price places.
- **Two yardsticks.** Measure the floor in PPP terms and the wealth limit in a market-rate basket unit.
- **Plural money, one yardstick.** Let communities use national, community or private money. Measure all Charter amounts in one Commonwealth unit.
- **A clearing union for stage two.** Settle balances between member currencies in the Commonwealth unit. Charge large surpluses and deficits alike, paying the charges to the Commons Fund.
- **Book money before cash.** Any Commonwealth currency could start as an accounting unit for revenue and floor payments, as the euro did in 1999–2001. A single currency would follow only with fiscal and banking backstops, by amendment.
- **A money board that fits Part VIII.** Terrans set money's goals by vote. An independent board with single long terms runs operations, under Court and Ombudsman oversight, and may not lend to Stewards or the Commons Fund.
- **Commons Fund as recipient, not issuer.** It could receive clearing charges or issuance profits, but not create money for itself.
- **A privacy floor.** For any digital money: offline use, holding limits, no programmable spending rules, and cash kept.
- **A new name.** Avoid "terra" for any unit or currency.
