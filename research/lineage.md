# Lineage and prior art

*Research note · Charter Preamble, Part II, Part VI and the movement's name · first pass, 10 October 2026 · compiled by Claude, not yet reviewed by Kara*

The Seven Principles track The Satanic Temple's Seven Fundamental Tenets in number, order and substance, yet Part II credits no source. Part VI restates Robert Nozick's "framework for utopia", also uncredited, while adding a floor and wealth limit that Nozick opposed. Studies of exit and of 19th-century communes suggest exit rights protect less than they seem, and most secular communes failed within a decade. The humanist and global-rights precedents share most of the Charter's ethics, but none abolishes nations, and the rights texts work through states.

## What the Charter says

- **Preamble.** Terrans are "one species, sharing one planet and one future". Between floor and ceiling "lies a safe and just space for humanity", where each terran may build a utopia. It belongs to "every terran who chooses to live by it".
- **Part II.** Compassion, Justice, Autonomy, Freedom, Knowledge, Repair, Spirit over Letter. They "bind every terran and every institution in the Commonwealth".
- **Part VI.** A right to pursue one's own good (VI.1). Communities that respect the principles, floor and ceiling (VI.2). Exit, and no imposed utopia (VI.3). A Commonwealth that "chooses no utopia for anyone" but guarantees floor, ceiling and resources (VI.4). Limits at the floor, others' freedom and the ceiling (VI.5).
- **The name.** "Terran Humanism", "terrans" (Part I), "Terran Commonwealth" (I.4, XII), "Terran Assembly" (VIII.3).
- **Credits.** Parts III, IV, V, VII, VIII, IX, X and XII end with "draws on" notes. The Preamble and Parts I, II, VI and XI have none. "A safe and just space for humanity" is the title of Kate Raworth's 2012 Oxfam paper, credited only under Part III [raworth/2012_safe-and-just-space].

## The Satanic Temple's Seven Tenets

TST publishes its "Seven FUNDAMENTAL TENETS" on its "About us" page, checked 10 October 2026 [tst/2026_about-us]. It says they were "inspired by 18th Century enlightenment values" but "designed for current times" [tst/2026_about-us]. NPR names Lucien Greaves as a co-founder [npr/2018_satanists-sue-netflix].

In January 2013 TST listed nine tenets, each tied to "one of the choir of nine Angels" [tst/2013_nine-tenets]. The last invoked a spirit of "compassion and wisdom" [tst/2013_nine-tenets]. Captures from July to 12 December 2013 show a revised list, still nine [tst/2013_nine-tenets]. Seven tenets first appear in a capture of 15 January 2014 [tst/2014_seven-tenets]. That text added "and justice" to the last tenet and "should prevail over laws and institutions" to the second [tst/2014_seven-tenets]. Later edits were minor, such as "forego your own" becoming "forgo one's own" [tst/2014_seven-tenets] [tst/2026_about-us]. The Charter follows the post-2014 text.

| Charter principle | TST tenet, current text [tst/2026_about-us] | What the Charter changed or added |
|---|---|---|
| I. Compassion | Act "with compassion and empathy toward all creatures in accordance with reason" | "Should strive" becomes "owes"; "every living being"; a maxim on sentimentality and cruelty |
| II. Justice | Struggle for justice "should prevail over laws and institutions" | Laws are tools that "must change" when unjust |
| III. Autonomy | "One’s body is inviolable, subject to one’s own will alone." | Adds the mind; names government, community, majority, market |
| IV. Freedom | Respect others' freedoms, "including the freedom to offend"; willful, unjust encroachment forfeits one's own | Adds equality, dissent, doubt; drops "willfully"; forfeits "the Charter's protection" |
| V. Knowledge | Beliefs "should conform to one's best scientific understanding of the world" | "Evidence" for "science"; knowledge as common property; no burying it |
| VI. Repair | "People are fallible"; rectify mistakes and resolve the harm | Covers institutions and the Charter; adds "learn"; a duty |
| VII. Spirit over Letter | "The spirit of compassion, wisdom, and justice should always prevail over the written or spoken word." | Same triad, now a rule for reading a constitution (XI.2) |

Verbatim overlap is limited to short phrases, chiefly "compassion, wisdom, and justice" and "laws and institutions".

TST's legal notice lists its name, logo and SatanCon as trademarks, and claims copyright in its logo and Baphomet statue [tst/2019_legal-notice]. It ends "© 2019 All rights reserved" [tst/2019_legal-notice]. It says nothing specific about the tenets; I found no licence or attribution terms for them. TST offers the tenets as a downloadable poster [tst/2026_about-us]. It has litigated over its intellectual property, suing Netflix and Warner Bros. in 2018 over its statue design [npr/2018_satanists-sue-netflix]. It describes its reproductive-autonomy advocacy as work "in accordance with our tenets" [tst/2026_about-us].

**For the Charter:** Part II closely adapts a religious organization's creed, and is one of the few Parts without a credit. It also turns TST's "should" into duties that "bind every terran". In a creed, forfeiting freedom is a moral warning; in a constitution, it strips legal protection. Whether and how to credit TST is a question for Kara.

| Text | World order | Economy | Nature and future |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manifesto I, 1933 [aha/1933_humanist-manifesto-i] | Not addressed | "acquisitive and profit-motivated society" inadequate; "a socialized and cooperative economic order" | "man is a part of nature" |
| Manifesto II, 1973, Kurtz and Wilson [aha/1973_humanist-manifesto-ii] | "transnational federal government", keeping "pride in national origins" | "a minimum guaranteed annual income"; cut "extreme disproportions in wealth" | Planet "a single ecosystem"; check "excessive population growth" |
| Manifesto 2000, Kurtz [kurtz/2000_humanist-manifesto-2000] | Elected World Parliament in the UN; no Security Council veto; world court with enforcement | An income tax to help poorer countries; free markets "one strategy" | Environmental agency; "generations yet to be born" |
| Manifesto III, 2003 [aha/2003_humanist-manifesto-iii] | "global community" only | "a just distribution of nature’s resources" | "a planetary duty to protect nature’s integrity, diversity, and beauty" |
| Amsterdam Declaration 2022 [humanists-international/2022_amsterdam-declaration] | Backs "the rule of law"; rejects "authoritarian nationalism" | Not addressed | Duty of care to future generations and "all sentient beings" |

Manifesto II says: "We deplore the division of humankind on nationalistic grounds" [aha/1973_humanist-manifesto-ii]. Its ninth article says the state should not "espouse a single ideology" [aha/1973_humanist-manifesto-ii]. Manifesto 2000 proposes a "Planetary Bill of Rights and Responsibilities" that goes beyond the UDHR; only an abridged web text was read [kurtz/2000_humanist-manifesto-2000]. Manifesto III calls its values "subject to change as our knowledge and understandings advance" [aha/2003_humanist-manifesto-iii]. The AHA treats Manifesto I as superseded by III [aha/1933_humanist-manifesto-i]. Humanists International adopted the 2022 Declaration at its General Assembly in Glasgow [humanists-international/2022_amsterdam-declaration]. It says "no one is infallible or omniscient", and "we seek neither to avoid scrutiny nor to impose our view on all humanity" [humanists-international/2022_amsterdam-declaration].

**For the Charter:** Terran Humanism inherits this tradition's core: reason with compassion, fallibility, revisable values, a world community, an economy judged by need. Kurtz's "planetary humanism" is its nearest named ancestor. The Charter departs in three ways. It abolishes nations, which Manifesto II keeps inside a federation. It binds every terran, which the Amsterdam Declaration declines to do. And it puts justice above law, where these texts stress the rule of law.

## Global charters and rights texts

**Earth Charter.** Maurice Strong and Mikhail Gorbachev launched the Earth Charter initiative in 1994 [earth-charter/2024_history]. The text was agreed at UNESCO in March 2000 and launched on 29 June 2000 in The Hague [earth-charter/2024_history]. It has 16 principles under four pillars [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter]. They are Respect and Care for the Community of Life; Ecological Integrity; Social and Economic Justice; and Democracy, Nonviolence, and Peace [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter]. Within five years it gained over 2,000 organizational endorsements, including UNESCO and IUCN [earth-charter/2024_history]. Earth Charter International, at the University for Peace in Costa Rica, calls it "a people’s treaty" and "a soft law document" [earth-charter/2024_faqs].

It speaks of "one human family" and calls Earth's protection "a sacred trust" [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter]. Development is "about being more, not having more" [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter]. Like Part X.6, it puts "the burden of proof" on those who say an activity is safe [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter]. Like the doughnut test (VII.7), it is "a common standard" for individuals, businesses, governments and transnational institutions [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter]. But it keeps nations: "We are at once citizens of different nations and of one world" [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter].

**The floor in rights law.** The UDHR was proclaimed on 10 December 1948 in resolution 217 A [un/1948_udhr]. The ICESCR was adopted on 16 December 1966 and took effect on 3 January 1976 [un/1966_icescr]. On 10 October 2026 it had 174 parties; the US signed in 1977 but never ratified [un/2026_icescr-treaty-status].

| Water and sanitation | None | None | Res. 64/292 [un/2010_resolution-64-292] |

Resolution 64/292 "Recognizes the right to safe and clean drinking water and sanitation as a human right" [un/2010_resolution-64-292]. It cited about 884 million people without safe drinking water and over 2.6 billion without basic sanitation [un/2010_resolution-64-292]. It passed on 28 July 2010 "by 122 votes to none, with 41 abstentions" [un/2010_a-64-pv-108]. Abstainers included the US, UK, Canada and Japan [un/2010_a-64-pv-108].

Both treaties assume states. ICESCR duties fall on "States Parties", acting "to the maximum of its available resources" and "progressively" (Art. 2(1)) [un/1966_icescr]. Its Article 1 gives peoples self-determination [un/1966_icescr]. UDHR Article 15 says "Everyone has the right to a nationality" [un/1948_udhr]. Article 28, by contrast, promises "a social and international order" in which these rights can be realized [un/1948_udhr].

**Mother Earth.** The declaration came from a people's conference in Cochabamba, 20–22 April 2010 [bolivia/2010_rights-of-mother-earth]. Bolivia sent it to the UN on 7 May 2010 as a draft for the General Assembly to adopt [bolivia/2010_rights-of-mother-earth]. The UN translation dates it 24 April; GARN dates it 22 April [bolivia/2010_rights-of-mother-earth] [garn/2026_mother-earth-declaration]. It gives all beings rights and the right to "live free from torture or cruel treatment by human beings" [bolivia/2010_rights-of-mother-earth]. Duties fall on "Human beings, all States and all public and private institutions" [bolivia/2010_rights-of-mother-earth]. Its preamble blames "the capitalist system" [bolivia/2010_rights-of-mother-earth]. GARN's youth wing has petitioned the UN to adopt it, aiming for 1 million signatures by COP30 [garn/2026_mother-earth-declaration]. I found no record of adoption.

**For the Charter:** These texts supply most of the floor and much of the Preamble's language. The Earth Charter is closest in spirit. All of them name states as the bodies that owe rights. The Charter replaces states with the Commonwealth, so it must say who owes each floor item before stage two (XII.3). Energy, meaningful work and open knowledge go beyond the binding texts.

## Part VI and Nozick's framework for utopia

Part III of *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* argues that the minimal state is also inspiring [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy] [nozick/1974_anarchy-state-utopia]. Nozick calls his framework "equivalent to" the minimal state (p. 333) [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy]. It protects voluntary communities and each person's liberty to enter and leave them [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy]. No planner could know what utopia is, so the framework works as a discovery procedure [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy]. Elsewhere in the book he rejects "end-state" and "patterned" principles of distribution, such as Rawls's [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy]. In 1978 he noted that only about 6% of Israeli adults in the 1970s had chosen kibbutz life [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy].

| Charter | Nozick, pages as quoted by Mack or Skoble | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| VI.1: the good "alone or alongside others" | visions "individually or jointly pursued" (p. 307) [skoble/2020_essential-nozick] | Near-verbatim |
| VI.2: visions that respect principles, floor, ceiling | "almost all particular utopian visions" (p. 319); communities "need not be" libertarian (pp. 320–21) [skoble/2020_essential-nozick] | Charter's limits far thicker |
| VI.3: exit; no imposed utopia | Free entry and exit; "imperialistic" utopians oppose the framework (pp. 319–320) [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy] | Same |
| VI.4: "chooses no utopia" but guarantees floor, ceiling, resources | Minimal state, perhaps no "central authority" (p. 329) [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy] | Opposite on the state's role |
| VI.5: limits at floor, freedom, ceiling | The minimal state's norms of liberty, property and contract [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy] | Adds floor and ceiling |

I read secondary accounts and the publisher's page, not Nozick's text [nozick/1974_anarchy-state-utopia].

**For the Charter:** Part VI is recognizably Nozick's meta-utopia, down to the wording of VI.1. But it is fused with a floor, a wealth limit and planetary revenue: the patterned principles Nozick wrote against. Libertarian readers will call these an imposed utopia, which VI.3 forbids. One coherent answer: the floor makes exit real (see Okin), and the ceiling protects the base every community needs. VI.2 is also narrower than Nozick: every community must honour all Seven Principles.

## Exit and its limits

- **Hirschman, 1970.** His publisher's summary: members answer decline by "exit" or "voice", and "exit often undercuts voice while being unable to counteract decline" [hirschman/1970_exit-voice-loyalty]. Loyalty slows exit so voice can work [hirschman/1970_exit-voice-loyalty].
- **Hirschman, 1978.** Montesquieu and Adam Smith hailed exit by the wealthy as a restraint on arbitrary rule or taxation [hirschman/1978_exit-voice-state]. Today "such exit (capital flight) tends to render the introduction of needed reforms more difficult" [hirschman/1978_exit-voice-state].
- **Hirschman, 1993.** In East Germany in 1989, mass flight and mass protest combined to topple the regime [hirschman/1993_exit-voice-gdr].
- **Okin, 2002.** A formal right of exit is not enough: girls and women often have far less real chance to leave [okin/2002_mistresses-of-their-own-destiny]. The state should require groups to treat them fairly inside. She criticizes Raz, Galston and Kukathas [okin/2002_mistresses-of-their-own-destiny].
- **Kukathas, 2003.** He defends exit-based pluralism rather than criticizing it. Authority "rests on the acquiescence of subjects rather than on justice", and "liberal societies should tolerate illiberalism" [kukathas/2003_liberal-archipelago].

Differs from the drafting-session research: that note says Hirschman "implies that when exit disappears, voice must become much stronger", citing Wikipedia. Hirschman's own framing is two-sided: easy exit can starve voice, yet exit and voice reinforced each other in 1989 [hirschman/1970_exit-voice-loyalty] [hirschman/1993_exit-voice-gdr]. Ending exit after stage three removes a lever that helped voice work, not just a rival to it.

**For the Charter:** Part VI makes exit the main protection inside communities. Okin argues exit is weakest for those most likely to need it, such as girls and women raised inside. The floor helps only if it reaches people who leave. Part II's binding principles side with Okin, not Kukathas.

## How long voluntary communities last

Sosis studied 200 US communes of the 19th century, 88 religious and 112 secular [sosis/2000_religion-intragroup-cooperation]. Religious communes lasted 25.3 years on average, secular ones 6.4 years [sosis/2000_religion-intragroup-cooperation]. Secular communes were four times as likely to dissolve within 2 years [sosis/2000_religion-intragroup-cooperation]. Owenite communes averaged 2.1 years and Fourierist 3.2 [sosis/2000_religion-intragroup-cooperation].

Sosis and Bressler coded 83 of them in detail: 30 religious and 53 secular [sosis-bressler/2003_commune-longevity]. Median lifespans were 25 years against 5 [sosis-bressler/2003_commune-longevity]. Costly demands on members predicted longevity only in religious communes [sosis-bressler/2003_commune-longevity]. Internal disputes (48 cases) and economic failure (43) were the leading causes of collapse [sosis-bressler/2003_commune-longevity]. By 2003, only the Hutterites in the sample still lived communally [sosis-bressler/2003_commune-longevity]. These were property-sharing US communes, so they are an imperfect model for Part VI.

**For the Charter:** If Part VI's communities behave like these, most secular ones will fail within a decade. The floor and free movement (VIII.10) then catch the people who leave. Lasting communities may demand costly commitments that clash with Principles III to V. In stage one the Commonwealth is itself a voluntary secular association, so these base rates may apply to it (inference).

## Tensions and open problems

- **Attribution.** Part II adapts TST's tenets and Part VI adapts Nozick, both without credit [tst/2026_about-us] [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy]. This is a question for Kara, not a finding of wrongdoing.
- **Binding or chosen.** Part II binds "every terran", and Part I makes everyone a terran from birth. Yet the Preamble says the Charter is for those who choose it. The Amsterdam Declaration refuses to impose its view [humanists-international/2022_amsterdam-declaration].
- **Forfeiture (IV).** TST's "forgo one's own" becomes loss of "the Charter's protection", without "willfully" [tst/2026_about-us]. Read literally, offenders could lose the fair process of IX.4. UDHR Article 30 denies a right to destroy rights; it does not strip the actor's own [un/1948_udhr].
- **Autonomy (III).** No one may claim a terran's body. That sits uneasily with confinement (IX.9), epidemic control (ICESCR 12(2)(c)) [un/1966_icescr], and parents' authority.
- **Knowledge (V).** "Belief follows evidence, never the reverse" binds everyone, while Part I.3 lets faith flourish and UDHR Article 18 protects religion [un/1948_udhr]. Is V a rule for public decisions or for private belief?
- **Discretion (II, VII).** Justice over law and spirit over letter give interpreters wide room. Both strain against "the rule of law" [humanists-international/2022_amsterdam-declaration] and the entrenched floor in XI.4.
- **Part VI against Part II.** Many religious and traditional communities may fail the Seven Principles. Yet demanding religious communes lasted longest [sosis-bressler/2003_commune-longevity].
- **Imposition.** The floor and wealth limit are patterned principles Nozick rejected [mack/2026_nozick-political-philosophy]. Critics will say VI.3 forbids what the Charter itself does.
- **Realistic exit.** VI.3 is silent on children, property when leaving, and support after [okin/2002_mistresses-of-their-own-destiny].
- **Rights law.** The ICESCR asks for progressive realization within available resources; the floor is absolute with no duty-bearer before stage two [un/1966_icescr]. UDHR Article 15 and ICESCR Article 1 protect nationality and self-determination [un/1948_udhr] [un/1966_icescr]. UDHR 27(2) protects authors' interests, against knowledge as common property [un/1948_udhr].
- **Gaps.** Manifesto II and the Earth Charter address population; the Charter does not [aha/1973_humanist-manifesto-ii] [earth-charter/2000_earth-charter]. Part X credits rights of nature, but the Charter grants none [bolivia/2010_rights-of-mother-earth].
- **Preamble wording.** "For most of our history we have divided ourselves by nation" may read as anachronistic, since nations are fairly recent. Not researched here.

## Options for the next draft

- **Credit Part II.** For example, a note reading *adapted from The Satanic Temple's Seven Fundamental Tenets (2014)*. Or credit TST and the humanist manifestos together, or redraft from the humanist texts. Kara decides.
- **Credit Part VI.** A note reading *draws on Robert Nozick's framework for utopia*, naming the departure: a floor and a ceiling.
- **Split who is bound.** Bind every institution; ask each terran who adopts the Charter to commit.
- **Narrow forfeiture.** Limits set by a court after fair process, proportionate to harm, with an intent element.
- **Make exit real.** Educate children to the floor standard. Give leavers a fair share of communal assets and the full floor. Keep information open to members.
- **Bound the spirit.** VII cannot lower the floor or weaken protections, mirroring XI.4; the Planetary Court interprets.
- **Footnote the floor.** Cite UDHR, ICESCR and 64/292 for each item. Mark energy, meaningful work and open knowledge as new claims. Say whether the floor is immediate or staged.
- **Decide two gaps.** Rights of nature or Guardians only; whether to address population.
- **Protect the name.** Keep "Commonwealth", avoid "Federation", register the free domains, and run trademark and web searches before launch.
