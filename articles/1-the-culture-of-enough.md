# Article 1 — The Culture of Enough

*One of the nine [Articles of Terra](README.md) in [The Terran Charter](../CHARTER.md) · [Commentary](../commentary/articles.md#article-1--the-culture-of-enough)*

In the Commonwealth, status comes from what a terran contributes, not what they accumulate.

1. **Greed defined.** Greed is taking or hoarding more than one needs while others fall below the floor, or while the planet is pushed past its ceiling.
2. **Shunned, not admired.** Greed is met with social disapproval, not admiration. Terrans honor those who build, teach, discover, heal, repair, and share.
3. **Enough is a virtue.** A good life is measured by flourishing, not by possession. Terrans may own homes, possessions, and savings, and live in real comfort.
4. **The wealth limit.** No terran may hold more than [$10 million][wealth-limit]. Wealth above the limit passes to the Commons Fund each year. The limit sits well above a comfortable life; no one needs hundreds of millions, or a mega-yacht.
5. **A gradual climb.** The first [$2 million][wealth-tax-free] of wealth is free of wealth tax. Above that, an annual wealth tax starts at [1%][wealth-tax-start] and rises toward the limit, and income is taxed on a steeply progressive scale. Terrans set the exact rates by planetary act (Article 3).
6. **A fair burden.** Below the limit, a terran's combined taxes never take more than their income and assets earn in a year.
7. **Paying in shares.** Owners whose wealth is mostly in businesses may pay in shares, which pass to the Commons Fund, so no one must sell or close a business to pay.
8. **Nothing hidden.** Every kind of asset is reported. Wealth held through companies, trusts, or relatives counts as the owner's.
9. **The commons.** Air, water, land, and the body of human knowledge are commons. No one may enclose a commons for private gain at the cost of the floor or the ceiling. The Commons Fund holds what returns to the commons on behalf of all terrans, living and future.

*Amounts are in 2026 US dollars, adjusted each year for prices, until the Commonwealth has its own money. The wealth limit adapts Ingrid Robeyns' limitarianism.*

## Business

Businesses exist to serve terrans, and they have no political voice of their own.

10. **Organizations are not persons.** Companies, nonprofits and other organizations may own property, sign contracts, and sue or be sued, so they can always be held to account. They hold no political rights.
11. **No money in votes.** No organization may spend to influence a vote or a public decision. Individual spending on votes is capped, and the press stays free.
12. **Pay stays proportionate.** Pay above [50 times][pay-ratio-tax] an organization's lowest full-time-equivalent pay is taxed on a steeply rising scale, and pay above [100 times][pay-ratio-cap] goes entirely to the Commons Fund. The lowest pay counts contractors and agency, franchise, and outsourced workers; the highest counts stock and is averaged over several years.
13. **Workers become owners.** Workers earn a growing share of ownership in the organizations they work for, and long-term workers become members with a vote. The Commons Fund helps new cooperatives start, since a lack of start-up money is one of the barriers that keep them rare.
14. **Workers have a voice.** In larger organizations, workers elect at least [a third][board-share] of the board.

*Worker ownership draws on Mondragon's cooperatives and on employee-ownership plans; board seats draw on Germany's codetermination.*
