# Article 6 — Emergencies

*One of the nine [Articles of Terra](README.md) in [The Terran Charter](../CHARTER.md) · [Commentary](../commentary/articles.md#article-6--emergencies)*

Emergency powers expire unless terrans renew them, and no emergency may silence a vote, a court, or the Ombudsman council.

1. **Emergencies expire.** The Council of Stewards, by a majority, may declare an emergency for a hazard on a list adopted in advance by planetary act (Schedule 5). An emergency lasts up to [21 days][emergency-days] and is published at once. It ends unless confirmed within [seven days][confirm-days] by a panel of [1,000][emergency-panel] drawn from an emergency pool of [20,000][emergency-pool], which is drawn by lot each year. A fresh panel of [1,000][emergency-panel] from the pool decides each extension, and an extension lasts at most [three months][extension-length]. The first extension needs a majority, the second [60%][extension-second], and each later one [ten points][extension-step] more. None may follow one passed at [90%][extension-cap].
2. **Limits on emergencies.** Any measure that suspends a right or bypasses ordinary procedure is an emergency measure, whatever law it uses. The Planetary Court may review any emergency or measure. No emergency may change this Charter, postpone a vote, or stop the Court or the Ombudsman council. Every emergency measure lapses with the emergency, and an inquiry with hearing powers follows.
3. **Continuity.** If the Council of Stewards, the Oversight Council, or the Council of Communities cannot act, a fresh panel from the emergency pool acts in its place for at most [90 days][continuity-days]. It may not change this Charter, postpone a vote, or extend itself.
4. **Urgent acts.** A deciding panel may make an act urgent, so that it takes effect at once, without the waits in 3.5 and 8.4. An urgent act carries an end date and lapses within [a year][urgent-lapse] unless it is ratified through the ordinary process, waits included. Once refused, it may not be renewed.

*Emergency limits draw on South Africa's constitution and Switzerland's urgent laws.*
